# Study records and synthetic worked example

Use these fields selectively in the current project or conversation. They support the complete [method](study-planning.md) and [guides](study-planning-guides.md). Do not create a new research repository or copy private study records into generic skill files. Everything in the worked example is synthetic planning/evidence for demonstration; no participant was recruited, contacted, consented or tested.

## Protocol record

- Decision/owner and primary RQ; change hypothesis or known gap.
- Authorized prior evidence IDs/date/version, what is already answered and missing.
- Method/mode rationale and unsupported inference boundary.
- Target experiences/context, inclusion/exclusion, required variation, channels/biases.
- Initial budget rationale, review checkpoint, stopping rule and uncovered gaps.
- Invitation/screener logic, recruiting/incentive permission and state.
- Consent, recording options/refusal alternative, contact/withdrawal, data access/storage/retention and secondary use.
- Relevant accommodations, language/device/technology, sensitive-action stops.
- Timed guide/tasks with RQ mapping, success definitions and capture fields.
- Roles/logistics/fallback, pilot/version/comparability, analysis and handoff.
- AI permission/actual use/reviewer/disclosure; unknown prerequisites and acceptance.

## Traceable notes and findings

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Session/source ID and evidence locator | Pseudonymous stable ID; note line, excerpt ID or approved recording timestamp |
| Protocol/product/task version and RQ | Which conditions and decision the record supports |
| Evidence kind | Observed behavior, verbatim quote, marked paraphrase/reported experience, measurement or hypothesis |
| Evidence and context | Actual content, relevant participant/context, task state and denominator eligibility |
| Assistance/deviation/data gap | Moderator help, prototype limit, changed task, missing capture or exclusion reason |
| Interpretation/code | Researcher inference or method-specific code, separated from source |
| Counterevidence/alternative | Contradictory source, negative case, competing explanation and open question |
| Confidence/limit | Grounded rationale, what can/cannot be concluded and follow-up |

Keep a task/session matrix for usability:

| Session/task | Context/version | First action/path | Outcome | Breakdown/quote | Assistance/deviation | Optional defined measure | Source locator |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Outcomes distinguish unaided success, partial, failure, abandonment and assisted success. For an issue, record finding ID, affected task/context, source IDs, observed consequence, count/eligible denominator, non-comparable data, severity rationale, confidence/counterexamples, implication and recommendation/owner/validation. For interview analysis, the existing qualitative method owns coding and theme records.

## Pilot and change log

| Version/change | Why | Affected tasks/sessions | Re-pilot needed/result | Comparability decision | Evidence inclusion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Record invitation, screener, consent/refusal, accessibility/setup, guide/tasks, capture/analysis, fallback, closing and storage checks. “Not checked” remains an explicit gap. Material task changes usually require separating pre/post-change outcomes, even if the same product was used.

## Worked non-Oppia protocol: appointment sandbox

**Synthetic input.** A community arts studio is considering a revised appointment-booking sandbox. The coordinator needs to decide whether the prototype's time and confirmation labels are ready for a larger pilot. Only an invented sandbox and this example are available; no prior research corpus or live booking/account system is accessed.

**Decision/RQ.** Decide whether to proceed to a wider pilot or revise the booking labels, preserving safe participant participation. RQ1: can first-time prospective visitors identify a feasible appointment and understand whether it is confirmed? Usability observation fits comprehension/task behavior; a brief post-task interview can explain expectations, but neither establishes population success rates or real booking adoption.

**Sample/recruiting rationale.** Target adults who have recently arranged a comparable appointment. Cover two relevant situations: rigid work schedules and flexible schedules; include primarily phone-based booking where that is the intended context and relevant assistive technology when the pilot must support it. Recruit outside the studio's staff/enthusiast network. A provisional initial budget of six sessions supports initial coverage across these contexts and analysis depth; it is a logistics estimate, not a universal adequacy rule or a claim to find most issues. Review after initial coverage and two-session analysis batches. Continue for an uncovered required context, decision-changing explanation or critical negative case; otherwise stop and describe achieved coverage. If access/budget is exhausted first, narrow the readiness recommendation and name the gap. No sessions are actually scheduled.

**Screener logic.** “When did you last arrange an appointment or class time?” qualifies relevant recent experience, with the interval defined for actual recruitment. “What usually limits the times you can attend?” classifies relevant scheduling context without promoting the prototype. “Which device or access support would you use for this activity?” plans the intended conditions and accommodations. Exclude the prototype's designers and testers from the target-user evidence; avoid irrelevant sensitive demographics. Form implementation, if later requested, belongs to Survey Studio. Invitation is prepared only; recruiting and any incentive must be authorized before contact.

**Consent/data plan.** Voluntary 30-minute sandbox session, skip/stop at any time, separate screen/audio permission, notes-only alternative when adequate. Use source IDs instead of names; contact/incentive records stay restricted. No real payment, credential, account or booking action. Storage location, access roles, retention and withdrawal route/cutoff must be supplied by the accountable owner before collection; this example makes no compliance claim. Provider transcription/AI processing and portfolio use remain off pending explicit appropriate permission.

**Task guide.** Five-minute welcome/consent/setup; three-minute think-aloud warm-up; two seven-minute goal tasks; five-minute post-task probes; three-minute closing. T1/RQ1: “You need an appointment after work next week. Find a time you could attend and show where you would confirm it. Use only the sandbox details provided.” T2/RQ1: “Using this sandbox's result, explain whether you have an appointment and what, if anything, you would do next.” Scenario availability is standardized. T1 success is selecting a feasible time and reaching the sandbox review state unaided; T2 success is accurately explaining its displayed state. Partial, failed, abandoned and assisted outcomes remain distinct. Record path, hesitation/backtracks, label interpretations, intervention and context. Optional task time has defined read-task-to-outcome endpoints and assistance/pause notes; no mandatory SUS or A/B comparison is added.

**Pilot.** Pilot invitation/screener, recording refusal path, phone/access setup, safe state/reset, timing, task wording, capture locators, analysis, fallback, closing and approved storage. Try the matrix on pilot notes. If T1 changes from “book a visit” to “find a time and show confirmation,” mark v1→v2, re-pilot, and keep v1 outcomes separate because the success construct changed. These are planned checks, not a completed pilot.

**Illustrative synthetic notes (not findings from real participants).**

| ID/source | Evidence kind | Evidence | Interpretation | Limit/counterevidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S01-T2/N12, protocol v2 | Observed behavior | Pauses at review state and searches for a confirmation number before describing status | Review-state distinction may be unclear | Prototype fidelity could cause uncertainty; inspect stimulus and other cases |
| S01-T2/N13, protocol v2 | Synthetic verbatim quote | “I think this is booked already.” | Reported understanding conflicts with the sandbox's unconfirmed review state | A quote does not prove booking behavior; retain N12 context |
| S02-T2/N08, protocol v2 | Observed/reported | Correctly says a final confirmation action remains and points to it | Negative case limits a universal confusion claim | Relevant prior experience may explain difference; probe without inventing it |

If these were actual authorized records, a narrow finding could say “One of two eligible v2 sessions misread the review state; one recognized it.” It could not say “Half of customers fail” or “the design is validated.” Keep the count and denominator, source IDs, context and conflicting case. A proposed implication is to clarify review versus confirmed status; the coordinator owns the decision, and the next validation is another comparable sandbox round covering remaining relevant contexts. Actual evidence collection and analysis are still unexecuted.

**Handoff.** Prepared protocol needing policy/storage/withdrawal values and recruitment authorization, a pilot and achieved sample before a readiness decision. No Oppia corpus lookup, private dataset, provider upload, participant contact, live booking or publication occurs. Acceptance checks: neutral tasks/RQ mapping, justified context/sample logic and stopping, explicit consent/recording refusal/AI-off status, full-flow pilot/change comparability, traceable notes and negative-case/denominator limits. The useful end state is ready to resolve collection prerequisites and pilot; it is not a research result.
