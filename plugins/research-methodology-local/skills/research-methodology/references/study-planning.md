# Decision-linked interview and usability study planning

Use this complete method for a generic discovery interview, semi-structured interview, post-test guide, usability test or UX study protocol. Read the [selected session branch](study-planning-guides.md) and [record templates and example](study-planning-records-and-example.md) before producing the protocol. Keep the selected project's records in that project or the conversation. This reference introduces no corpus retrieval, participant service, tool execution or external transmission.

## 1. Frame the decision and choose evidence

Write: “Decide [choice/action] for [people/context] by [event/date] using evidence about [uncertainty], while protecting [critical guardrail].” Identify the accountable owner and what would change their action. State the primary research question; add secondary questions only when they change that same decision. Infer routine protocol choices, and mark unsupported assumptions; ask only about missing conditions that change recruitment, protection or what the method can establish.

Inspect authorized, relevant prior research supplied or selected for this project. Record what it answers, dates/product versions, consent scope, contradictions and the remaining gap. Do not retest a settled issue without a change hypothesis. Unavailable prior evidence remains unavailable; generic planning never triggers Oppia-specific retrieval. Oppia work uses its original project owner and evidence-first gates.

Choose the smallest method with a clear inference boundary:

| Need | Starting method | Cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Motivation, language, context, trust, decision pathway or mental model | Recent-experience interview | Observed task performance, prevalence or actual future adoption |
| Understanding or completion of a real task | Usability observation | Population prevalence, causal effects or long-term use |
| Interface risk without participants | UX Design Studio's expert review | Observed participant impact |
| Population estimates or comparisons | Survey/statistical design through Survey Studio | A qualitative coverage rationale is not statistical precision |
| Market patterns | Competitor/market evidence with dated sources | Target participants' needs or product effectiveness |
| Longitudinal context | Bounded field/diary design when feasible | Do not pretend a one-session interview supplies longitudinal observation |

Interviews can follow a usability task, but distinguish observed outcomes from reported explanations. Do not combine an interview, prototype test and survey just because they are available. Budget each necessary part. Analytics can inform an observation question when actual authorized analytics are available; do not invent behavioral metrics or silently add collection.

For usability, name product/prototype version, critical journey, context and mode. Choose moderated when probing, accommodations, task ambiguity or safety intervention matter; unmoderated requires self-contained instructions, safe/fidelity-sufficient states and a dependable exit/help path. State the mode's limits. Stop if the flow cannot answer the question.

## 2. Justify sample coverage and recruitment

Specify the intended claim first. Define behavior/experience eligibility, exclusions, relevant variation, recruitment channels, known coverage gaps and who those channels miss. Language, disability, device, infrastructure, age, socioeconomic context, culture and power relationships may affect validity; include those that matter to this decision. Avoid geography/demography as a convenient proxy for behavior.

For qualitative studies, justify initial coverage from question focus, meaningful participant/context variation, expected evidence depth, analytic approach and decision risk. An initial session budget is provisional logistics, not proof of adequacy. A small formative round can reveal problems but never guarantees finding “most major issues.” Neither five participants nor five-to-eight per type is a universal default.

Define the checkpoint and stopping rule before recruitment: review whether required contexts are covered, evidence is analyzable, negative cases have been examined, and further sessions could change the decision-level explanation or expose a critical uncovered case. Continue only for a named gap; stop, narrow the claim or mark a gap when budget/access prevents coverage. Report the achieved sample and recruitment limitations. Estimates, experiments and subgroup comparisons require a suitable statistical rationale, target quantity/effect and uncertainty; do not borrow this qualitative rule.

Write a short neutral invitation: study purpose without the desired answer, target experience, duration, mode/access arrangements, voluntary participation, incentive if actually approved, and contact/withdrawal route. Recruitment permission is separate from a prepared invitation. Keep contact details and eligibility responses in a restricted operational record, apart from shared research notes.

A screener verifies eligibility without coaching participants or revealing the preferred product path. Ask concrete recent behavior, role/context and access/device questions only where needed. Define eligibility for each item, exclusions, screening-out language, incentive/no-show handling and accommodations. Do not select only enthusiasts or colleagues without reporting the bias. This protocol owns screening logic; refer an actual screener form/export or survey instrument to Survey Studio. Never create a competing form engine here.

## 3. Protect participants and the data

Prepare a plain-language consent and recording plan suited to the actual study/policy. State purpose, expected duration, voluntary participation, skipping/stopping/withdrawal, expected discomfort or sensitive topics, incentive terms if known, researcher/contact route, and who will access the data. Specify what will be captured, why, where it is stored, access, retention/deletion, withdrawal cutoff if one is required, and intended report/secondary use. Unknown policy values are explicit prerequisites, not invented compliance.

Ask separately for audio, screen/video recording and any secondary publication of identifiable material. Record a refusal and provide a notes-only path when the question permits it. Never assume consent to a session covers provider processing, portfolio use or participant quotations. Confirm permissions for actual tool processing before any transcription/AI service. For minors, vulnerable participants or consequential safeguarding/legal conditions, have the accountable owner resolve the applicable requirements before contact or collection.

Script example (customize known values): “We are learning about [goal], not testing you. This takes about [duration]. Taking part is optional; you may skip a question or stop. We will record [notes] for [purpose], stored in [approved location] until [approved retention], accessed by [roles]. [Withdrawal route/cutoff]. May we proceed? Separately, may we record [audio/screen] for [specific use]? We can [approved alternative] if you prefer.” Do not read bracketed unknowns to participants; unresolved essential consent/storage conditions block collection while planning can continue.

Use pseudonymous stable source IDs; pseudonymization does not itself make detailed quotes anonymous. Remove contact details, account identifiers and unnecessary identifying combinations from shared output. Use safe test accounts/data and stop before real payment, credential entry, account changes or irreversible actions. Consent is not authorization to change live systems.

## 4. Build and pilot the complete session

Use the selected full branch in [guides](study-planning-guides.md). Map every primary question/task to a research question and decision purpose. Keep questions neutral, single-focus and understandable; move from unaided context to product-specific probes. Include relevant accommodations, interpreters/translation, assistive technologies, device/bandwidth conditions, accessible alternatives and breaks in the session plan.

Assign moderator and note taker where available; state timing, prototype/link/state readiness, quiet/privacy needs, participant joining instructions, technology fallback and closing. Predefine evidence capture and analysis fields using [records](study-planning-records-and-example.md). A generated transcript or summary does not replace the evidence source.

Pilot the full experience: invitation/screener, eligibility, consent and recording choice, join/setup, accommodations, neutral questions/task interpretation, prototype/version and safe data, timing, evidence capture, fallback, debrief, withdrawal and storage. Attempt analysis on pilot notes: can each candidate claim be traced to a source and can observed performance be separated from interpretation? Log changes with protocol version, reason, affected sessions and comparability decision. Re-pilot critical changed sections. Do not pool materially different pre/post-change tasks or instruments without an explicit comparable subset; keep pilot-only data labeled.

## 5. Execute, analyze and hand off within scope

The protocol can be delivered as ready to pilot or needing named prerequisites. “Prepared” is not recruited, consented, conducted, analyzed or accepted. If the user requested a plan, stop with the plan and acceptance checks. A planned study authorizes neither participant contact nor provider upload.

During authorized sessions, capture reported experience, observed behavior or measurement distinctly. Record assistance, task deviations, context/version and missing data. Stop expansion once each task/question serves the decision or essential ethics/rapport. Stop a session at a participant's request or a predefined sensitive action.

For interviews, select the appropriate qualitative analysis approach and use the existing [qualitative analysis method](qualitative-analysis.md) and its branches/records/quality before coding a supplied corpus. For usability, build a task-by-session matrix: outcome, observable breakdown, intervention, route, context and limitations. Keep actual quotes separate from paraphrase and researcher inference. Group supported patterns or affinity clusters only if useful; do not turn every opinion into a theme or causal mechanism.

Trace source → observation/report/measurement → code/category (if used) → pattern/finding → interpretation → implication → recommendation → owner's decision. Preserve denominators, excluded/non-comparable sessions, negative cases, competing explanations and confidence rationale. Examine conflicting outcomes by relevant experience, device/access, context/version, assistance and protocol deviations rather than averaging them away. Small purposive samples do not support population prevalence; post-test preferences do not prove task performance or adoption.

Prioritize usability issues using observed consequence, task criticality, recurrence within the achieved sample, recoverability and confidence. Explain severity; avoid treating frequency alone as impact. Label alternatives, unresolved conflicts, recommendations and validation needs. Handoff fields: decision/question, method/version, achieved coverage, findings with source IDs, limitations/counterevidence, proposed action/owner, risk/guardrail, validation/acceptance and revisit trigger. The product owner remains responsible for the decision.

If AI is contemplated, state permission status and permitted data boundary. Use minimized/de-identified inputs only when approved, preserve original source IDs, compare a human-reviewed subset for omissions, minority/negative cases, contradictions and fabricated detail, record model/procedure/reviewer/changes/limits and disclose material use. Without permission, keep AI processing off and use permitted human/local analysis. Do not upload private project material to test this method.

## Acceptance and stop checks

- Decision, research question, method fit and intended inference boundary are explicit.
- Eligibility, recruiting channel, relevant variation, initial budget rationale, review checkpoint and stopping rule are justified; no universal count substitutes for coverage.
- Invitation and screener logic are prepared; contact/sent states and actual permission remain distinct.
- Consent, recording choices/alternative, data access, retention, withdrawal, inclusion and sensitive-action stops are explicit or named collection prerequisites.
- Each guide item/task maps to the question, uses neutral wording, fits the timing and has observable/codable evidence fields.
- Pilot covers the complete flow and attempts analysis; changes are versioned and comparability decided.
- Notes preserve source, evidence kind, context/version, quote/paraphrase distinction, intervention, interpretation, counterevidence and confidence.
- Synthesis/handoff preserves denominator and negative cases, narrows claims to achieved coverage, names owner and next validation.
- AI permission/use/disclosure status is recorded; private contacts and datasets stay out of generic references.
- Missing prerequisites remain visible. Stop planning when these criteria are met; do not add unrelated tasks, tools or ResearchOps infrastructure.
