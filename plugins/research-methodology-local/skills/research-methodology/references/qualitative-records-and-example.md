# Analysis records, output structure and synthetic example

Keep the records together in the selected project/conversation if that is sufficient. These are useful interfaces, not a mandate to create one file per phase. Never place private participants or raw transcripts in the reusable skill.

## Minimum traceable record

Charter: question/aim; audience/deliverable; corpus/analysis-set boundary; permission/processing/quote limits; method and rationale; breadth, inductive/deductive, semantic/latent and epistemology choices; analyst role and assumptions; completion criteria.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Excerpt ID | Source + exact locator | Verbatim text or labeled redacted/translated text | Context | Code/category IDs | Analytic memo/alternative |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E01 | S01:L12–14 | … | … | C01, C03 | … |

| Code ID/name | Meaning (or codebook definition + inclusion/exclusion) | Linked excerpts | Version/changes |
|---|---|---|---|

| Candidate/final theme or category | Organizing concept/definition | Evidence IDs | Negative cases/conditions | Review action + reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T01 | … | … | … | Retain/split/merge/refine/park/reject |

Reflexive log: date/phase; assumption or positionality issue; interpretation proposed; challenging evidence; decision and unresolved alternative. Coverage and revision records can be concise but must distinguish completed from pending work.

## Usable output

1. Direct answer and bounded scope: what this analysis explains and its status.
2. Question, method and actual process: analysis set, context/permission boundary as relevant, epistemological/analytic choices, analyst authorship and AI/tool assistance, complete versus partial coverage.
3. Findings: theme/category definition, analytic claim, verified quote/excerpt IDs with approved attribution, interpretation, variations and negative cases. Include a table/map if useful.
4. Implications/recommendations, if requested: finding → proposed action/hypothesis → evidence still needed to validate it. Do not imply a redesign was tested.
5. Limits/unresolved questions: selection, prompt/collection effects, missing material, transcript/translation uncertainty, interpretive alternatives and transferability boundary.
6. References and traceable record/appendix location: cite methods and supporting evidence without exposing private paths to an inappropriate audience.

For a manuscript, these fit Introduction, Method, Findings, Discussion and References according to venue needs. A short client memo can integrate them in clear prose. Publishable quotes and external transmission require actual permission; an internal coded table is not public consent.

## Synthetic worked example: onboarding confidence

The following three fictional excerpts illustrate method choices. They are not participants, a completed study, market evidence or Kyle's research. A real analysis would require the full authorized corpus and both levels of review. Question: 'How do new facilitators decide they are ready to lead a session?' Proposed contextualist, semantic, inductive reflexive TA of accounts of readiness.

| Excerpt ID/source | Fictional excerpt | Provisional codes |
|---|---|---|
| E01/S01:L10 | 'I knew the steps, but I waited until Maya watched me run a practice session.' | Knowing steps is insufficient; rehearsal witnessed by a trusted colleague |
| E02/S02:L20 | 'The checklist was useful. I still wanted someone to tell me I hadn't missed anything.' | Checklist as support; seeking reassurance about omissions |
| E03/S03:L30 | 'I skipped the practice. I'd taught similar sessions before and felt ready.' | Prior teaching as a readiness resource; proceeding without rehearsal |

A weak topic label is 'Training'. A candidate organizing concept is 'Readiness is negotiated through reassurance'. E01 and E02 provisionally support an interpretation that procedural knowledge can coexist with uncertainty and that external reassurance helps people authorize themselves to proceed. This adds an analytic claim beyond repeating the quotes.

E03 is a negative case for 'every facilitator needs witnessed rehearsal'. It suggests prior relevant experience may supply another readiness resource. Refine the candidate to 'Finding a credible basis to proceed': some seek interpersonal validation, one draws on experience. That remains a tentative teaching illustration, not an established shared pattern from a complete study. Do not infer E03 is careless, that E01 lacks skill, or that rehearsal improves session outcomes.

Review would inspect all relevant excerpts and the full set, look for accounts challenging both interpretations, and decide whether reassurance and experience belong in one theme or separate patterns. A final definition would state its scope and exclusions. The narrative might propose testing optional rehearsal plus a way to recognize prior experience, but the accounts alone do not prove that this design works.

For a **codebook/framework** version, define categories such as procedural materials, interpersonal validation and prior experience, pilot their boundaries, allow multiple coding, and chart each case with contextual summaries and E IDs. These category headings can be legitimate applied outputs; do not pretend they are fully developed reflexive themes.

For a **content-analysis** version with each excerpt as one response, two of three fictional responses mention an external person as reassurance. Show `2/3 included synthetic responses`, the unit and overlap rules. This arithmetic does not establish saturation or the prevalence of reassurance among real facilitators. Counting the word 'practice' would require checking E03's negation and could otherwise misrepresent its meaning.
