# Research questions and contrasting perspectives

Use when the user needs questions worth researching, alternatives to a leading interpretation, or optional independent review of a consequential research decision. Preserve the selected study/evidence owner and project records. Do not create a panel, research artifact or external job for routine framing.

## Choose the lightest mode

Resolve the installed `work-management:worker-delegation` owner and read its `references/decision-council.md` only when contrasting perspectives or delegation will help. It owns the shared quick/explore/compare/council method, worker independence and evidence-based synthesis. If unavailable, use one labeled single-pass perspective review. Do not copy its runtime, invent independent workers or count agreeing lenses as corroboration.

- Quick: check the one uncertainty most likely to change the next action.
- Explore: generate a few distinct, decision-linked questions or explanations before narrowing.
- Compare: evaluate existing questions, study approaches or interpretations on the same criteria.
- Council: use the shared procedure when independent review is warranted and authorized; it is optional, not the default for every research task.

## Brainstorm useful research questions

1. Infer the decision, population/context, current evidence and constraints from the request. Ask only for a missing fact that materially changes framing.
2. Generate three to five distinct candidate questions. Include a competing explanation or a question whose answer could disconfirm the leading assumption. Keep questions neutral, answerable and bounded; do not presuppose a desired product or outcome.
3. For each question, state the decision it changes, evidence/method needed, scope limit and the observation that would change the answer. Do not promise causal identification or representative findings from a method that cannot provide them.
4. Select the smallest useful question set by decision value, evidence access, ethical fit and effort. Route a real interview/usability protocol to the existing study-planning branch and survey construction to Survey Studio.

A compact output can be `Question | decision affected | evidence/method | disconfirming observation`. These are candidate questions and collection plans, not findings.

## Optional independent perspectives

Give each actual worker the same decision, authorized source bundle, scope/constraints and evidence labels. Choose only useful distinct assignments, for example method fit, alternative causal explanation, or evidence/transfer limits. For the initial independent pass, withhold other workers' conclusions and the lead's preferred answer. Workers must cite inspected evidence, distinguish inference/assumption and return the strongest objection, missing evidence and a conditional recommendation.

The lead checks sources and methods before synthesis. Compare disagreements at the claim level; repeated citations to the same origin are not independent evidence. An additional model may share training, assumptions or source errors. Preserve a credible minority objection when it changes action or uncertainty. Choose the answer warranted by the evidence and decision criteria; do not vote on truth or average confidence scores.

If one assistant supplies several lenses, label them `single-assistant perspectives`. If workers execute separately, identify the actual separation and source dependence without claiming guaranteed independence. Neither synthetic audiences nor simulated interviews are participants. Use perspectives to expose questions for real research, not to fabricate quotations, prevalence, lived experience or validation.

## Deliver and stop

Return the selected question or warranted conclusion, its basis, the strongest unresolved alternative and the next evidence check. Keep optional raw worker outputs in the owning project only when useful. Stop once the question is actionable or the material disagreement is bounded; another panel without new evidence does not resolve it.
