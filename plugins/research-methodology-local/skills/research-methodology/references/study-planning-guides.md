# Complete study-session branches

Read the shared [study-planning method](study-planning.md) and [records](study-planning-records-and-example.md). Use only the matching branch. Timings below illustrate a bounded session and must fit the chosen question, access needs and actual participant availability.

## A. Discovery or semi-structured interview

Use for motivations, mental models, context, trust, discovery pathways and meaning that participants can describe. State why an interview fits; do not report its answers as observed task performance. Write the decision, research question and participant criteria before the guide.

### Guide construction

1. Start with welcome, duration, voluntary participation and consent. Confirm recording choice and the notes-only fallback. Explain that there are no desired answers.
2. Establish unaided context through recent concrete experience before naming the product or stakeholder vocabulary.
3. Explore behavior and decision pathway: trigger, actors, sequence, tools/information, choice, result and follow-through.
4. Probe mental model, expectations, constraints and workarounds tied to an actual episode.
5. Introduce necessary product-specific probes only after unaided context. Do not ask the participant to endorse a feature, validate stakeholder wording or speculate about a future behavior as if it were a fact.
6. Close with reflection, what was missed, questions, withdrawal/contact reminder and actual incentive/next-step information.

### Timed guide template (illustrative 35 minutes)

| Phase | Time | Primary wording | Purpose and optional neutral probe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Welcome/protection | 5 min | Tailored consent script from shared method; separately ask recording permission | Confirm actual consent before capturing data; accommodate notes-only choice |
| Recent context | 5 min | “Tell me about the last time you [relevant real goal].” | Locate an episode; “When was that?” “What was happening around it?” |
| Behavior/pathway | 10 min | “Walk me through what happened from the moment you needed to [goal].” | Trace sequence; “What happened next?” “What did you use?” “What led to that choice?” |
| Expectations/constraints | 7 min | “At that point, what did you expect to happen?” | Mental model versus outcome; “What made that difficult?” “Can you show or describe a specific example without sharing sensitive information?” |
| Product probe if needed | 5 min | “[Neutral description/stimulus]. What do you understand this to mean?” | Compare unaided context; “What makes you say that?” “What, if anything, would you need to know?” |
| Reflection/closing | 3 min | “Is there anything about that experience we have missed?” | Invite negative/exception cases; remind withdrawal route and close |

Tie primary questions to RQ IDs. If two questions address independent concepts, split them or remove one. Use “Tell me about the last time…” instead of future “Would you…?” Prompts such as “Was our helpful calendar easy?” are leading; “What happened when you tried to find a time?” preserves the participant's framing. Hypothetical responses may be useful as expectations but must remain labeled hypothetical.

Predefine notes with stable session/source IDs, verbatim quote or marked paraphrase/reported experience, context, interpretation, confidence, counterevidence, assistance/deviation and follow-up. Plan an analysis approach and explicit confidence limits; for actual coding use the existing qualitative-analysis references. Preserve contradictions and minority experiences.

Pilot invitation through consent, complete guide, accessibility/technology, capture, closing and storage. Attempt the analysis and version changes. Stop adding questions when every item maps to the primary question or an essential rapport/ethics need. Output: decision/RQ, participant/sample rationale, consent/data handling, timed guide/probes, notes schema, analysis/limitations, pilot/change/comparability/stopping plan and inclusion/AI status.

## B. Moderated or unmoderated usability testing

Use to observe whether target participants understand or complete a real goal in the stated product/prototype context. Expert-only inspection belongs to the UX owner. Record decision, research question, target experience/context, critical journey, product version, mode and why the mode fits.

### Task design and observation

Write one goal-oriented scenario and task at a time. Use a plausible situation and useful goal without interface labels or the intended click path. Poor: “Open Calendar, click Thursday and press Reserve.” Better: “You need an appointment after work next week. Find a time you could attend and show where you would confirm it. Stop before making a real booking.” Explain only genuine scenario conditions; safe sandbox actions may have a different stop point.

Define success before sessions from an observable state, with partial success, failure/abandonment and an assisted outcome distinguishable. Set a justified task budget and stop rule. A prototype limitation is not participant failure. Record the first action/route, hesitation, backtracking, errors, help requests, interventions, comprehension/confidence and notable quotes. Include device/accessibility, language and connectivity context.

Add optional measures only if they answer the decision: completion/outcome, time with defined start/stop/pause handling, error definition, post-task ease item or a properly implemented standard scale such as SUS through the instrument owner. These are complementary evidence, not a compulsory battery. If using an instrument, preserve its validated administration/scoring through Survey Studio and state limits. Formative task observations do not automatically justify A/B testing or causal claims; comparisons require a separately suitable design.

### Moderated session template (illustrative 30 minutes)

| Phase | Time | Verbatim wording or action | Evidence/check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Welcome/consent/setup | 5 min | “We are testing this design, not you.” Tailored consent/recording script; confirm safe test state | Consent and recording choice, access needs, version/device, capture/fallback |
| Think aloud and warm-up | 3 min | “As you work, tell me what you are looking for, expecting and thinking. You can pause or stop.” | Familiarize without teaching the target path |
| Task 1 | 7 min | Read the complete neutral task once, answer only scenario clarification | First action, path, outcomes, errors/help, time if justified, intervention |
| Task 2 only if same decision | 7 min | Independent realistic goal; reset state if needed | Carryover/context, separate success and assistance |
| Post-task probes | 5 min | “What did you expect at that point?” “What made you choose that?” | Reported explanation kept distinct from observed behavior |
| Closing | 3 min | “What else would you like us to know?” Remind contact/withdrawal and actual incentive arrangements | Negative case/exception, close and approved storage |

Neutral silence nudge: “What are you thinking now?” Task clarification repeats the goal or genuine scenario constraint, not the interface route. Do not teach. Predefine when to offer help or stop: participant request/distress, inaccessible flow, safety boundary, unusable prototype or justified task budget. Record the exact assistance and time/context; an assisted completion is not unaided success. Prefer neutral follow-ups after the task so probing does not alter the route.

### Unmoderated adaptation

Supply self-contained consent/recording choice, accessible joining/setup instructions, safe test data, one scenario/task per screen or step, neutral think-aloud/capture instruction when appropriate, stopping/exit/help route, follow-up questions and closing/storage details. Pilot from a participant's perspective without live help. Confirm the tool can capture the evidence and permitted context needed; do not infer absent observations from telemetry. Missing recordings, ambiguous state or inaccessible instructions are data-quality gaps. If intervention is needed to answer the question or protect participants, choose moderated or redesign the scope.

### Analysis and verification

Use the task/session matrix and issue record from [records](study-planning-records-and-example.md). Keep success denominators, unavailable/non-comparable data, assistance and prototype limits visible. Separate observed behavior from post-test opinion. Explain impact/severity with task consequences and confidence, inspect contradictory cases by context, and attach source IDs to recommendations. A repeated issue in a purposive sample is a formative finding, not a population percentage.

Pilot invitation, consent, setup/accommodations, wording, links/states, timing, capture, analysis, fallback, closing and storage. Record protocol versions and whether pre-change tasks remain comparable. Stop if fidelity cannot answer the question or before sensitive/irreversible actions. Output: decision/RQ, criteria/sample rationale, mode/environment, scenarios/verbatim tasks, success/observations/measures, neutral moderation/consent/safety, logistics/fallback, analysis/limits, pilot/change/stopping/inclusion/AI status.
