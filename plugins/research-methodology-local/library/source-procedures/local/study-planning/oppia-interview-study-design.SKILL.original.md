# Oppia Interview Study Design

## Overview

Create a neutral, ethical discussion guide that answers one decision-linked
question. Retrieve prior Oppia interview and persona evidence before recruiting
new participants.

Source anchors: `drive.oppia.usability-script.v1`,
`drive.oppia.research-plan-obsolete.v1`, and
`drive.oppia.persona-handoff.v1`. Cross-cutting quality rules are adapted from
`external.ux-research-skill.v1.0.0`.

## Workflow

1. Confirm the decision, primary research question, target participant, and why
   an interview is the right method.
2. Browse Oppia interviews and search the corpus for prior answers and language
   used by the participant group.
3. Define sampling criteria, exclusions, recruiting channel, and known coverage
   gaps. Justify coverage using question focus, meaningful participant/context
   variation, evidence depth, decision risk, and an iterative stopping rule;
   do not default to a universal count or convenient proxy.
4. Build a guide in this order:
   - welcome, duration, voluntary participation, and consent;
   - context and recent concrete experience;
   - behavior and decision pathway;
   - mental model, expectations, constraints, and workarounds;
   - Oppia-specific probes only after unaided context is understood;
   - reflection and closing.
5. Prefer “Tell me about the last time…” over hypothetical preference prompts.
6. Write neutral probes: “What happened next?”, “What made that difficult?”,
   and “Can you show or describe a specific example?”
7. Define a note structure before sessions: stable source ID, quote or reported
   experience, context, interpretation, confidence, counterevidence,
   assistance/deviation, and follow-up.
8. Plan relevant language, cultural, disability, device, infrastructure, age,
   and power conditions; provide accessible participation and withdrawal paths.
9. Pilot the complete flow from invitation and consent through closing and data
   storage. Attempt the analysis on pilot notes, version material changes, and
   decide whether pre-change evidence remains comparable.
10. If AI will assist transcription, coding, or synthesis, confirm permission,
   minimize inputs, retain source links, test a human-reviewed subset, and
   record and disclose material use.

## Output Contract

- research decision and question;
- participant criteria and sample rationale;
- consent/recording script and data-handling note;
- timed discussion guide with primary questions and optional probes;
- note-taking fields;
- analysis approach and confidence limits;
- pilot checklist, change log, comparability rule, and stop condition;
- inclusion, privacy, and AI-assistance status where applicable.

## Guardrails

- Do not ask participants to validate stakeholder wording or a desired feature.
- Do not combine a 60-minute interview, prototype test, and survey unless each
  part is necessary and timed.
- Do not infer actual behavior from a hypothetical answer without a caveat.
- Never include participant contact data in the shared research output.

## Stop Conditions

Stop expanding the guide once every question maps to the primary RQ or an
essential rapport/ethics need. Remove nice-to-know questions that will not
change analysis or action.

## Verification

- Questions move from broad context to specific Oppia probes.
- Each primary question has a decision-relevant purpose.
- Leading, compound, and jargon-heavy questions are removed.
- Consent, recording, withdrawal, and sensitive-data handling are explicit.
- The analysis plan distinguishes quotes from researcher interpretation.
- Sample size follows an explicit coverage and stopping rationale.
- Pilot evidence is analyzable and protocol changes are versioned.
- Contradictory and negative cases remain visible.
