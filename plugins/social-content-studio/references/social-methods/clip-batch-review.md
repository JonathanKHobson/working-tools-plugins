# Review a batch of clips before distributing it

Use when several Shorts, excerpts or posts derive from one recording or related sources. A single post does not need a batch ledger. Reuse the source-to-derivative method and current calendar; this is an additional comparison within those records.

For each candidate keep stable ID, source recording ID/hash, all source intervals, final render hash when available, target channel, one supported promise, independent payoff, CTA, context/prerequisites, and current editorial/file/platform state. Link to Video's source/output mapping; do not transcribe an unchanged source again.

Compare the whole selection before assigning dates:

- First check the brief's actual promised outputs against reviewed source sections and candidate IDs. Mark each obligation covered, intentionally deferred or unsupported. Attractive highlights must not displace a promised participant excerpt or learner demonstration. Do not invent a quota per person or pattern when the brief did not promise one.

- Check reused source intervals AND repeated ideas. Different intervals can repeat one claim; overlapping intervals can serve distinct audiences. Record intentional reuse and its reason; do not impose a universal overlap percentage or automatically reject a recap, translated version or accessibility variant.
- Explain what each item adds. Merge or drop redundant candidates when they have no distinct reader benefit. Preserve an explicit series sequence and any prerequisite needed to understand the excerpt.
- Reconcile confirmed speaker names and corrected terms with the project's recording review. Don't promote an unconfirmed spelling into titles, captions or thumbnail text.
- Show a compact review row with the asset, proposed copy/action, evidence and any decision genuinely missing. Separate editorial selection (proposed/accepted/rejected/held) from file readiness (planned/rendered/QA reviewed) and platform state (absent/draft/scheduled/published). Acceptance never establishes a native schedule or publication.
- Assign dates from the current approved calendar, timezone and native queue. Where cadence or dates are absent, leave a draft without invented slots. Existing authorization may allow continued scheduling; preserve a draft-only hold.
- On an edit, invalidate only dependent fields: changed source selection affects meaning/timecodes; changed render affects its QA/hash; corrected name affects related copy; rescheduling affects calendar/native readback. Other reviewed items remain usable.

Handoff includes selected IDs, distinct purpose, intentional overlaps, unresolved decisions, current files/QA and exact publishing authorization. Route clip editing to Video, thumbnail/still composition to Graphic, learner sequence to Instructional Design and platform actions to Social Publishing.

Acceptance example: three clips repeat the same explanation at different timecodes; keep the strongest or state a distinct use for each. A fourth reuses an opening sentence but demonstrates a different result; retain it with rationale. Two items marked accepted remain unscheduled until the platform provides schedule evidence.

Method provenance: original local comparison procedure informed by Bloom Studio 0.1.0 clipmap/postplan/series methods, reviewed 2026-10-02. Existing source lineage and publishing controls remain authoritative; no Bloom code, UI, fixed posting cadence or platform-duration rules adopted.
