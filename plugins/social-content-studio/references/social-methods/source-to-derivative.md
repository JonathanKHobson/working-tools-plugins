# Source to useful derivatives

Use when turning a workshop, course recording, article, project case or event story
into selected channel-native pieces. The source establishes evidence; each derivative
must give its intended reader a complete useful payoff. Do not expand one-post work
into a campaign or add platforms, quotas, schedules or publication without scope.

## 1. Establish the source and target intent

Read the supplied complete source or the relevant complete passage with enough context
to preserve its meaning. Record source ID/path/URL, version/hash for local assets,
author/date, intended audience, publication state and permitted reuse. For recordings,
use output-timed transcript and reviewed media; source time requires an edit map.
Captions alone do not establish what was shown. Keep project voice samples and
audience/channel decisions in the existing project; a new mandatory context file,
provider or account is not a prerequisite.

Set the target intent in plain language: teach one action, explain a decision with
proof, invite a suitable attendee, or help the reader decide whether the full resource
fits them. Choose a reader action that is supported by an actual artifact/link. Use
Marketing for unresolved audience/offer/channel strategy, Research for live claims,
Career for portfolio evidence and Instructional Design for learning objectives.

## 2. Extract with claim and timestamp lineage

Build only the amount of ledger useful for the requested derivatives. Do not force a
number of insights. For each candidate, record:

| ID | Supported idea / exact quote | Source locator / output time | Evidence and qualification | Reader payoff | Rights/privacy | Proposed format |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D1 | Demonstrated way to compare a draft with its source | Source hash + 02:10–02:42; final transcript lines / source paragraph | Demonstration, not a claim of better accuracy across all tasks | A concrete review step they can try | Owned recording; mask private tab titles | Short demo or LinkedIn example |

This example is synthetic. Distinguish quotation from paraphrase, proposal from shipped
work, demonstration from measured outcome, and third-party claim from established
fact. Preserve caveats, prerequisites and attribution. An excerpted statistic needs
the original population, date, units, denominator and limit; if those do not fit,
reframe without the number or use a format that can carry them. Never invent metrics,
testimonials, participant research, availability or a public release.

## 3. Select for independent value and feasible effort

Rank by fit to the requested reader/action, evidence strength, independent payoff,
available assets and production effort. Do not rank by presumed algorithmic reach or
predict engagement. An excerpt earns its place when a reader who never opens the
source still gets something useful: a demonstrated step and result, a decision with
support, a clear explanation or a relevant invitation. Reject vague teasers, unresolved
references, incomplete instructions, sensational quotes, repeated filler, and snippets
that hide a material limitation. Combine or skip weak candidates rather than fill a
quota. A teaching excerpt may need a short prerequisite line and complete result.

Suggested format choices are editorial options, not performance guarantees:

- One usable screen action: demonstration clip with readable framing and result.
- A sequence or framework: a thread/carousel with one useful step per item and proof.
- A decision/story with an evidenced implication: a native text post or newsletter.
- An event invitation: verified experience, fit, logistics and actual booking route;
  keep spoilers, player details, pricing and availability checks in the event project.

## 4. Draft and produce for the target

Preserve the central supported claim while adapting opening, register, structure,
length, proof presentation and action to the target reader. Voice remains recognizable;
platform stereotypes do not override real samples. Current character/asset limits and
link controls need verification at publishing time. Avoid fixed hashtag counts, invented
reach forecasts, forced first-comment links, or mandatory channels/providers.

Write the complete native piece, not just “I made a video” and a link. A link may offer
depth after the independent payoff. Mark needed assets as unproduced until they exist.
For a clip, route to Video's existing `edit-dialogue-footage` course Shorts repurposing reference
(`skills/edit-dialogue-footage/references/course-shorts-repurposing.md` in
`video-production-workflow`), resolved through the installed skill/tool inventory. Use a source/output time map, new standalone opening if needed,
selective captions, readable screen framing and full rendered QA. Do not crop out a
necessary setting, warning or result. Route image/carousel asset composition to
`graphic-design-studio:graphic-asset-compositing` and format adaptation/review to the
existing Graphic owners. Social owns the channel copy and asset handoff.

## 5. Rights, privacy and final handoff

Before an excerpt or quote leaves the project, confirm recording/quotation/reuse rights
and any participant consent, client restrictions, licensed music/images, screenshots,
embargoes, private UI, identifying details and public permission. Redact on the actual
asset, then review that render. Source ownership does not grant rights to every embedded
asset or participant. Separate evidence retention from public reuse. Do not upload
private source files or analytics to a new provider merely to repurpose them.

For each chosen derivative, deliver full copy/script plus: target/intent, source locator
and claim IDs, quote/paraphrase status, edits/caveats, asset path/hash and state, alt text/
captions, real link/action, rights/privacy status, review status and authorized publication
state. Text-only work needs factual/editorial review; visual assets need rendered review.
Use `social-editorial-review` when readiness review is primary, `content-calendar` only
for an authorized calendar, and `social-publishing` for authorized external actions.
Prepared copy, produced asset, scheduled, published and public readback are distinct.

## 6. Feedback without inventing a forecast

If requested or already part of the campaign, retain measured platform, date/window,
metric/denominator and cohort/format context against derivative/source IDs. Separate
reach, clicks, viewing, booking and task/learning outcomes. Qualitative replies can show
confusion or useful questions but cannot prove broad impact. Route measurement strategy
to Marketing and viewing intervals to Video's retention review. Record one useful next
change or retain the piece when evidence is inconclusive; do not infer a cause from a
single post or treat engagement as learning. Feedback does not authorize new releases.
