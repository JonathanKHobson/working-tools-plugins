# LinkedIn document posts and source repurposing

Use for li-carousel or li-repurpose intent. Preserve the fuller [source-to-derivative](source-to-derivative.md) procedure, [channel rules](channel-adaptation-rules.md), rights and project cadence. [Source/research](../../libraries/linkedin-agent/PROVENANCE.md).

## Repurpose a long source

Read the complete relevant source or disclose unavailable sections. For a transcript, retain timecodes; for prose, paragraph/section IDs. Inventory claims, numbers, stories, mechanisms, mistakes and quotable lines, with exact spans, authorship, uncertainty and public-use status. Counts describe what exists; they do not require a four-to-six-post yield. A weak or thin source may justify one post or none. Distinguish the author's actual words from an edited paraphrase.

Select the requested number of distinct angles that stand alone. Each needs its own reader payoff, source map, permissible claims and reason for its format. Vary opening forms when helpful. Do not turn a source author's event into the user's experience, convert a recording into a study, or imply a proposed feature shipped. Do not force readers to consume the upstream asset to understand the post. Give an honest attribution/link where useful.

For a requested plan, return inventory and sequence. For a request for finished posts, deliver all requested drafts rather than withholding them behind a 'write Tuesday' loop. Use the LinkedIn writing method for each and compare the set for repetitive openings, conflicting claims and duplicate value. Pass candidates into the existing calendar with dependencies and state; no automatic posting quota.

## Document/carousel structure and copy

Use a document when order or comparison helps: steps, decision examples, annotated evidence, a before/after sequence or a reusable framework. A text post may better fit a single small claim. Choose the shortest complete sequence, not a mandatory 8–12 slides. A useful pattern is cover/promise → stakes → ordered ideas/examples → standalone recap → one relevant next action. Preserve a strong existing source structure when it works.

For every page specify page number, purpose, headline, concise body, evidence/source span, intended visual and accessibility notes. Cover must make an honest promise. Each middle page advances the explanation; do not split paragraphs arbitrarily or hide the payoff. A recap should work when shared on its own. Use source-specific diagrams/screenshots only when rights and privacy permit. A small attribution/handle is useful where appropriate; no fake evidence or decorative metric.

Create accompanying post copy, document title, source/rights notes and a plain-text summary. The user asking for a finished PDF has authorized its construction; do not insert an upstream copy-approval gate unless the user requested one. Use the available document/presentation/PDF or Graphic Design provider and its current instructions. 4:5 portrait can be a starting design choice, not a LinkedIn requirement. Current official guidance lists PDF, PPT/PPTX and DOC/DOCX, up to 100 MB and 300 pages; verify before upload. Uniform page dimensions and accessible reading order matter; animations become static. Uploaded document content cannot simply be edited in place, so confirm the final file.

## Visual acceptance

Before making visual claims, map 3–8 anchors including cover, densest page, evidence visual, recap and final action (page/region/node if available, observed fact and intended fix). Render the actual exported file, review every page for clipping/overflow, and inspect anchors at a plausible mobile display size. Check contrast, readable type, page order, links, embedded fonts, text extraction and public-safe details. A slide-copy outline or successful PDF generation is not rendered QA. Fix and recheck changed anchors using the relevant design owner. Supply final PDF plus editable source and copy when requested; label an unrendered export unverified. Social Publishing owns a separately authorized upload and receipt.
