# Substack: source to useful article

Conditional Social Content Studio procedure for authorized Substack work. Controls observed October 2, 2026 on Substack web; recheck the current account. Project ledgers own article receipts; this guide proves no particular article was scheduled.

## Scope and source

Use when an authorized video/course/project-evidence handoff calls for Substack. Preserve narrower user scope. Start with source-parent IDs, full relevant transcript passages, approved evidence and public release state. Combine related Shorts as supporting material rather than creating thin articles. An article must deliver a complete lesson, decision, example or useful resource even if the reader never watches the source. No invented case-study outcomes, customer research or personal anecdotes. Label synthetic examples; identify AI narration when linking old synthetic course clips. Coauthors require substantive contribution and verified account/permission, not friendship alone.

Prioritize creative, compassionate and critical thinking over trends. Article craft: recognizable reader problem, concrete supported teaching, a useful example or exercise when the subject calls for one, and a way to inspect the result. Ask a specific discussion question when hearing from readers would improve the piece or future work. Keep source attribution close to claims. Adapt rather than dump transcripts. Writing Quality Studio owns voice/editorial review. Apply `natural-prose-editor` and its editorial gate to every finished article before marking it ready or scheduling it. Review the saved headline, subtitle, body, captions and action labels again after native-editor changes. Authored newsletter email editions must contain no em dashes; preserve authentic quotations. Use `humanizer-deep-audit` when the sources or audit scope require it. Use actual voice samples or source performance, and report limitations. Use a bounded independent writing review when it adds value; critique remains advice reviewed by the lead. No detector score establishes authorship or quality.

Include the author's intended emotional effect in the brief when supplied. A strong opening, emotion, creative scene or hopeful future vision can be central to the article rather than decoration; preserve it through the evidence pass. Distinguish reported fact, interpretation, prediction and explicitly imagined possibility at the sentence where each appears. Keep any personal voice profile and article-specific emotional arc in the owning project; do not impose one author's tone or a fixed frustration-to-hope pattern on other work.

Judge the complete reader experience, not prose alone. Before redesigning an author's article, inspect attributable examples of their work for what to preserve in voice, rhythm, research-to-reflection transitions and distinctive endings; do not turn an older article's structure or repeated subscription prompts into a house template. For each proposed feature, state what this reader needs and how the feature helps. Choose a clear primary native action that fits the article: subscribe, try a prompt, comment, read further, take a course or answer a survey. Optional interior images, diagrams, screenshots, reusable prompts, quotations, polls, surveys, video, audio and author voice earn their place by explaining, proving, inviting or helping the reader act. A strong article may need none of them. Verify any expert quotation against its original source rather than inheriting it from an older bibliography.

Link consequential claims where readers encounter them. Add a compact Sources/Further reading section when research density warrants a consolidated trail; for source-derived teaching with no public citation, use an honest source note and disclose fictional examples. A bibliography does not replace claim-level support. Check the publication bio/profile and author identity for accuracy when they shape reader trust, within the current account and requested scope.

## Optional source-grounded companions

During an authorized article brief or adaptation, decide whether a visual or short video would help this reader understand, inspect or use one supported idea. Record **selected**, **deferred** or **no companion**, with the reader benefit and format rationale in the existing article record. Do not create a second queue or automatically produce every format. The article must remain useful on its own; a text-only request stays text-only. Personalized means grounded in the author's supplied voice, examples and intended audience, not invented experience or an unrequested change of identity.

Choose the representation by communication need:

| Reader need | Possible companion and production owner |
| --- | --- |
| See an action, change over time or a short explanation | Video Production's `plan-video-production`, with `design-video-storyboards` / `create-motion-graphics` as needed; reuse its source-to-video procedure |
| Understand one relationship or inspect a proof object quickly | Graphic Design's `graphic-design` / `infographic-design`; a real proof crop, image, drawn SVG or Draw and Think treatment when it communicates clearly |
| Compare supported quantities, structures or evidence | Data Visualization's `data-visualization`, then the appropriate chart, structural-diagram or interactive-explanation owner; retain units, uncertainty and sources |
| Explore a mechanism or follow a briefing | Existing interactive HTML or presentation owner, selected through Graphic Design / Data Visualization and Work Management; a static image or useful link must carry the meaning where interaction is unavailable |

Social owns the article brief, placement and native state; these owners retain production and their full methods. Give the selected producer one bounded handoff: parent source ID and version/hash, complete relevant passage or transcript times, article version, audience, one takeaway, supported claim IDs/locators and caveats, voice/credit/rights constraints, chosen format, placement and release dependency. Freeze that source version for the derivative. If a material claim or source changes, identify affected companions and revalidate them before use instead of silently mixing versions. A useful asset already present may satisfy the need.

Return the companion under the same article/source lineage with its own variant ID: final asset path/hash and review state; editable project/source path and dependencies; what remains editable versus flattened or lost on export; caption/transcript/alt-text paths appropriate to the format; and the producer's actual verification. Use Work Management's `work-handoff` editability procedure for destination-app claims. A rendered image or MP4 is not proof of an editable diagram or timeline. For video, provide synchronized captions and a transcript carrying essential visual information; for stills, provide meaningful alt text and a caption/source note where needed. Keep an accessible static/text fallback for motion, HTML and decks. Retain source files on the owning project's storage, not in the reusable plugin.

Review the actual output at its intended article size: factual labels and claim fidelity, readable hierarchy, relevant motion, caption timing, audio/visual clarity and mobile comprehension. Keep the existing visual anchors and production QA. Apply only the destination's approved branding; a Substack companion does not automatically inherit a YouTube long-form intro, Shorts overlay or TikTok watermark. No new brand direction follows from format selection.

For an authorized insertion, verify the account's current upload/embed support and reuse rights; do not assume SVG, HTML, an interactive chart or a deck runs inside Substack. Use an appropriate export or accessible hosted link when needed. Private or future YouTube URLs are not usable public embeds. Read back upload completion, saved placement and playback/link behavior, plus the actual Mobile, Desktop and Email previews and fallback. Keep local **prepared**, **uploaded**, **native draft**, **native scheduled**, **published** and **publicly verified** states separate for the companion and article. An uploaded video does not publish or schedule its parent article, and an article schedule does not verify its media playback. A method update creates no production job or publication authority and does not reopen previously scheduled articles.

## Verified editor controls

- Title and subtitle are separate native textboxes; author chip is separately editable. Preserve the account identity unless changing it is in scope.
- Inspect each draft before editing; never assume the supplied URL is blank. Content autosaves, with Saved/Saving status and Version history.
- Style menu exposes Normal text and Heading 1–6. Toolbar exposes bold, italic, strikethrough, inline code, text color, highlight, superscript/subscript, links, images, audio, video, quote, bulleted/numbered lists, alignment, Button, Template and More. Presence is not a completed insertion test.
- Rich HTML clipboard paste through the native editor preserved paragraphs, H2, emphasis, links and blockquotes. Markdown clipboard format inserts plain Markdown; do not assume conversion.
- Insert image > Image opens a file chooser. Wait for upload completion before continuing. Click the image, then its visible three-dot overlay; Edit alt text opens an Alt text field with OK. A click selecting the image alone does not open its menu. Other observed options: Edit image, Edit caption, Add watermark, Wide width, Full width, Delete image.
- To embed an existing YouTube clip, paste its public watch URL as its own paragraph; this produced a native iframe. Insert video opens an upload/record panel, a different route. Close unused panels explicitly. Verify actual public playback separately and inspect the embedded card in native preview.
- More currently exposes Audience-specific content, Code block, Divider, Financial chart, Footnote, LaTeX, Paywall, Poetry, Poll, Prediction market and Recipe. Use only what serves the teaching purpose and current authorized scope.
- Template menu exposes What is a template? and New template. Native creation, saving and reopening were exercised. The template editor has its own name and body; a slash in the name grouped it under a collection. Saving a template does not insert it into an article. Verify the saved template by reopening it; local template files do not prove native retention.
- Settings > Audience: Everyone; comments can be Everyone, Free or paid subscribers, Paid subscribers only, or disabled. An inspected blank draft defaulted to paid-only for BOTH article and comments. Always verify these explicitly for free-reader work. Audience and metadata forms can have separate Save buttons.
- Settings > Add tags accepts existing or new tags. Typed input alone is not a saved tag; verify a named tag chip after saving.
- Settings > SEO Options exposes SEO title, description and Post URL. UI guidance recommends under 60 characters for title, 50–160 for description. SEO fields and URL have separate Save actions. Keep human title useful; use natural searchable phrases without promises of ranking or reach.
- Social preview uses the article title, subtitle and an image; Edit is exposed. Check actual crop. The title and artwork should communicate a source-specific idea. Do not claim decorative style improves SEO.
- Preview opens Mobile, Desktop and Email tabs plus free-subscriber view. A preview timestamp such as Just now is preview UI, not publication evidence. Review headline wrapping, imagery, prompt blocks, headings and ending in the actual preview.
- For every material inserted element, read back the saved native draft and its Mobile, Desktop and Email previews. Check order, alt text/caption, links and button destinations, media fallback, source notes, CTA and any truncation warning. Open important link and survey destinations; HTTP 200 alone does not show that a survey contains the intended questions. Interactive embeds may degrade to linked previews in email, so surrounding text must carry the essential meaning.
- Continue opens Publish, with audience, comments, tags, social preview, email/app delivery and Schedule time to email and publish. Default final action is Send to everyone now: never click it for schedule-first work. Enable scheduling, choose an actual future date/time, inspect the visible timezone, and verify the SAVED native timestamp by stable post ID afterward. Input display alone is not enough; a stale relative-time button can reveal an uncommitted input.

If a filled datetime is not committed, compare the input, timezone and relative send label before proceeding. In the observed editor, ArrowUp, ArrowDown, then Tab committed the intended value; use this only as a checked UI recovery and re-read the exact final date/time afterward. A later "Publish without buttons" dialog retained the selected future schedule. Button wording alone is not evidence: record final success, then reopen the post and verify its saved timestamp.

## Meaningful visuals

Keep 3–8 review anchors: source/viewport, region or selector, observed fact, intended action and changed-render verification. Choose real proof/screenshots or a designed diagram when clearer than illustration. Ask what specific fact or relationship the image teaches. Generated optics/contact/perspective must be coherent; meaningless symbols are defects even in a polished rendering. Inspect full composition and phone scale. Preserve assets and exact provenance/hash locally. New work must reflect current feedback; do not revisit already scheduled/published artwork without scope. Factual text inside cards is not automatically an effective visual: use Graphic Design's distinct meaning, visual-interest and construction checks. Preserve accepted tradeoffs. After an image replacement, reconcile its asset path/hash, alt text, current metadata, social preview and downstream references; retain rejected versions only as clearly identified historical evidence.

## Scheduling and downstream handoff

Schedule after required public media release, with sensible spacing from related channel promotions. If the article stands alone, linking the current free course avoids dependence on an unavailable future video embed. Retain exact timezone offsets and source release timestamps. Scheduling horizon and permitted controls can vary with current product/account state. Treat an official help limit as guidance to recheck, not a hard-coded block: inspect the current native scheduler, try only an authorized future date, and read back the saved post timestamp. If the target date is rejected, keep the article prepared and record the observed limit. A future queue receipt does not prove public release. For a poll in a scheduled post, published help does not establish whether timed expiry starts at poll creation or post publication; verify this in the native editor/readback or prefer an open-ended poll when appropriate.

Ledger fields: content ID, source-parent IDs, title, native post ID/editor URL, canonical public URL, status, publish_at with zone, media dependencies and actual availability, topic/project tags, source/asset paths, editorial/visual proof, delayed LinkedIn opportunities. Feed the existing common calendar rather than starting another automation. LinkedIn promotion comes weeks after the YouTube promotion; native LinkedIn article adaptation comes weeks later again. Proposed dates are candidates until reconciled and saved by that channel owner. No claim of natively scheduled future comments/Notes unless proven.
