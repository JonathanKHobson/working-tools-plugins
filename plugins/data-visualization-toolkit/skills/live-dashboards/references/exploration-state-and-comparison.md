## Comparative dashboard construction and review

Use this checkpoint when a reader needs distribution, change over time or relationships across several tools, products or groups. Select only the views that answer the brief; this is not a mandatory dashboard layout.

- Map each reader question to one view before coding. A shared-scale multi-series line chart supports change over time; aligned bars support magnitude or share; a matrix can show a supported relationship. An aggregate total cannot substitute for a requested comparison. Give a second encoding a distinct job.
- Keep series identity consistent across views with color plus labels or patterns. Use a common time domain and comparable units. At a selected time, expose each series value together. Define share denominators and whether a series toggle filters totals or only hides a line; the visible result must follow that choice.
- Apply useful date, category and search controls consistently. Preserve selection through compatible updates, provide reset and handle no matches. Use the viewer's local calendar boundaries when that is the requested scope, not just locally formatted labels over differently bucketed data.
- Keep raw model/tool identifiers, instrumentation notes and long methods behind clearly named details. Make freshness quiet when healthy and actionable when stale. A dated screenshot is not a live data source. Relationship views need a valid shared key and must state the actual relationship in plain language; co-use does not establish causation or quality.
- Separate transient hover, keyboard focus and persistent selection. A highlight must stay attached to the correct mark, keep other series readable and leave layout stable. Make the same comparison available by keyboard and touch. Test entering, leaving, selecting, clearing and switching series, including after a filter or incoming update.
- When motion is requested or improves continuity, specify the event, start/end states, duration and interruption behavior. Animate a meaningful change, such as a filtered series moving to its new values, without making values unreadable. Preserve stable identities and avoid replaying entrance animation on every update. Provide an immediate or gentler reduced-motion path that preserves the same values and comparison. No perpetual animation is needed.
- Review the actual transition in the rendered result. Hover styling, an animation specification, CSS declarations or a reduced-motion rule alone do not establish implemented motion design. Record what moved and whether rapid changes, focus, selection and reduced motion worked. Keep missing or awkward motion as an open item rather than counting it as delivered.

For reuse, retain a small recipe in the owning project: source/derived data shape, aggregation and missingness rules, chart/domain decisions, state model, relevant implementation entry points, final screenshots and interaction evidence. Retain the user’s actual acceptance scope or criticism separately from reviewer checks and unresolved defects; do not invent acceptance thresholds. Adapt the recipe to the next audience and identity instead of copying its palette or private dataset. A reference can be strong in comparison design while remaining unfinished in motion.
