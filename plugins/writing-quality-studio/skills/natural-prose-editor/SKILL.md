# Natural prose editor

Use this skill for the final wording pass on authored prose. Bring the supplied text or drafting facts, the intended reader and medium, and any source or claim constraints. Return the requested rewrite, located diagnosis, or release review at the depth the task needs.

## Final outgoing pass

Review all agent-authored reader-facing content before presenting it as ready or handing it to a delivery action: websites, product UI, slides, documents, reports, teaching materials, scripts, emails, replies, contact messages, posts, articles and newsletters. Apply this pass even when review was not requested. This is the everyday Humanizer; load the Deep Audit corpus only for its evidence-specific triggers. Domain owners retain facts, structure, accessibility and interaction requirements. Routine messages need a close read, not a strategy exercise. Check the exact final subject/title, body, closing, captions and action labels; recheck changed text after personalization, shortening, formatting or native-editor edits. Reuse a review only while its text and relevant context remain unchanged.

For a saved reader-facing artifact, record the completed review through Work Management when installed. Read its `references/operations/artifact-quality-evidence.md`; the portable commands keep the receipt in the project without Codex instructions or private hooks. Mark `communication_review` for the final file with this skill as reviewer and specific evidence, then verify under the same session or workspace identity. Changed bytes require a new review. Skill reads do not count as review events. If the recorder is unavailable, complete the wording review and state the recording gap.

Start with the supplied text or drafting facts. Infer the reader, purpose, medium, and intended voice; ask only if a missing choice would materially change the result. Keep the author's strongest specific detail, cadence, and deliberate roughness. Preserve names, numbers, quotations, uncertainty, technical terms, and claim boundaries.

First identify what the reader needs to understand or do here. Prefer concrete subjects, active verbs and sentences of useful length. Cut filler, repeated explanations and production commentary the reader does not need. Let the surrounding interface or visual carry meaning it already makes clear. Preserve necessary consequences, qualifications, recovery steps, accessible labels, deliberate rhythm and voice. Move useful secondary detail to an appropriate location instead of deleting it indiscriminately. Shortest is not the acceptance standard; accurate, sufficient and easy to use is.

Compare the final copy directly with its source. Every capability verb, guarantee, number and qualifier needs support. “Review” does not establish “approve”; “saved locally” is not equivalent to “saved”; a median latency is not a promise for every result. Preserve those distinctions and remove unsupported additions before claiming the meaning is intact. A proposed alternative must pass the same check as the main version.

Keep internal deliberation, abandoned options, implementation notes and reviewer instructions in the work record unless they change this reader's decision. A fresh recipient needs the accepted result and relevant constraints. Do not introduce an alternative merely to announce its rejection. Explain material omissions or tradeoffs to the commissioning user when useful, separately from the delivered copy.

When writing on Kyle Hobson’s behalf, his authored outgoing email and direct correspondence, including newsletter email editions, must contain zero em dashes (U+2014). Other recipients use their own stated house style. Rewrite the sentence naturally with a period, comma, colon or parentheses; do not mechanically swap in an en dash or double hyphen. Preserve authentic quoted/forwarded source text and attachments. An exact-text instruction that conflicts with this standing rule needs a specific resolution, not a silent rewrite or silent pass. Use `gmail-send-review` for an authorized Gmail action; browser and other-provider actions need the same content review even though the local connector hook cannot intercept them. A punctuation mark is not evidence of AI authorship.

Choose the requested output: a clean rewrite, a diagnosis with exact locations and suggested changes, or a release review. If the user asks only for a rewrite, return the rewritten prose directly. If they ask only for detection, identify observable writing patterns without guessing who or what wrote it. Do not promise AI-detector outcomes.

Match the depth of change to the request: a light polish preserves the working structure; a substantive rewrite may rebuild it. A word limit is a constraint to check, not a reason to lose caveats or add facts. For procedures, runbooks, or explanations where ambiguity could change an action, read [technical clarity](references/technical-clarity.md).

Revise in this order when needed: unsupported claims and attribution; the point and paragraph sequence; sentence construction; then punctuation and formatting. Generic language is a clue to inspect, not a banned-word list. Replace abstractions with supplied facts; mark missing evidence rather than inventing anecdotes, outcomes, emotions, sources, or deliberate mistakes. Preserve dialect, fragments, repetition, humor, formality, and sharp opinions when they serve the author.

For product-interface copy, the UX-writing specialist owns placement, state and interaction. Apply this final wording pass within that contract.
