# Document revision and version choice

Use when the user provides a long document, several drafts, a tight length constraint, or a passage to paraphrase. A sentence polish needs none of this. Keep the source document, intended audience, required terms and protected spans in view; do not turn a revision into a new factual claim.

## Revise a long document

Before changing sections, make a small working style sheet from the document itself: intended reader and point, spelling variant, key names and terminology, citation form, heading pattern, voice, and any deliberate exceptions. Keep it within the current task; do not create a durable style guide unless requested. Work by meaningful section when needed, but inspect transitions and recurring claims across sections before calling the whole document consistent. A section can read well alone and still contradict the abstract, reuse an old term, or change the level of certainty in the conclusion.

For each section, check its job, strongest evidence, terms, and link to the next. After revision, scan the complete artifact for name/term drift, heading and number styles, duplicate explanations, dangling references, citations, and claims that became stronger or broader. If the whole source is available, complete this pass without asking the user to feed arbitrary chunks. If only part is available, state the boundary of the check.

## Compare drafts when choice is the task

Compare versions against the user's purpose, not an invented naturalness score. Use short exact excerpts or locations to show: what changed, what became clearer, what evidence or caveat moved or vanished, and what voice choice each draft makes. Recommend one version, or a specific combination, with the deciding reason. Distinguish a deliberate tonal tradeoff from an error. A compact review may use:

| Location | Version A | Version B | Reader effect / meaning check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | names the decision late | names it first | B lets the reader act sooner; both preserve the condition |

Do not claim that a revision preserved meaning until the claims, negations, numbers, conditions, quotations, citations and commitments have been compared.

## Shorten, expand, or paraphrase

- **Shorten:** remove duplicated points and filler, combine sentences that do the same job, then compress examples. Cut an actual idea only if the requested limit requires it; tell the user when a material idea or caveat was removed. Count words or characters when the limit is binding.
- **Expand:** add a reason, step, distinction or example grounded in supplied material. Mark a needed personal example or evidence gap rather than inventing one. Do not pad with generic setup or a repeated conclusion.
- **Paraphrase:** change the sentence structure and emphasis while retaining the original proposition, scope and attribution. A paraphrase of someone else's work still needs the appropriate citation; new wording does not make the idea the user's. Preserve technical terms when a synonym would change meaning.

Example: “The change may reduce retries when the cache is available” can become “When the cache is available, the change may reduce retries.” It cannot become “The change eliminates retries.” The condition and uncertainty are part of the claim.

Finish with the requested artifact first. Add a change note only when the user asked for one or a material cut, unresolved citation, or cross-section inconsistency needs attention.
