# Editorial gate for substantial prose

Read only for a release review, consequential public draft, citation-sensitive work, or a repeated voice problem. Ordinary sentence edits need no checklist ceremony.

1. **Evidence:** Trace consequential claims, numbers, quotes, and named sources to the supplied material. A citation is useful only if its source exists and supports the sentence, including the page or time span when claimed. Mark unresolved support; never make an inference look sourced by attaching a nearby link.
2. **Voice:** Compare a representative paragraph with the source or an authentic writing sample when one exists. For substantive writing on the user's behalf, use the [writing-style reference](writing-style.md) for bounded retrieval and provenance. Keep the sample's directness, rhythm, domain vocabulary, cultural idiom, and emotional temperature. Do not substitute polished corporate voice for a clear personal one.
   For an existing public series or newsletter, inspect attributable prior work before a substantial redesign. Preserve the author's deliberate research-to-reflection transitions, humane observations, and distinctive endings where they serve the new piece; an old article is a voice reference, not a fixed template or current fact source.
   Preserve the emotional effect the author intends, including urgency, wonder, anger, optimism or a bold speculative voice when supplied. Verify the evidence without flattening the piece into restrained instructions. Mark what is established, interpreted, predicted or imagined so creative possibility does not masquerade as sourced fact. Keep an individual author's voice profile in the project, not this generic gate.
3. **Structure:** Give each paragraph one job. Read adjacent sentences: each should add relevant information, explain a supported consequence, supply a needed example or serve a deliberate rhythmic function. Repair repeated hooks, metaphors that restate the same point, and contrasts whose alternatives are never explained. Preserve intentional refrains and useful transitions. Check that the opening reaches the actual point and that the ending advances or closes it. Remove recaps that repeat the piece. Keep lists, headings, tables, quotes, emoji, and emphatic punctuation only when they help this reader and medium.
4. **Language:** Inspect clusters of vague importance claims, anonymous authorities, publicity gloss, forced contrast, repeated three-part sequences, synonym cycling, and trailing explanations that add no evidence. Repair the reader's understanding rather than chasing surface markers.
   **Reader attention:** Check every explanation against the reader's current task and what the surrounding design already communicates. Keep decision-critical detail visible, move useful secondary detail to an obvious appropriate location, and omit internal production reasoning or rejected options that do not affect the reader. Preserve meaning, evidence, uncertainty, accessibility and intended emotional effect when shortening. Report cuts only when requested or when an omission changes scope or meaning.
5. **Release:** Re-read exact names, dates, links, citations, placeholders, and destination format. Inspect for unfilled template fields, assistant-only citation or attachment markup, and irrelevant tracking parameters before release. Treat these as cleanup leads: preserve legitimate tokens and required URL parameters, and verify a link before changing it. If source verification is outside the task or unavailable, say what remains unverified. A clean edit is not a fact-check, approval, publication, or detector result.
   For a native newsletter or article, also inspect the reader path: useful interior visual or reusable artifact only when it serves a stated need, claim-level links plus a compact source trail when warranted, one clear primary action, and the saved mobile/desktop/email rendering. Verify expert quotes against originals and button, survey, and media destinations against their actual content, not only a successful page load. These are conditional checks, not a feature quota.

This concise gate does not claim exhaustive citation research, source-archive coverage, or deterministic style-drift measurement. Use an appropriately sourced specialist workflow when those are required.

## Post-edit check

After the last rewrite, personalization or native-editor edit, inspect the exact text that will leave the task. Check the subject/title, body, closing, links, captions and action labels. For Kyle’s authored email/direct correspondence and newsletter email editions, confirm zero U+2014 em dashes, including decoded HTML entities; preserve authentic quotes and forwards. Check plain and HTML alternatives when both are sent. For a tiny message, a deliberate final scan is sufficient; registered Gmail sends also use the deterministic `gmail-send-review` gate. Other providers and browser clicks require manual final-text readback.

For substantial anti-slop work, run the existing `humanizer-deep-audit/scripts/humanizer_lint.py` before revision to locate cues and again afterward to detect leftovers or newly introduced patterns. Its `compound_stack` cue asks whether nearby hyphenated compounds make a sentence hard to decode. Keep established domain terms and meaningful distinctions. Do not chase zero advisory cues or a numerical quality score. Check meaning and claim support after every accepted revision; never add numbers, anecdotes or confidence to satisfy a checker.

A second model can review a substantial consequential piece when it adds useful independent scrutiny. Give it the reader, source facts and scope; compare its changes against those facts. It is optional and neither model agreement nor a clean lint result proves quality. Do not launch another model for a routine confirmation.
