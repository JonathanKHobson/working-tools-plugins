# Spoken composition and rehearsal

Use for a speech, narration, presentation or a passage that must work aloud. Writing Quality owns language and delivery text; teaching sequence, visuals, recording and media editing retain their specialist owners. This independent procedure needs the supplied content and target format, not a separate method plugin.

## Compose for the listener

1. Identify the occasion, audience, main point, intended next action and available duration. Establish whether this is a verbatim script, speaker notes, a voiceover or a live explanation. Use supplied facts and sources; a compelling story must not invent an event, quotation or result.
2. Choose a useful listening arc. Establish the question or situation, develop the evidence or example in the order a listener needs, and end at the requested action or resolved question. Keep essential caveats near the claim they qualify. A lesson may need pauses for practice; a short announcement may need only the action and its condition.
3. Separate spoken words from screen directions, stage cues, literal prompts, slide text and editor notes. Use unmistakable cue formatting so production instructions are not accidentally read aloud. For instructional walkthroughs, apply the complete [spoken lesson script procedure](../../natural-prose-editor/references/spoken-lesson-scripts.md).
4. Write sentences that can be understood without rereading. Explain a necessary unfamiliar term once, place concrete referents before pronouns and keep numbers or comparisons interpretable. A signpost should help the listener locate the argument, not add generic presentation filler.
5. Estimate duration from an explicit assumption about delivery rate, then label that estimate. Technical terms, pauses, demonstration and audience response need additional time. Word count alone is not a timed performance. Reduce duplicated setup before removing evidence or qualifications.

## Rhythm and delivery pass

Read the changed passage aloud when a recording or rehearsal is available; otherwise mark it as a text-only speakability pass. Note exact places where a breath, stress pattern, long clause or repeated sound makes comprehension harder. Break or reorder the sentence while preserving meaning. Use intentional repetition when it helps listeners retain a distinction. Keep the speaker's observed delivery choices instead of imposing a universal brisk or enthusiastic persona.

Write acronyms, names and pronunciation cues for the actual speaker when needed, keeping the display spelling unchanged. Distinguish an emphatic pause from omitted evidence. Any suggested gesture or emphasis is a production proposal, not an observed delivery trait.

## Rehearse and deliver

Time the actual read if timing is claimed. Check alignment with the real slide, demonstration, handout or sound cue and retime changed sections. A fit in a written time budget is still an estimate until rehearsed. Confirm that the ending lands on the requested action and that no private production notes remain inside public spoken copy.

Return the requested script or notes, then the measured duration or labeled estimate, consequential source gaps and only necessary delivery cues. Do not claim a recording or rendered video was produced from a script review.

## Worked check

For a two-minute product explanation, place the user problem before the mechanism, give one source-backed example, retain the limitation beside its claim and end with the real next action. If the read exceeds the available time, remove the repeated introduction before cutting the limitation. A source saying a feature is planned cannot become a spoken promise that it has launched.
