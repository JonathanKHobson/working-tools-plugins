# Audience and story choices

Read when a message needs a deliberate frame, a case story, or a reader-specific explanation. Start with the decision this reader can make and the uncertainty that might stop them. Use the language they know, then explain any necessary insider term once.

Useful structures are options, not templates to fill mechanically:

- **Decision update:** current state → evidence → consequence → recommendation or answerable ask.
- **Case narrative:** situation and user goal → constraint → the author's actual contribution → artifact or decision → supported result or open question.
- **Service pitch:** buyer's problem → fit and scope → comparable proof → a safe next step. Do not invent amenities, outcomes, or scarcity.
- **Teaching explanation:** learner goal → likely misconception → concrete example → practice or feedback → transfer to the real task. Let the instructional-design specialist own the larger lesson or course.

For a public portfolio or career claim, verify authorship, chronology, role, and result before polishing. For a stakeholder update, separate observation, interpretation, recommendation, and requested decision. A vivid example can help the reader understand a pattern; it is not proof of prevalence by itself. The next ask should be no larger than the evidence and relationship support.
