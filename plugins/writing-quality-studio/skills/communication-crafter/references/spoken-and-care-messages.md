# Spoken and care-sensitive messages

Use when the requested artifact will be spoken aloud, delivered to someone dealing with a problem, or used for an apology, condolence or repair. Match the relationship and the stakes. Do not turn every short note into a speech or customer-service script.

## Script for the ear

Identify the occasion, audience, speaker, available time and one thought listeners should retain. Draft in speakable units, with one main idea per breath where natural. Repeat a key term when listeners cannot scroll back; use a short signpost for a real turn in the argument. Keep complicated numbers, parentheticals and long lists out of the spoken line or support them with a visual. Mark a pause only when delivery needs it. Read the draft aloud, estimate duration from its actual words and delivery style, and trim or expand against the requested time. A word-per-minute estimate is a planning aid, not proof of live timing.

For a toast, one authentic memory may carry the speech. For a eulogy or other sensitive occasion, ask only for a detail that materially affects the draft; do not invent a relationship, memory, feeling or belief. For teaching narration or software walkthroughs that depend on authentic prior delivery, use the natural-prose-editor's [spoken lesson scripts](../../natural-prose-editor/references/spoken-lesson-scripts.md). Video production owns footage and edit decisions.

## Customer support or service recovery

Ground the reply in the customer's actual issue and channel. Acknowledge the specific problem in their terms, apologize once if warranted, give only a fix or next step that is known to work or is clearly proposed, and set an expectation only if the owner and timing are confirmed. A numbered procedure helps when the reader must perform ordered actions. Do not imply that a ticket, refund, escalation, shipment, or technical fix has occurred merely because the message was drafted. Close with a clear way to continue the conversation when relevant.

Example structure: “I can see the export stops after the third page. Please send the file type and the error text so we can reproduce it. I’ll update this thread after the test.” The last commitment is appropriate only if the sender has agreed to own that follow-up; otherwise ask who will provide the update.

## Personal repair, apology and condolence

Keep a personal message proportionate to the relationship. An apology names the specific action and its effect, accepts the author's real responsibility, and offers repair without demanding forgiveness; avoid “if you were offended.” A condolence can be brief, specific and quiet. Do not invent a shared memory, prescribe a feeling, promise that things will be fine, or turn grief into a lesson. Preserve the user's ordinary texting style, including fragments or punctuation, when it fits. Return the requested message first; variants are optional only when asked or when a real tone choice remains unresolved.
