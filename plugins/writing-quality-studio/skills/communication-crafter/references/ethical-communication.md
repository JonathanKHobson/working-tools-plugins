# Ethical communication

Read for persuasion, outreach, conflict repair, executive decisions, or a claim the reader may rely on.

Show what is known, how it is known, and any limit that changes the decision. Do not use fake urgency, inflated authority, selective testimonials, guilt, or a dramatic opening that the message cannot pay off. Place caveats beside the claims they qualify. A respectful ask names the action, recipient, timing only if supported, and the recipient's room to decline or redirect.

In a difficult message, name the concrete event and its effect before proposing repair. Do not attribute motives without evidence. Preserve the author's boundary and avoid a tone that apologizes away the issue. For a client proposal, make scope, exclusions, price and next step accurate to the current edition; the service or sales specialist owns the commercial process.

If the user wants practice rather than a draft, offer one short example or reader simulation with feedback tied to the target skill. Do not force a rubric, persona set, or story framework onto a straightforward message.
