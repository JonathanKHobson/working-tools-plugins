# Follow up from comments, reviews or a working thread

Use for a message that advances an existing discussion, design/research review, interview debrief or stakeholder decision. A generic email does not need this reference. Start from supplied excerpts and the latest relevant conversation; retrieve exact comments or app state only when they change the draft.

Distinguish who authored each material point and whether it was proposed, reviewed, posted, sent, resolved or still open. The user's final edits outrank earlier assistant suggestions. A historical recording proves what was said then, not today's team state or approval. For review findings, separate observation, likely consequence, recommendation and unanswered question; an opinion is not a user-tested result.

Open with the contribution and its verified status. Summarize only the themes the recipient needs to understand the linked detail, then answer the pending question or make one request that advances the decision. Keep detailed critique with its artifact. Do not invent owners, deadlines, meetings, study commitments, response times or consensus.

Use the current channel and requested output. Preserve an email's existing subject when replying. Verify names, links, list counts and status claims. Return one paste-ready message unless variants are requested; keep source/status notes outside its body. A missing behavioral fact can become an answerable question, not an invented recommendation.

Drafting, saving an app draft, posting and sending remain separate states. Complete an authorized external action through its existing owner and verify the result; a reference or old message does not itself authorize sending.


## Check what this reader already has

Use this pass when the actual thread or supplied exchange shows that a reply repeats the recipient's own background, diagnosis, or agreed evidence before reaching the decision. A standalone article, a forwarded message for new recipients, and a teaching explanation may need that context. If the surrounding conversation is unavailable, do not assume the reader knows it or remove necessary background.

1. Identify the pending question or decision and the intended recipient. Mark which statements the recipient already supplied or explicitly accepted, which are new, and which change their ability to agree or act. Use the visible exchange; familiarity with the topic is not evidence that they saw this particular fact.
2. Open with the supported answer, decision, or bounded recommendation. Preserve the relationship and the writer's actual degree of agreement. A proposed next step must still read as proposed.
3. Retain the new evidence, consequence, caveat, or link that could change the recipient's decision. Trim repeated diagnosis and proof that merely rehearses shared context. Put detailed commands, queries, and supporting counts in the ticket or document when that artifact exists or the user requested it; do not invent a ticket, link, or completed follow-up to make the reply short.
4. Check every cut against the source. Keep an essential safety, legal, access, privacy, or uncertainty statement even when familiar; retain detail requested by the recipient. For a reply copied to a new audience, restore enough background to stand alone. Keep a greeting or sign-off that serves the real relationship.
5. Return the requested message directly. Re-read its opening against the pending question and confirm that the recipient has enough information to take the next authorized step. Do not turn this into a detector judgment or a mandatory length target.

**Worked example (fictional review thread):** The recipient has already identified that a patch is a workaround. The supplied draft explains a shared merge-service defect, proposes a backfill, cites 123 past merges and six currently wrong rows, then asks whether to widen an account-specific PR.

**Reply:** “Keep this PR scoped to the account. Fixing the shared merge service also requires reviewing the merge flow across accounts and backfilling the affected rows, so I propose a separate ticket for that work.”

This keeps the decision, scope reason, and proposed state. The counts and query may move to a requested technical ticket, but they stay in the reply when their scale affects the reader's choice or the recipient asked for them. Creating or sending that ticket remains a separate authorized action.

**Counterexample:** A new reviewer asks for the diagnosis and affected-row count before approving the workaround. Provide that evidence; brevity must not hide the basis for approval.
