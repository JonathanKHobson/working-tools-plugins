# claim, warrant and reader agency

Integrated 2026-09-30 in the existing communication-crafter owner. Use for consequential recommendations, invitations with material uncertainty and objection responses. Existing ethical-communication.md remains the primary ethics rule; audience-and-story.md remains the first reader/decision framing reference. This reference supplies a small audit when “point + proof + next action” is insufficient.

## The six-part working map

1. Claim: write exactly what you want the reader to believe or decide, at the strength the evidence allows.
2. Grounds: list observations, records and current status, each with provenance. A citation is not a warrant and a plausible anecdote is not prevalence evidence.
3. Warrant: state the assumption connecting grounds to claim. Ask whether it holds for this audience, time and setting. If it is a value judgment, label it; if it needs empirical backing, identify the missing study.
4. Backing: add a source only when it supports the warrant. Separate a professional ethical norm from evidence that a tactic causes a result.
5. Qualifier: bound the claim with the specific uncertainty, not vague hedging. “Availability unknown” belongs beside a capacity statement. “Captions planned, not confirmed” belongs beside access information.
6. Rebuttal: articulate the strongest serious objection the reader could reasonably make; concede its supported cost, then answer it without inflating the claim. If the objection survives, narrow the recommendation or withhold it.

NCA's 2024 Credo is a normative ethics source (E04), not a guarantee that this map changes behavior. Current local owner rules prohibit false urgency, invented authority, selective proof and caveats detached from claims.

## Route by purpose

For an internal recommendation, complete the map and turn it into plain prose; do not display the scaffold unless the reader needs it. For a public announcement, keep only the grounds and qualifications the reader needs to make a fair choice. For an objection response, show the real tradeoff before the answer. For a neutral research question, stop: use Survey Studio's instrument method, avoid a benefit frame and any premise that presupposes a positive outcome. Kairos means a real decision window such as the October 17 deadline; it never means “seats are filling fast” when current availability is unknown.

## Worked example

Claim: stage Open Practice Lab copy now but hold a registration CTA until a live destination is verified. Grounds: the brief supplies two October dates and a deadline, but the CTA is a placeholder. Warrant: asking someone to register should provide a usable path. Qualifier: the recommendation changes when registration becomes live. Objection: delay reduces planning time. Response: resolve the route promptly, or publish a factual details-forthcoming notice with a real update channel; do not pretend the link works. The full map and prose response are in packet examples/argument-and-neutrality.md.

Counterexample: “Since the lab is free and has 24 places, register now before it fills.” Free and total capacity do not prove current availability, and the registration path is not live. Another counterexample is a soft tone that says “we hope it helps” while a headline still promises a proven outcome.

## Acceptance test

A reviewer must be able to underline the claim, each ground, the warrant, the specific qualifier and the strongest objection in the finished passage. Every factual ground must match the ledger; every rhetorical appeal must have a legitimate role. If the evidence supports only “an opportunity to practice,” do not expand it to “improves skill.” If a neutral question leads the respondent, route back to Survey Studio. Human reader and instrument testing remain separate; none is claimed here.
