# Working brief and applied revision record

This is the design record for the fictional Open Practice Lab examples, not an active campaign brief or Kyle's personal voice profile.

## Rhetorical situation

The speaker is an unnamed program publisher; no organizer credentials are supplied. Prospective readers are adults deciding whether a free, online practice opportunity fits their needs. The relationship is a public invitation, not a promise of training results. The real decision window is an October 17, 2027 registration deadline before October 20 and 27 sessions. The two sessions run 6:00–7:30 p.m. Phoenix time. Total capacity is 24, while current availability is unknown. A dashboard can be brought, or a supplied fictional dataset used. Captions are planned but unconfirmed; a recording decision is pending; no participant outcomes have been evaluated. The CTA is a nonlive placeholder. The communication should permit a reader to wait for access details or decline. No urgency beyond the deadline is justified.

## Voice decisions (task voices, not personal imitation)

| Version | Stance / reader relationship | Observable prose choices | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| V1 notice | Public administrator to reader | Declarative heading; facts and limits in short blocks; no metaphor | Does not turn total capacity into open seats |
| V2 conversation | Peer invitation | Direct question and second person; contractions; logistics in ordinary sentences | Does not claim to know the reader's frustration or need |
| V3 decision note | Adviser to autonomous reader | Question as decision; known offer then constraints; conditional next action | Does not claim validated learning benefit |
| V4 reflection | Quiet editorial guide | One image about inspecting a dashboard; varied sentence length; logistics quickly follow | Image does not become an invented participant story |
| V5 orientation | Facilitator-like instruction | Reader choices first, then schedule, then access caveats; restrained imperatives | No implied organizer identity or guaranteed support |
| V6 playful | Informal peer invitation | Brief joke/fragments followed by labelled facts and limitations | No joke about access and no actionable CTA before the route exists |

Stable across all: fact ledger F1–F11, the distinction between total capacity and current availability, planned versus confirmed access, pending recording, no measured outcome, and a nonlive CTA. Across a longer document these terms and claim strengths must remain stable; warmth or detail may change between invitation, FAQ and limitation sections.

## Claim and evidence audit

| Candidate claim | Evidence in brief | Permitted wording | Disallowed expansion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| It is free | Explicit brief fact | Free | “No cost of any kind” if travel, internet or equipment costs are unknown |
| Adults can attend without experience | Explicit brief fact | For adults; no prior experience required | “Suitable for everyone” or accessibility guarantee |
| Capacity is 24 total | Explicit brief fact | 24 participants total | “24 seats remain” or 24 per session |
| Registration closes October 17 | Explicit brief fact | Deadline is October 17, 2027 | “Almost full” or “last chance today” |
| Captions | Planned, unconfirmed | Captions are planned but not confirmed | “Captioned sessions” |
| Recording | Decision pending | Recording decision is pending | “Replay available” or “never recorded” |
| Skill outcome | No participant-outcome evaluation | No result promise | “Proven to improve dashboard skills” |
| Registration action | Placeholder, not live | Registration information forthcoming | “Register now” link |

The warrant for an invitation is limited: a free opportunity with an option to bring one's own dashboard may be relevant to some adults, but no evidence in the brief supports a learning-effect claim. A citation to rhetoric theory would not change that evidentiary gap.

## Applied revision workflow

1. Draft each version from the same ledger, choosing a reader job and sequence first.
2. Compare openings, paragraph order, sentence shape, direct address and caveat placement. If only adjectives or excitement level change, redraft.
3. Audit status-bearing words: “planned,” “confirmed,” “pending,” “available,” “will,” “may,” “proven,” “remaining.” The six passages keep planned/unknown/pending distinctions.
4. Audit numbers and nouns: 24 total rather than seats left; two dates with year and Phoenix time; one dashboard versus supplied fictional dataset.
5. Check the claim-to-warrant bridge for the internal publication recommendation, then write and answer the strongest objection (lead time) in argument-and-neutrality.md.
6. Stop after the material facts and voice choices are defensible. Further embellishment would add risk without changing the reader's decision.

This record was completed by the writer. It does not replace a second reviewer, actual participant reading or a current registration/access readback.
