# Worked argument, objection and neutral counter-route

These are original Codex examples about the same fictional Open Practice Lab brief in same-fact-voices.md. They are internal exercises, not a live policy decision or released copy. The Toulmin terms here follow Purdue OWL's teaching page, which describes Toulmin's method; the original 1958 book was not inspected (source E02).

## Rhetorical situation and decision

Speaker: a program editor advising the person who controls an announcement. Reader: that decision owner. Medium: internal decision note. Exigence: an October 17, 2027 registration deadline exists, but registration information is only a placeholder; caption and recording decisions are unsettled. The decision is whether the current announcement can be published as if registration were open. The ethical constraint is to preserve prospective participants' ability to plan around access and availability, not to generate urgency from uncertainty.

## Claim chain

- **Claim:** Prepare the announcement now, but do not publish a registration call to action until a real registration destination and its status are confirmed. If captions and recording remain unsettled, label them exactly as such.
- **Grounds:** The brief supplies two October sessions, a deadline and a total capacity, but no live registration link, no current availability, no confirmed captions, no recording decision and no participant-outcome evaluation.
- **Warrant:** A call to register should give the reader a usable path and should not imply access or a place that the publisher cannot substantiate. The facts support an invitation to consider the lab, but not a claim that registration is currently possible or that seats remain.
- **Backing:** Current Writing Quality Studio ethical-communication.md requires proof and caveats near claims; NCA's 2024 Credo is a normative ethical statement favoring truthfulness, accuracy and informed decision-making (E04). This backing does not prove the lab will work.
- **Qualifier:** This is a publication recommendation under the brief's current state. It changes if the registration destination goes live and access details are confirmed.
- **Strong objection:** Waiting may reduce the time adults have before the October 17 deadline, and the two October sessions could need advance notice.
- **Response:** That concern is real. Publish a factual “details forthcoming” notice only if it has an actual channel where people can check updates, or first make the registration route live. Do not convert the deadline into “places are filling fast”: capacity is 24 total, but availability is unknown. State captions and recording as pending so readers can decide whether to wait for those details. The remaining tradeoff is less lead time against a reliable call to action; neither side has measured outcome evidence here.
- **Evidence gap:** No data on expected attendance, registration behavior, access needs or participant learning. No result claim can be justified from these facts.

## Full internal prose response to the objection

I agree that delaying the announcement costs people planning time before October 17. The answer is to resolve the registration route quickly, not to publish a button that goes nowhere. Right now we can accurately describe a free two-session online lab for adults, the October 20 and 27 schedule, the 24-person total capacity and the choice to bring a dashboard or use fictional data. We cannot say places are available, that captions are confirmed, that a recording will exist or that the lab has demonstrated outcomes. I would stage the five copy directions, make the registration destination live, and then publish the chosen version with the pending access details plainly marked. If that route cannot be ready soon, a “details forthcoming” notice needs a real update channel. This accepts the timing objection while keeping the reader's decision grounded in what we know.

**Rationale:** The claim is a process recommendation, not a disguised outcome claim. Its grounds are the known/missing fact ledger. The warrant explicitly connects a functioning action path to a fair invitation. The objection receives a genuine cost, not a caricature, and the response preserves a path to earlier notice without inventing urgency.

## Counterexample: a weaker argument

“Seats are almost gone, so register now; this proven lab will transform your dashboard skills.” Every material phrase exceeds the evidence: current availability is unknown, the link is not live, and no participant outcomes have been evaluated. A smoother voice cannot repair this argument.

## When persuasion is the wrong route

A neutral question in a future evaluation should not prompt favorable answers. Draft question: “If you attended Open Practice Lab, what, if anything, changed about how you inspect a dashboard afterward?” Add response options and sampling only through the research owner. Do not ask, “How much did our helpful lab improve your dashboard skills?” The latter presupposes improvement and steers the answer. No survey has been run, and this question is not evidence of an outcome. U13 is a user-authored research reflection that cautions against leading questions; Survey Studio owns instrument design, consent, distribution and analysis.
