# same-fact voice selection

Integrated 2026-09-30 in the existing voice-and-style owner. Use when the user requests genuinely different voice directions or a reusable task voice. Existing Write Like Me and brand/long-document procedures remain operative for explicit style matching. This method changes selection and evaluation, not the user's approved personality.

## First establish the truth and the writing job

Write a compact fact ledger with known, unknown, proposed and prohibited inferences. Record exact names, dates, times/time zones, counts, prices, links, accessibility status, outcome status and source/approval state. Select a single writing job: inform, invite, recommend, explain, teach, repair, or ask a neutral question. A research instrument exits the persuasive branch and goes to Survey Studio. Do not use an old writing sample as approval for new facts or a historical class paper as the user's current default voice.

## Choose two to five viable directions

For each direction, choose a distinct opening move and reading sequence before choosing adjectives. Example directions: public notice puts logistics first; conversation starts with a reader question; decision note leads with a bounded claim and evidence; editorial reflection uses one concrete thought then quickly supplies facts; teaching orientation begins with a reader choice. Record the relationship: peer to peer, public administrator to reader, adviser to decision owner, or teacher to learner. Then make 2–3 observable prose decisions: sentence length and cadence, degree of direct address, register and jargon, narrative distance, imagery, warmth/humor/intensity. Do not require every axis to differ; require the differences to serve the job.

A voice profile is a working decision record, not a slider set: “Direct, second person, short first sentence, longer explanatory second sentence; no jokes about access needs; never convert planned captions to confirmed.” Add one positive 2–3 sentence sample and one near-miss counterexample. Stable across a longer work: terms, relationship, evidence standard, claim strength and source status. Local tone may change between welcome, instructions and limitations while those invariants hold. If the writing samples are not attributable and authorized, call it a task voice rather than a personal voice match.

## Draft, audit, choose

Draft each version independently from the ledger, not by synonym-swapping the first draft. Compare structure, stance, rhythm and reader agency. Then inspect every number, date, noun phrase, modal and promise for mutation. Place caveats next to the claim they change. Treat “capacity 24 total” and “24 places available” as different claims. Treat “planned” and “confirmed” as different states. Keep a placeholder visibly nonfunctional. Reject a version that sounds fresh but changes one material fact. Ask reviewers to name actual text differences; a label such as “warm professional” is not evidence of distinct voice.

## Worked example

The fictional Open Practice Lab has two online sessions October 20 and 27, 2027, 6:00–7:30 p.m. Phoenix time, for adults, free, no prerequisite; total capacity 24 with availability unknown. A notice can begin with dates; a conversational invitation can begin “Have a dashboard you want to think through?” Both must still say captions are planned but unconfirmed, recording is undecided, no participant outcomes have been evaluated, and the registration link is not live. Six complete examples and the F1–F11 audit are in packet examples/same-fact-voices.md.

Counterexample: “Only 24 seats left—register today for guaranteed improvement.” It changes capacity into availability, turns a placeholder into an action and invents an outcome. Another counterexample is five identical paragraph orders with only adjectives, exclamation points or emoji changed.

## Acceptance test and limits

On a same-fact brief, produce three directions. Pass only when at least two dimensions among hierarchy, stance, reader relationship, syntax/rhythm and narrative distance differ for a task reason, and the fact/qualification ledger has zero material mutations. A blind human reader review can probe recognizability and fit; none was run for this packet. Do not promise improved persuasion or comprehension from the method alone. Aristotle's style/arrangement distinction is historical theory (E01), Mailchimp is one practitioner's brand example (E03), and Morkes/Nielsen's same-information website test has a narrow 1997 task context (E05).

## Resolve meaning before asserting status

If a count could mean capacity, registrations or attendance, preserve its supplied wording and mark its meaning unresolved; do not choose the convenient interpretation. “Page unavailable” does not establish that no page exists, that registration is closed, or that no alternate route exists. “No evaluation supplied” does not establish that nobody has evaluated it. Keep these distinctions through every voice.

Copy no fixture date, personal pronoun or organizer decision into a new brief by analogy. Do not infer a short deadline window without a relevant current/reference date. Different versions keep the same audience and factual offer unless the user explicitly asks for a different audience or deliverable. A decision-note voice for prospective attendees remains for attendees; it is not automatically an internal publication recommendation. Record deliberate omissions when a channel requires them, and keep every decision-changing qualification.

## Status drift counterexamples and independent readback

After drafting, compare each assertion to its actual source, not to the draft’s own audit table. A self-reported pass is not evidence of fidelity. Correct the copy before marking a row passed.

| Source | Unsupported expansion | Faithful wording |
|---|---|---|
| Registration page not yet available | “You cannot register yet”; “nothing to sign up through” | “The registration page is not yet available.” Other channels remain unknown. |
| No participant-outcome evaluation | “Unassessed practice”; “nobody evaluates participants” | “No participant-outcome evaluation has been reported in this brief.” Program results and individual assessment are different. |
| Two dates, one hour on each | “An hour of practice” as the entire commitment | State both dates and each session’s duration without collapsing the commitment. |
| No current/reference date | “The deadline window is short” | State the supplied date; omit relative urgency. |
| Optional personal artifact | “What did you do with your artifact?” asked of everyone | Establish participation/artifact use before a relevant outcome question. |

These are negative transfer fixtures, not language to reproduce. When an independent readback is available, give it the original brief and actual prose rather than only the writer’s mutation checklist. If it is not available, perform the same source-first check and state its limits.
