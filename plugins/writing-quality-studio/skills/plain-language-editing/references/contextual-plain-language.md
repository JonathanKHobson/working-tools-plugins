# Contextual plain-language adapter

This owner governs the inherited Plain English, Non-native English and Multilingual editing procedures linked by this skill. Read their complete relevant methods and supporting files; preserve their useful examples, editing sequence and language detail. Where they conflict, current user instructions, exact meaning, the actual audience and deliberate author voice govern. The archived source bodies stay unchanged as provenance, not as a higher-priority rule set.

## Resolve the inherited conflicts

Establish the reader's task, required terminology, intended register and requested edit depth before assessing a sentence. Use the source checklist to find candidate issues, then decide whether a change would help that reader. Do not require a flag merely because a vocabulary or punctuation rule fires; report an issue only when you can name its actual consequence, such as an unclear actor, unsupported claim or distracting redundancy. The source's flag-first requirement and restricted one-word override reasons do not displace this contextual assessment.

- Treat the hard vocabulary list as prompts to inspect usage, not automatic substitutions. “Robust” may describe a defined statistical method; “leverage” may name a mechanical property. Explain necessary terminology for the audience rather than weakening its meaning. An explicit user or publication vocabulary constraint still applies.
- Assess punctuation by syntax, readability, medium and voice. An inherited em-dash quota is not a default acceptance rule. Preserve a deliberate aside or interruption where it works; change punctuation where the sentence becomes difficult to follow.
- Vary sentence length only if doing so improves the passage. Repeated short sentences may support scanning, instructions, emphasis or a supplied voice. Do not invent long clauses, synonym rotation or deliberate mistakes to manufacture variation.
- Retain meaningful uncertainty, modality and attribution. “Should” cannot become “must” unless the actual requirement supports it. One hedge may represent a genuine condition; a stronger sentence must not become a stronger claim.
- Follow the requested output mode. A clean rewrite does not require a flag report or affiliate footer. For a critique, cite the problematic span and its consequence; distinguish optional taste from a meaning or task defect. Do not infer authorship or promise detector outcomes from vocabulary, sentence lengths, punctuation, placeholders or any other motif.

Preserve quotations and protected file spans under the source's edit-mode boundaries. Learner corrections and localization follow [language-sensitive editing](../../natural-prose-editor/references/language-and-learning.md); “native-like” is not a mandate to erase dialect or regional expression. Technical controlled-language requirements belong to `technical-instructions`, not a blend of conflicting prose rules.

## Paired examples (fictional)

**Revise:** “Our robust ecosystem leverages synergies to deliver value.” If the supplied facts say that the tool combines duplicate invoices, write “The tool combines duplicate invoices.” The fix adds specificity grounded in the source, not a forbidden-word substitution.

**Retain:** “The confidence interval uses a robust standard error.” Keep the method name when it is accurate; explain it for a newcomer if needed. Replacing “robust” with “strong” would change the technical meaning.

**Revise:** “Check availability, choose a session, and register” when availability checks, approval and payment are separate necessary steps. Restore the supplied dependencies; do not preserve a triad at their expense.

**Retain:** “Read. Try. Reflect.” when these are the three verified stages and the brief deliberately uses short parallel prompts. Similar length and three items are not themselves defects.

For a consequential restructure, use the [controlled contrast and mutation audit](../../natural-prose-editor/references/controlled-contrast-revision.md); a routine sentence edit needs only a direct source comparison.

## Evidence and limits

This adapter is an editorial policy and proposed synthesis, not a validated detector or a measured intervention. It draws on the admitted *Intentional UX* research package (2 October 2026), especially its content/voice distinctions and false-positive protections. No screenshots, transcript, or embedded automation handoff are incorporated here.

[Agarwal, Naaman and Vashistha (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.11360v2) studied AI suggestions in an English-writing setting with 118 participants in India and the US. The package reviewed the full author version. It supports attention to cultural flattening in that setting; it does not define how entire demographics speak or validate universal editing rules. [Design Space Analysis](https://europe.naverlabs.com/history/past-research/design-space-analysis/) provides Questions, Options and Criteria as a rationale method (project overview and linked bibliographic reference reviewed in the package). Recording a reason is not evidence that readers benefited. Reader comprehension, voice-owner approval and model-output comparisons remain separate checks; no new efficacy or benchmark result is claimed here.
