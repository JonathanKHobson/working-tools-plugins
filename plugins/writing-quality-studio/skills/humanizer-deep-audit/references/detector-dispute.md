# Responding to a disputed AI-writing allegation

Use only when the user asks about a detector result, a false accusation, or a process response. Ordinary editing and an author's curiosity about style do not need an appeal workflow. Start with the actual notice and the current school, employer, publisher or platform policy when available; do not infer a violation, right of appeal, deadline or standard of proof from a generic detector score.

## Establish the record

Separate what is documented from what the author remembers. Record the passage, version, detector name and report if supplied, date, reviewer statement, applicable policy text, required response channel and deadline. A percentage is a tool output, not a probability of misconduct. If the report or policy is unavailable, say so and ask for the smallest missing item that changes the response. Do not fabricate false-positive rates or quote a detector's terms from memory.

Collect only evidence the user already has or authorizes you to inspect: dated drafts, version history, outline, reading/source notes, tracked edits, assignment or project brief, disclosure made at the time, and records of permitted tools. Preserve originals and timestamps; describe each item accurately. The author can also explain a specific argument, example, source choice or revision in their own words. An explanation is useful process evidence, not an interrogation or proof by itself. Do not manufacture drafts or backdate notes.

## Choose the next action

1. Check whether the current policy permits the writing assistance actually used. If assistance occurred, describe it truthfully; do not coach the user to conceal it.
2. Identify what the reviewer is asking the user to answer. Request the exact passage, tool/report, decision basis and review path when those are unknown and material.
3. Build a short response around verifiable process evidence and a specific request: correction of a factual error, human review of the drafts, a meeting to explain the work, or the stated appeal route. Keep emotion proportionate; avoid accusing the reviewer of bad faith without evidence.
4. Distinguish a prepared response, submitted appeal, acknowledgement and outcome. If authorized to send through an external channel, use its owner and verify the actual state.

Example opening when supported: “I wrote this section from the attached outline and source notes. The version history shows revisions on [verified dates]. I would appreciate a review of those materials alongside the detector report and the policy section applied to this passage.” Replace bracketed dates with actual records; never leave them as evidence claims.

If no history exists, say that directly. The user can still explain their process, check citations and ask for a fair review. Do not promise a detector will reverse its result or that an appeal will succeed. Use [human signals and false positives](human-signals-and-false-positives.md) for limits of style judgment and the provenance evidence ladder.
