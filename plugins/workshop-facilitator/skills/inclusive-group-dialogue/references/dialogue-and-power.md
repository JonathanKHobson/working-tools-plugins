# Dialogue, power, and participation

## Opening
State purpose, what is open, who decides, how notes will be used, how to pass, and any confidentiality limits. Use brief language. “We're gathering experiences to inform a recommendation; the sponsor will make the final decision.”

## Observation before interpretation
Name something observable: “We have heard from three people.” Avoid motive claims: “The rest of you are resistant.” Offer a structure that allows contributions without demanding them.

Knowledge Atlas's needs-based dialogue pattern informed the distinction between observation, tentative reflection, and a specific request. Use those moves in ordinary language. Do not require a participant to disclose feelings or follow a formal NVC script.

## Power checks
- Can a participant disagree without being evaluated by someone in the room?
- Is the requested input actually capable of changing the outcome?
- Are anonymous tools anonymous in this setting, including metadata and recognizable stories?
- Does the facilitator hold a substantive position that should be disclosed?
- Are dissent and unresolved matters recorded rather than edited away?

If a decision is fixed, consultation can still help implementation, but say what remains open.

## Participation options
Individual writing; paired practice; verbal contribution; chat; pass and return; short asynchronous input; equivalent solo exercise. Select based on the objective and group. Never imply every modality is equally appropriate for every assessment.

## Repair
1. Pause the process.
2. Describe what happened without diagnosis.
3. Restate the working boundary.
4. Offer a concrete route forward or break.
5. Check willingness to continue.
For harassment, threats, discrimination, or safety concerns use the relevant organizational response rather than trying to mediate everything in public.

## Capture
Record substantive ideas with source IDs if authorized. Ask before attributing a sensitive anecdote. Keep a private disclosure out of the shared summary unless the speaker agreed to that use. A facilitator cannot promise absolute confidentiality on behalf of the room.

## Close
Ask whether the summary reflects the contributions and what is missing. Preserve an objection without treating it as obstruction. Distinguish being heard, agreeing, consenting, and committing.
