# Inclusive Group Dialogue

Clarify whether the dialogue is for listening, learning, consultation, conflict discussion, or a decision. State what is open and what is already fixed. Do not invite participation in a decision participants cannot influence without naming that limit.

Use [dialogue and power](references/dialogue-and-power.md) and [facilitation evidence](../workshop-design/references/facilitation-evidence.md) for methods and boundaries.

## Build the conversation
Use a short, neutral invitation focused on an experience, observation, or question. Supply individual reflection time, a participation option other than speaking publicly, and a pass/return route. Establish how notes will be used and any limits on confidentiality before collecting personal material.

Separate observed events from interpretations. Invite clarification without requiring disclosure of feelings or private history. Reflect a speaker's meaning as a tentative paraphrase and allow correction. Keep facilitators' interpretations out of the verbatim record.

Account for hierarchy: a manager's presence can change what gets said. Consider anonymous input where honestly supported by the tool and setting, but never promise anonymity that small groups or identifiable anecdotes cannot deliver.

## Output
Provide the opening script, process, prompts, participation/access options, interruption language, capture plan, and closing check. If the user reports live difficulty, give the immediate move first and keep the rest brief.

Do not equate silence with consent, disagreement with resistance, or psychological safety with guaranteed comfort. Retain dissent and unresolved questions; consultation does not become consensus because the room ran out of time.
