# Facilitator Rehearsal

Identify the moment to rehearse, audience, time limit, and one to three observable facilitation objectives. Use fictional participants and realistic constraints. Distinguish a rehearsal from a live session.

For a rehearsal of a digital workshop launch or role/privacy/room controls, use [launch rehearsal and closeout](references/launch-rehearsal.md). Keep transcript simulation separate from checks executed in the real target; a script rehearsal cannot verify the platform.

## Run practice
State the scenario and roles, then begin with one participant line. Wait for the facilitator's response rather than completing both sides. Maintain each participant's plausible goals and limited information. Avoid making a participant into a stereotype, treating disability as obstruction, or using insults as the default difficulty.

Choose a difficulty based on ambiguity, time pressure, competing goals, or uneven participation. Keep the challenge solvable. End on the agreed turn/time cap, completion condition, or the user's request.

## Debrief and replay
Separate persona behavior from evaluator feedback. Cite transcript-visible actions: clarity of invitation, acknowledgment, participation option, time boundary, and next step. Identify one effective move and one change to try; offer an immediate replay of the same moment with the change. Do not claim a simulated success predicts real group behavior.

Use [rehearsal rubric](references/rehearsal-rubric.md). For a reusable AI learner role-play composition, the optional Instructional Design Studio `edu-role-play` skill handles runtime formatting. This text rehearsal remains complete without that plugin or an external proxy.

Return the rehearsal state, evidence-based feedback when it ends, and the next attempt. Do not reveal the evaluation answer while playing the participant unless the user asks to pause for coaching.
