# Rehearsal rubric

Use a small rubric tied to the chosen moment. Do not evaluate personality or predict professional competence from one simulation.

| Dimension | Emerging | Usable | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Task clarity | Output or action unclear | Gives an actionable instruction | Action, output, and success state clear |
| Participation | Ignores access or coerces input | Offers a viable participation route | Supports multiple routes and preserves agency |
| Evidence and acknowledgment | Assumes motives or misses the point | Reflects the stated concern | Checks interpretation and separates facts from inference |
| Time and transition | No boundary or next step | Gives a cut point | Protects the goal with a clear transition |

Only score dimensions observed in the transcript. Use unobserved where appropriate.

## Example rehearsal setup
“You are opening a 20-minute problem-framing session with six colleagues. One asks, ‘Can we skip this and vote on my solution?’ Practice a concise response that preserves the agreed discovery purpose.”
Play the participant and wait. A plausible follow-up could ask what the exercise will produce. Do not write the facilitator's response for them.

## Feedback
Point to an exact line or observable omission. Give one actionable change, then replay the same moment. Keep coaching separate from persona turns. If the user asks for a script instead of rehearsal, provide the script directly; do not insist on a simulation.
