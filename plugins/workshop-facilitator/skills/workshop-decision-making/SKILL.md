# Workshop Decisions & Convergence

Establish the decision, scope, decision owner, participants' authority, constraints, and decision rule before choosing a voting technique. If authority is missing, the workshop can recommend options but cannot honestly claim to decide.

## Converge
Separate option generation from evaluation. Define criteria in plain language and identify which are evidence-backed, estimates, or values. Make tradeoffs visible. Use a simple criteria comparison before adopting weighted scoring; numerical precision does not eliminate uncertainty.

Use [decision patterns](references/decision-patterns.md) for fit, voting limits, and tie handling. Dot voting records preferences, not proof of the best idea or consensus. Preserve the minority concern that could invalidate a favored option.

For a shared-board decision, keep the chain explicit: evidence/need → candidates → shortlist with reasons → simultaneous comparison → decision or deferral → accepted actions. Preserve original IDs through summaries and merges. Independent thinking precedes collective display; a quiet or absent contributor has not consented.

For impact/effort maps define scale and time horizon. Separate unknown effort from high effort and confidence from impact. If participants disagree about facts, identify the smallest check or experiment rather than forcing a vote to settle the fact.

## Close
Return a decision receipt: selected option or recommendation, rationale, evidence, rule used, participants/authority, dissent, assumptions, owner, next action, date, and revisit trigger. Distinguish proposed owners from accepted commitments.

When asked for discovery only, stop at options or findings; do not introduce prioritization. Draft follow-up communications when useful, but do not send them without authorization.
