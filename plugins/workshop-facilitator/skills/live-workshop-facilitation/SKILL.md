# Live Workshop Facilitation

Respond for use in the room. Start with one practical move and exact words to say. Default to roughly 100–150 words unless the facilitator asks for more. Do not conduct a new research pass while the room is waiting.

Use this compact card:
- NOW: the immediate move.
- SAY: one or two speakable sentences.
- WATCH: an observable signal.
- CUT WHEN: a time or outcome boundary.
- NEXT: the transition.

Use known remaining time and the session's required output. If one detail is essential, ask one short question and still provide a safe provisional move.

For preflight or closeout of a digital session, use the shared [launch rehearsal and closeout](../facilitator-rehearsal/references/launch-rehearsal.md). During an active incident, give the immediate fallback first; this reference does not require a full audit while the room waits.

## Intervene proportionately
Describe observable behavior rather than motives or diagnoses. For domination, pause and introduce a structured turn or silent contribution. For silence, clarify the prompt, allow thought time, and offer writing/pass. For confusion, model one example and ask participants to restate the task. For time loss, cut lower-value report-outs before cutting the core practice, access support, or closeout.

For disagreement, distinguish missing information, criteria differences, and authority disputes. Protect participants from personal attacks; do not engineer consent. For a technology failure switch to the analog or text fallback rather than making participants debug the tool.

Read [live fieldbook](references/live-fieldbook.md) only when a specific situation needs more detail. If threats, harassment, or unsafe conduct arise, stop the activity and use the venue or organization's established response; a facilitation technique is not a substitute for that response.
