## 1-2-4-All
Use for one clear prompt with many possible contributions. Current primary guidance allocates one minute to introduction, one to individual reflection, two to pairs, five to quartets and seven to plenary. Treat these as proposed facilitation timings, not learning-effectiveness evidence; adapt openly to the actual available time. [Original method, Lipmanowicz and McCandless](https://www.liberatingstructures.com/1-2-4-all).
**Say:** “Write one answer first. Compare with a partner. Keep both the overlap and the difference.”
**Watch:** contributions disappearing when a group selects a spokesperson.
**Adapt:** groups of three can compare independently written ideas in a round; label that as an adaptation, not literal 1-2-4-All. Offer shared text for remote participants. Do not force public report-out from each person.
**Stop:** when each group has captured distinct ideas and one unresolved question.

## Think-pair-share
Give a question and quiet thought time, then exchange in pairs and share a question or disagreement. A proposed short version is 2 minutes alone, 3 paired, 3 plenary. The archive cites Harvard Project Zero broadly; this build does not establish the card's exact wording as an official Project Zero routine.
**Access:** allow private writing or an asynchronous response. Do not let the partner speak for someone without permission.
**Debrief:** “What changed after comparison?”

## Troika-inspired peer consultation
For three participants, rotate one challenge owner and two advisers. Use a concrete challenge, clarification, possible approaches, and the owner's chosen takeaway. The inspected source describes the owner turning away during advice; keep the listening phase but offer an accessible orientation instead. This adaptation is not a verbatim method reproduction.
**Boundary:** advice is optional; the challenge owner retains authority. Avoid confidential personal or workplace details.
**Failure signal:** advisers debate their preferred solution without clarifying the owner's goal.

## What / So What / Now What
Use the three questions separately and record their answers in separate fields. [Original method](https://www.liberatingstructures.com/what-so-what-now-what).
**Proposed compact version:** 2 minutes observations, 3 interpretation, 3 actions.
**Watch:** a recommendation being written as a fact.
**Close:** identify an action owner or mark the action proposed.

## Teach-back with counterexample
Card fof-exp-0449 proposes explaining a concept, producing an example and plausible non-example, and explaining the boundary. Its source record cites Cornell study resources but labels the card an original adaptation.
**Workshop version:** learners explain briefly, compare examples in pairs, and save a revised explanation.
**Assess:** the distinction and rationale, not fluency or confidence.
**Fallback:** write or dictate privately and submit a short note.

## Evidence confidence grid
List claims before placing them by decision importance and evidence strength. Attach source IDs; unknown evidence remains unknown. Card fof-exp-0332 is an adaptation with broad research guidance, not an externally validated scoring instrument.
**Prompt:** “Which claim could change our decision?”
**Output:** one consequential uncertainty and the smallest check that could resolve it.
**Avoid:** averaging opinions to manufacture certainty.

## Plus/delta and after-action review
Use plus/delta for brief process improvement, after-action review for comparison between intent and observed events. Source cards fof-exp-0342 and fof-exp-0348 cite broad professional resources; do not claim those resources originated these techniques.
**Ask:** what to retain, what to change, and what evidence supports the change.
**Keep:** process critique separate from blame.
**Close:** select one feasible change rather than a long unowned list.

## Working agreements
Ask participants to identify collaboration needs and friction, then translate them into observable agreements. Source card fof-exp-0355 cites [Atlassian's Working Agreements play](https://www.atlassian.com/team-playbook/plays/working-agreements); the external play was not independently reviewed in this build.
**Example:** “Publish the decision owner in the agenda” is actionable; “be respectful” needs examples.
**Boundary:** a group agreement cannot override rights, policy, or legitimate refusal.
**Review:** name when an agreement will be revisited.
