# Workshop Debrief & Transfer

Match the debrief to what occurred: a practice attempt, group decision, experiment, or exploration. Refer to actual participant artifacts and observations. Do not invent learning or agreement.

Use [debrief patterns](references/debrief-patterns.md) and preserve the distinction between observation, interpretation, and action. Ask one question at a time in live facilitation. A compact sequence can be: “What happened?” then “What changed your view?” then “What will you try?”

## Make learning usable
Compare intention with observed action. Surface a misconception or decision that changed, then ask learners to identify where the principle applies next. A teach-back or changed-context attempt gives stronger evidence than asking whether everyone understood.

Capture one feasible next action, owner, opportunity, support, and check-in condition. If the person has not accepted responsibility, label the assignment proposed. Preserve unanswered questions and dissent. Do not turn reflection into a therapy session or require emotional disclosure.

## Deliver
Produce speakable prompts, timing, a simple reflection/action artifact, and a follow-through message draft if requested. Distinguish intention from later verified behavior. Sending reminders, calendar events, or emails is a separate action requiring authorization.

For a short workshop protect a minimal closeout even when behind schedule: one result, one unresolved issue, one next action. Avoid creating a whole monitoring system when a single dated follow-up answers the need.


## Conditional operational closeout
For a post-session wrap-up involving recordings, attendee messages, learning evidence, agreed billing or permissioned portfolio proof, read [post-session operational closeout](references/operational-closeout.md). Select only applicable work within the request, retain the debrief and transfer above, and hand off current facts and permission rather than repeating intake. A wrap-up request alone does not authorize sending, publishing, payment commitments or recurring monitoring.
