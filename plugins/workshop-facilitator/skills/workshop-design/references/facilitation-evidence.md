## F1. Professional practice
The IAF Core Competencies address client relationships, appropriate process design, participation, useful outcomes, professional knowledge, and the facilitator's stance. The framework includes adapting to changing conditions and distinguishing process from content. [International Association of Facilitators, Core Competencies, official PDF](https://www.iaf-world.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/IAF-Core-Competencies.pdf).

**Application:** make the purpose, authority, roles, and outputs explicit. Adapt the process to the group rather than defending an agenda. This is professional guidance, not evidence that one timing template is universally effective, and using it does not confer IAF certification.

## F2. Psychological safety has limits
Amy Edmondson's research discussion links team psychological safety with interpersonally risky learning behaviors such as asking for help, discussing error, and experimentation. The evidence concerns team context; it does not establish that a facilitator can guarantee safety through an icebreaker. [Edmondson, “Managing the Risk of Learning: Psychological Safety in Work Teams,” HBS working paper 02-062](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Publication%20Files/02-062_0b5726a8-443d-4629-9e75-736679b870fc.pdf).

**Application:** normalize questions and revision, respond constructively to uncertainty, provide ways to pass, and be explicit about how input is used. Do not call disagreement unsafe by default or pressure disclosure. Observe conditions and participation; do not diagnose people.

## F3. Structured participation
1-2-4-All is a method developed by Henri Lipmanowicz and Keith McCandless that moves from individual reflection to pairs, fours, and whole-group contribution. It is intended to make contributions possible beyond the fastest speakers. [Liberating Structures, “1-2-4-All”](https://www.liberatingstructures.com/1-2-4-all).

**Application:** use a concrete invitation and protect initial independent thinking. Adapt group formation to access and size. It structures participation; it does not guarantee equal influence or correct decisions. Attribute the method and label altered sequences.

## F4. Reflection before action
What, So What, Now What is a Liberating Structures method that separates facts, interpretations, and next actions. [Lipmanowicz and McCandless, “What, So What, Now What”](https://www.liberatingstructures.com/what-so-what-now-what).

**Application:** prevent a debrief from jumping to recommendations before participants establish what occurred. Keep dissent and uncertainty. This is a facilitation procedure; it does not prove that a stated intention will transfer into behavior.

## F5. Motivation and participation
Ryan and Deci discuss autonomy, competence, relatedness, and differing degrees of internalization of external goals. [Ryan and Deci (2000), “Intrinsic and Extrinsic Motivations”](https://www.selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_IntExtDefs.pdf).

**Application:** offer meaningful choices within real constraints, explain relevance, and provide specific feedback. Avoid promising that games automatically motivate a group or that rewards always undermine motivation.

## F6. Access and learner variability
CAST UDL 3.0 offers a framework for varied ways of engaging, perceiving, and expressing. [CAST, UDL Guidelines](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/). W3C defines technical web accessibility criteria that may apply to the workshop's digital surfaces. [W3C, WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/).

**Application:** support written, spoken, seated, and asynchronous contribution where they preserve the purpose. Check real room and technology access. Keep captions even if slides use less text. Do not mistake a facilitation inclusion plan for a complete web conformance audit.

## F7. Participation is not learning evidence
The Freeman et al. meta-analysis supports active learning in undergraduate STEM, not every interactive activity in every context. [Freeman et al. (2014), PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1319030111). Abstract metadata was accessible; full text was not accessed in this build.

**Application:** a learning workshop should elicit actual skill evidence. A decision workshop should produce a traceable decision. Count the output that matters, not just attendance, energy, or sticky notes.

## Avoid unsupported norms
Do not present universal attention spans, learning-style diagnoses, fixed brain types, “93% of communication” rules, or learning-pyramid percentages as pedagogical evidence. Do not let dot counts masquerade as consensus or brainstorm volume masquerade as validated demand.

## Research-to-practice check
For a selected method, record purpose, participation mechanism, expected artifact, access adaptation, failure signal, and evidence limit. Pilot the proposed version in the actual context. Keep source text short and attributed; do not vendor third-party manuals or the entire File-O-Fun archive.
