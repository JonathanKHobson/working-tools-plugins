# Critical source evaluation

Use for a bounded check of a supplied article, study, report or source-dependent claim before it informs a decision. A source check can be brief; do not expand it into a full literature review or demand a council.

## Check the claim, then the source

1. Identify the exact claim the decision depends on and what use the user intends. Separate factual support, interpretation and values.
2. Open the source or supplied artifact. Record origin, author/publisher, date/version, source type and access limits. Search snippets and abstracts locate evidence but do not establish the full claim.
3. Trace the claim to the closest available originating evidence. Check the relevant population, sample, method, measures, comparison, time window and context. A prestigious publisher or primary source can still make an unsupported claim.
4. Look for missing denominators, conflicts/incentives, duplicated reporting, alternative explanations and evidence that could overturn the reading. Several reports repeating one study are one evidentiary origin, not independent corroboration.
5. State what this source supports, the boundary of transfer to the user's question, and the smallest next check. Distinguish not checked, missing support, contradicted and demonstrably false.

Keep the record proportional: `Claim → source/location → supports/limits → next check`. A short source check needs only the material rows. For a requested broad investigation, return to Research Methodology's research record.

## Use the existing Compass

When a critical reasoning check helps, read [the integration contract](../../../docs/critical-compass-integration.md) and use the callable Critical Compass: `quick_scan` for supplied text or `claim_stress_test` for one claim. Use `audit_text` only when a guided full audit is warranted. Preserve current pause/consent questions and source-readiness limits; do not synthesize human answers. If no tool is exposed, perform a labeled internal source/argument check from material actually read.

KB matches, integrity scores and framework labels are reasoning aids. They are not a fact check, a source-quality probability or external corroboration. Do not add bias labels unless the passage warrants them; a missing citation is an evidence gap, not proof of deception. Report supported content and legitimate alternative readings alongside concerns.

## Optional perspectives

If a material interpretation remains contested, use [alternative perspectives](../../alternative-perspectives/SKILL.md) and the installed `work-management:worker-delegation` owner's `references/decision-council.md`. Default to one focused check. If the shared owner is unavailable, use a single-pass perspective review labeled as such; never claim an independent council.

## Deliver

Lead with a calibrated reading of what the source can carry for this decision. Give the strongest supported point, one or two material limitations and an actionable next check. Cite what was actually inspected and state access limits. Stop when the requested source-dependent claim is supported within its scope or the unresolved evidence gap is explicit.
