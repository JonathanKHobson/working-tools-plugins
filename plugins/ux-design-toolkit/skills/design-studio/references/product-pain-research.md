# Source-grounded product pain research

Use when the user asks for current UX friction, user complaints, onboarding, help/documentation, setup/account, support or developer experience for a named digital product. This original UX method owns the design-facing scan and synthesis; existing Research owns deeper methodology and evidence review. It is public/internal source research, not an observed interface audit or representative participant study.

## Scope and gather

State the exact product/version or surface, audience, decision, relevant period and exclusions. Identify whether public sources alone suffice or authorized internal evidence is available. Use current web/search tools for public material and the actual available connectors for permitted internal records. A missing connection or sparse results is an evidence limit, not proof that a problem does not exist. Do not require broad private access for a narrow question.

Choose sources by audience and task: product forums/reviews, relevant social/developer communities, issue trackers, help/docs and release notes; internal support, research, usage or team records when authorized. Search the named product plus concrete task/failure language and competing explanations. Include recent fixes/version changes so resolved old complaints are not presented as current defects. Record source URL/file, date/event period, version/context, bounded supporting evidence and public/internal access class. Keep private excerpts within their authorized destination.

Triangulate material claims across suitable sources and look for contrary evidence. Avoid treating duplicate reposts, a viral complaint or self-selecting reviews as independent frequency evidence. Distinguish observed behavior, a person's reported experience, verified product documentation and reviewer inference. Inspect primary evidence rather than relying on search summaries. Never create participant quotes or numerical prevalence from anecdote counts.

## Separate and prioritize mechanisms

Group evidence by the user's goal and the mechanism that breaks it. Distinguish UI/navigation/workflow friction, help/documentation gaps, first-use/setup, account/billing/permissions, developer/API/SDK ergonomics, reliability/performance and missing features. These may interact but imply different fixes. Example: a timeout report may be a reliability fault; a missing recovery path is a related UX fault. One louder category is not automatically the most frequent or valuable one.

For each candidate problem record affected task/surface, evidence and contradictory evidence, consequence/severity, frequency signal with its actual denominator or coverage limit, confidence, likely mechanism and feasible product move. Rank using impact, evidence, reach and implementation constraints, without pretending a qualitative score is a validated formula. State the strongest credible alternative explanation and what observation would change the recommendation.

## Deliver and verify the brief

Lead with the supported product story in concise prose. Supply a ranked problem table and a source map showing searches, contributions and weak/missing coverage. Group proposed actions by near-term repair, longer product work and questions needing deeper study when useful; use the project's real planning horizon rather than inventing deadlines. Link each recommendation to evidence and an acceptance task. Preserve private/public boundaries in the final brief and label the time window/version reviewed.

Check every consequential citation against its actual source, recheck resolved/version-specific issues and avoid causal or population claims beyond the method. Current public complaints can guide a prototype or study; they cannot prove task completion, conversion uplift or whole-product accessibility. For an actual flow audit, use [Design Review](../../design-review/SKILL.md); for real participants, use [usability testing](usability-testing.md).

Fixture: three forum threads describe trouble finding export. Two repeat the same original report; current docs show a renamed control after an update. The synthesis reports a potentially stale naming/discoverability issue with two unique sources, checks the current interface before asserting an active defect and proposes a find-export task test. It does not report three failed users or a population failure rate.
