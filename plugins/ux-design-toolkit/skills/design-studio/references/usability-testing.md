# Usability testing for a bounded design decision

Use to evaluate whether intended users can complete a defined task, including navigation, prototypes and workbook operation. This is a complete UX-owned procedure; no separate UI/UX method plugin is required. Research specialists can support recruitment, advanced analysis or sensitive studies. Tool simulation and expert walkthroughs remain different evidence from participant testing.

## Define and prepare

State the decision, audience/context, tasks, source artifact/version and success/failure conditions. Choose moderated or unmoderated work, remote/in-context setup and sample coverage for the actual risk and question. Include relevant experience, accessibility, device and language differences rather than declaring a fixed sample universally sufficient. Document recruitment bias and limits. If no participants are available, run a labeled expert task walkthrough and deliver a study-ready guide; do not fabricate participant results.

Get appropriate participant consent, explain recording/data use, minimize identifiers and preserve access boundaries. Use fictional or sanitized task data. Ensure destructive, payment or messaging actions are simulated or specifically authorized in a safe environment. Check prototype access, reset state and moderator capture before recruiting. A connected analytics tool is not participant consent.

## Write neutral task scenarios

Give a goal and realistic context without naming the intended control or leading the solution. Example: “You need a 30-minute appointment next Tuesday afternoon. Find an available time and make the reservation.” Avoid “Click the calendar and select the blue slot.” Define successful completion, time start/stop, allowed assistance and what the moderator does if the service is unavailable. Counterbalance task order when learning/order effects matter.

Cover the core task, a consequential error/recovery and a return/interruption when relevant. Include open probes such as “What did you expect to happen?” and “What makes you say that?” Avoid selling the design or interpreting silence as approval. Pilot the guide for ambiguity, task realism, capture and timing before the main sessions; revise without mixing incomparable versions silently.

## Observe and record

Record participant/session pseudonym, artifact/version, task, starting state, actions, outcome, errors, assistance and supporting timestamp/quote. Note direct observation separately from moderator interpretation. Agree on a think-aloud approach if used; it can affect task time and is not evidence of unconscious motivation. Do not coach during the measured attempt unless the study design permits it; log any intervention.

A minimal observation record:

| Session/task | Observation/evidence | Outcome | Interpretation/limit | Change to test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fictional pilot T1 | Person searches date list twice; selected date is offscreen | Completed with prompt | Visibility may obscure context; one pilot, no prevalence claim | Keep selected date next to slots |

The row is a fictional training fixture, not collected user evidence. A real report must link its actual observation.

## Analyze without overstating

Group failures by task/mechanism, preserve counterexamples, compare participant contexts and distinguish artifact faults from access/service faults. Count outcomes with denominators and assisted/unassisted definitions. Small purposive studies reveal problems and possible explanations; they do not establish population prevalence or causal conversion uplift. Quotes support what the speaker said, not everyone else's belief.

Prioritize findings by observed impact, reach/coverage, recoverability and consequence. For each finding, state source evidence, explanation, strongest alternative, confidence/limit, recommended change and acceptance task. A misleading saved-state indicator may be consequential even if infrequent. Do not average away a failure that blocks a relevant access need. Formal rubric scoring belongs to the complete UX Heuristic Compass method rather than being invented from this study.

## Iterate and close

Apply only authorized changes. Recheck affected tasks and rendered anchors with the same artifact/viewport where possible; rerun participant tasks when needed to assess usability rather than mere implementation. Keep version changes and repeated-participant learning visible. Deliver the guide, evidence-linked findings, prioritized changes, checks and unresolved questions in the owning project.

Completion: source access works; tasks are neutral and evaluable; consent/data handling are defined; observations and interpretations are separable; conclusions fit coverage; changes have an acceptance task; requested artifact/review is delivered. If only prepared, say study prepared. If expert-tested, say expert walkthrough. If participants completed sessions, report actual sessions and limits. Sending invitations or sharing recordings is a separate authorized action.
