# Embedded and conversational widgets

Read for designing, implementing or reviewing a bounded interactive widget embedded in a product, chat or side panel. The task, action, disclosure, access and evaluation guidance applies broadly; the conversation-continuity section applies when a widget participates in an assistant conversation. This is original Design Studio synthesis from the 2026-10-06 Conversational MCP Widget Design Research package and the primary sources linked below, not an MCP protocol specification or a claim of host support. Build MCP owns the protocol; confirm the current provider's capabilities before promising an action. Use [compact library chooser](mcp-app-widgets.md) only when a person is browsing or selecting from a collection inside chat.

## Choose the job before the component

Name the user's immediate task, the object and source of truth, the host surface, and what direct interaction adds to a plain answer. A concise result may need no widget. Choose a recognizable form for the work: labeled rows for disambiguation, aligned attributes for comparison, direct controls for repeated adjustment, a draft or difference view for review, and a scoped action view for commitment. Use a larger workspace when spatial inspection, dense editing or simultaneous comparison needs it. A carousel is useful for recognizable previews but can hide the attributes needed for a precise comparison. These are candidate patterns to test, not universal component rules.

The first view should make the subject, relevant scope or freshness, current state and next meaningful action understandable. Identify whether a card is informational, navigational, selectable or editable; do not make a complex card one giant click target when it also contains links, selection, text or fields. Keep decision-critical facts visible, including recipients, cost, material uncertainty, conflicts, and unsaved work when they affect the next action.

Use progressive disclosure for secondary evidence, history and uncommon controls, with labels that say what opens. It should not force repeated panel opening to compare essential facts. Preserve a clear path back and the person's selection. [GOV.UK Details](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/details/) cautions against hiding information most users need; [Nielsen's disclosure guidance](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/) distinguishes optional detail from a required step in a sequence.

Keep the common inline footprint bounded and predictable through loading, empty, filtered and error states. Reserve space for transient feedback rather than appending whole panels below the widget. Check popovers against the actual iframe bounds; viewport units refer to that frame and absolute overflow may escape size measurements. Preserve accessible reflow for enlarged text rather than enforcing an invariant pixel height. Longer tasks use a supported larger view or an honest fallback.

Show source-backed fields, previews, counts and statuses. Use a text preview when no real image exists; label genuinely illustrative material as such. Do not invent usage badges, filters or apparent actions to make a widget look complete. A supported assistant-request route may be offered with an accurate label, even when direct authoring is unavailable.

## Make state clear; coordinate with conversation when present

For the task at hand, define initial, editing, pending, confirmed, partial or failed, stale and resumed states where relevant. Distinguish local selection or draft from an applied change. A click, accepted request or timeout is not proof of a completed external action; use the authoritative result and give a way to check an unknown outcome before repeating a consequential write. Keep useful input through recoverable errors. Define whether edits use explicit save, autosave or a commit step; make that boundary visible.

A past card may be a snapshot, live view, recoverable draft or superseded result. Label this when old content could mislead. If an action depends on current external state, recheck identity and revision before commitment and surface conflicts. In a conversational host, preserve current selection, query, relevant filters and draft status through supported context mechanisms; do not assume the assistant sees every pixel or that remount recovery is guaranteed. Share only context needed for the next turn, with permissions and private-content boundaries respected.

In chat, local filtering, opening details and other view changes usually stay in the widget. A deliberate request for interpretation, a meaningful selection that advances the task, a committed outcome or a failure requiring help may warrant a chat turn. Do not fill conversation history with narration of every control change. Use accurate verbs and receipts: prepared, copied, sent, accepted, saved and confirmed describe different states.

## Motion, access and feedback

Add motion only when it clarifies a meaningful change or relationship; keep the final state and information available without the motion. Respect reduced-motion preferences for nonessential spatial movement, avoid shifting a reachable target or replaying an entrance when an old message remounts, and do not invent percent progress. The relevant [WCAG animation criterion is AAA](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html); automatically moving or updating content also needs its own [pause, stop, hide assessment](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#pause-stop-hide).

Check the entire keyboard path from conversation into the widget and back, visible focus, zoom/reflow, touch or single-pointer alternatives when dragging is involved, contrast, and meaningful status announcements. Use native controls where they meet the interaction need. For custom disclosure, follow the [WAI disclosure pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/disclosure/) for keyboard and expanded state; use modal semantics only when focus and host behavior are actually modal. Coordinate widget status with the assistant so both do not announce the same routine update. Apply [WCAG 2.2 criteria](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) at their stated levels and exceptions; a component check does not establish conformance of a whole host process.

Give errors at the level where they occur: a field, one item, or the whole dependency. Tell the user what remains preserved and what retry would repeat. The invoking control should show pending and the confirmed result or failure; a distant status paragraph is insufficient for a consequential action.

## Evaluate the actual task

Use representative first-use, correction, interruption, stale-card, failure and return scenarios. Test the real host at a narrow viewport and with keyboard, touch and the assistive technology relevant to the audience. Check whether people can identify the current selection, destination, applied status and uncertainty, then complete or recover from the task. Record errors, duplicate attempts, lost input, backtracking and extra chat turns alongside time and preference. Compare with a credible text-only or conventional path when claiming the widget adds value.

Set any numerical release target from the product's risk and baseline before evaluation. A screenshot, component checklist, local harness or visual rating cannot establish task success or real-host accessibility. Report the observed state and host limitations separately from inference. [DynaVis](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/dynavis-dynamically-synthesized-ui-widgets-for-visualization-editing/) is a useful hybrid-control analogue for repeated chart edits (24 participants), not proof that widgets outperform chat for other tasks.

## Elicit preferences without making another form

When the desired view is unclear, a small, optional choice can help a person discover and express what matters: for example which comparison attributes to emphasize. Let them skip, revise and return to the original view. Do not infer durable preferences from a single click or promise retention without supported storage. Treat this as a design hypothesis to test, not a requirement to interrupt every task. The September 2026 preprint [Elicitive User Interfaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23642) reports two design-probe studies and differences across users; it does not establish universal superiority of direct manipulation or a standard widget layout.

Avoid redundant narration, but retain a meaningful textual result or fallback for unavailable widgets and for decision-critical outcomes. A “silent widget” rule must not suppress errors, uncertainty or action receipts. A local read-only receipt also does not prevent a repeated server action; the service owns durable confirmation and deduplication.

Candidate counts, dimensions, motion durations and weighted review scores are product choices, not universal research thresholds. Do not average a consequential action or accessibility defect away with visual polish. For target sizing, WCAG 2.2 [2.5.8 (AA)](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html) uses 24 CSS pixels with specified exceptions; 2.5.5 (AAA) uses 44, without a mobile/desktop split. Prefer native keyboard semantics; blanket tabindex=0, iframe focus traps and live announcements on every change are not accessibility fixes.

## Default widget themes

Kyle’s default for owned MCP widget dark-mode chrome is neutral black, white and gray unless he explicitly requests another design. Do not inherit green/teal branding into dark controls, headings or charts by default. Preserve semantic colors inside user-authored artwork or source content. Pair foreground and background tokens for page, surface, text, muted text, input, button, selected, focus and error states; do not rely on host background alone. Explicit host light/dark theme wins; system preference is the fallback when theme is absent. Verify both initial theme and live host changes, including disabled controls, placeholders, SVG labels and nested editors. Errors and selections must remain recognizable through text, icons or shape without color alone.
