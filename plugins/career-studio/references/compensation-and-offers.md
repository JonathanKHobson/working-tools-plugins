# Compensation, offer comparison and negotiation

Load only for salary expectations, offer evaluation, counteroffer preparation or a compensation decision. Read the [worksheet and worked example](compensation-worksheet.md) and [provenance](compensation-provenance.md) with this method. Career Interviews owns preparation/comparison; Career Outreach owns usable correspondence. This reference adds no account access or automatic outreach. Keep offer letters, personal constraints and drafts in the existing private career workspace.

## 1. Establish the decision and present state

Infer known facts from the request and supplied evidence. Ask only for missing facts that could change the recommendation: role and actual responsibilities/level, geography and currency, employee or contractor status, current interview/offer stage, written terms and decision deadline, candidate priorities and viable alternatives. Record whether an amount is advertised, discussed verbally, offered in writing, revised in writing or accepted. A recruiter conversation is not a written offer.

Agree a concrete output: expectations answer, normalized comparison, one justified counteroffer draft, or accept/decline recommendation. Record top two or three priorities and any hard constraint (cash floor, schedule, travel, start date). A personal minimum is a private decision constraint, not evidence of the employer's market band. Do not require disclosure of past salary. Do not fabricate another offer, recruiter budget, competing process, deadline, NDA or interview feedback.

Separate three numbers privately: minimum acceptable package under the user's constraints; supported target; and proposed ask. They can coincide, but do not invent precision when evidence is weak. Record the actual fallback: present employment, continued search, another verified offer or another specific option. Having one offer is sufficient to prepare a grounded request; leverage need not be manufactured.

## 2. Build a dated, comparable market evidence set

Start with the employer's current job posting and communicated band. Verify role scope and leveling rather than equating titles across employers. Match benchmarks on function, responsibilities/seniority, geography/remote policy, employment type, company context and compensation definition. Include relevant current employer postings or official wage statistics, then transparently labeled self-reported surveys/databases if useful. The [BLS wage overview](https://www.bls.gov/bls/blswage.htm) routes U.S. occupation/area data; broad occupation data is context, not an exact UX or teaching offer band.

Keep each source's URL/document identifier, retrieval date, data period, currency, population, level/region match, base versus total-compensation definition, range/percentiles and limitations. Never reuse the upstream PM salary tables or company figures as today's benchmark. For live decisions, refresh time-sensitive claims from original sources. Public searches use generic role/location terms, not private salary letters or personal household information.

Compare like with like. Report a defensible range and source mismatch, not a universal 'market value.' Do not average an hourly contract posting, annual employee base and equity-heavy total compensation. If data is sparse, state what is known and use a budget/level clarification rather than an unsupported assertive counter. A large advertised range may span multiple levels or locations; ask which band applies.

## 3. Gather role and package intelligence

During interviews, ask about expected first-year outcomes, why the role is open, team priorities, manager/support, role scope, promotion/review process and location expectations. Ask which compensation components are flexible and who handles decisions. Use authentic contribution evidence relevant to those responsibilities, not promises of invented impact.

Possible early question: 'Could you share the budgeted base range and the level for this role? That will help me assess fit as we learn more about the responsibilities.'

If an expectation is required, give a researched conditional answer: 'For the scope discussed, I'm targeting [supported range] in base salary, with the full package and working arrangement also relevant.' Use a point or range that fits the form and user's strategy. Asking for the employer's band is an option, not a rule to refuse every number.

An unexpectedly low offer does not establish poor interview feedback or a hidden next-level band. Ask about the confirmed level, applicable range and constraints directly. Request another discussion with the manager only when it resolves a real question; do not assume access to executives or investors.

## 4. Normalize the complete package

Use written offer and benefit documents; mark missing fields rather than treating them as zero. Separate:

- Recurring guaranteed cash: base, contractual allowances, guaranteed recurring payments and their conditions.
- Variable cash: target bonus/commission, eligibility, ramp, quota, payout history if disclosed, discretionary terms and dates. A target is not a guarantee.
- First-year-only cash: sign-on/relocation payments, timing, eligibility and repayment/clawback provisions. Show first year and later years separately.
- Equity: instrument, shares/units, grant versus annual amount, vesting/cliff, strike or exercise price where applicable, exercise window, dilution, liquidity restrictions and valuation source/date. Annualize only according to the actual vesting schedule; do not divide an uneven grant blindly by four.
- Benefits/costs: premiums, eligibility/wait periods, retirement match and vesting, PTO, leave, learning support, equipment, commute/relocation and actual work hours/travel. Verify comparability before assigning a dollar value.
- Work/career: manager quality, learning/teaching opportunity, job scope/title, support, schedule, flexibility, stability and portfolio permissions. Record evidence separately from impressions.

Calculate cash scenarios transparently: recurring guaranteed annual cash; first-year guaranteed cash with conditional sign-on separated; target cash if bonus is achieved. State units, period, currency and assumptions. Gross cash divided by 12 is not take-home pay. Use actual tax/benefit inputs and current authoritative sources if take-home estimates are requested; tax, legal and investment judgments need appropriately qualified advice.

For equity, distinguish current public-market scenario from private, illiquid or uncertain value. Private preferred-share valuation is not necessarily employee common-share value. Do not add speculative equity to cash needed for bills. If exploring private option outcomes, show transparent hypothetical price, dilution, vested shares and exercise cost; exit proceeds depend on rights, preferences, tax and liquidity. Leave unknowns unresolved and route specialist questions appropriately.

For contractors, compare hours, billable utilization, unpaid time, self-funded benefits, overhead, payment terms and scope. A quoted hourly fee multiplied by 2,080 is not automatically equivalent to employee salary. Do not invent legal classification.

## 5. Decide tradeoffs before composing the ask

Apply hard constraints first. Then compare the remaining options against the user's few priorities with evidence and unknowns visible. A simple weighted score can structure reflection if helpful; weights are the user's values and scores are assumptions, not scientific certainty. Check whether changing an uncertain score or removing speculative equity changes the preferred option.

For each promising offer identify: strengths, decisive drawbacks, missing terms, and the smallest improvement that would change the decision. Ask first for the component that matters most: often recurring base, sometimes schedule, scope, location or another term. There is no universal 'total comp first, equity next, signing bonus last' sequence. If base is fixed, identify a meaningful alternative (sign-on, review date with documented criteria, PTO or working arrangement) and its limits. A future review does not guarantee a raise.

## 6. Prepare and exercise one collaborative counteroffer

Build a concise case: authentic interest; confirmed role/offer; one prioritized request with exact amount/term; relevant verified scope or comparable market evidence; optional secondary alternative; and a practical next action. Avoid a long list of demands, bluffing, manufactured obstacles, aggression or promises to accept unless the user is actually ready.

Choose a channel that fits accessibility, recruiter practice, complexity and the user's preference. Email is valid for clarity and a written record; a call can help discussion. For a call, provide a speakable opener, likely questions and a written follow-up. Do not guarantee success or imply that any negotiation is risk-free.

Practice responses to: 'base is fixed', 'what would close this?', 'do you have another offer?', 'we need a decision today', and 'can you share the other letter?' Answer truthfully; disclose only information the user has authorized and can share. If the deadline is restrictive, request a specific extension with a real reason rather than inventing pressure elsewhere.

Example response to a cap: 'Thanks for clarifying. [priority] is the main gap for me. If base is fixed, could we explore [specific alternative]? I'd also like to confirm [material unresolved term] before deciding.'

Deliver the exact draft and private decision rationale. Preparation does not authorize sending. If the user already instructed sending the specific counteroffer, finish the actual provider's required review and send without another approval loop. If they asked for a draft or human review, preserve that hold. Never silently accept, sign, withdraw or decline an offer under an analysis request.

## 7. Verify the revised terms and close

Record recruiter reply separately from revised written offer. Check requested changes and dependent terms in the final documents: amount, currency, level, location, dates, vesting, bonus conditions, clawback, contingencies and expiry. Ask for discrepancies to be corrected. Verbal agreement is pending written confirmation. Track sent/received/revised/accepted states and exact evidence. Acceptance or decline requires the user's actual instruction; capture the provider receipt/readback if performed.

Completion means the requested decision or communication is usable, all material figures trace to dated sources/terms, the cash calculations are reproducible, uncertainties remain visible and the actual requested action state is verified. Do not prolong research after additional sources stop changing the decision.

## Behavioral checks

- One offer with no competitor: produce a grounded ask without fabricated leverage.
- Old PM salary table supplied: label its date/population, refresh comparable evidence, do not present it as current.
- Private equity pitch: cash view remains separate and unknown valuation/rights remain unknown.
- Fixed salary band: explore only relevant levers and document what is actually flexible.
- Draft-only request: finish the draft without transmission; send-authorized request: use the provider review and verify sent state.
- Uneven vesting or clawback: calculate period-specific scenarios and expose repayment risk before comparing totals.
