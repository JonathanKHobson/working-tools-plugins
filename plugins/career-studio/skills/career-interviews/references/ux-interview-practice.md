# UX interview and design-challenge practice

Use the actual role, interview stage and candidate evidence. This Career Studio procedure supports UX, research and product-design interviews without a separate method plugin. Its practice scenarios are hypothetical; they are not user research or proof of job performance.

## Select and prepare the evidence

1. Read the posting and stage instructions. Identify the evaluated work: research judgment, interaction design, visual craft, collaboration, systems, delivery or leadership. Do not assume every UX interview rewards the same portfolio.
2. Select a real project that supplies evidence for the main criteria. Record the candidate's role, team context, constraints, decision, action, artifact and observed result. Distinguish personal contribution from team output; label an unavailable metric rather than inventing one.
3. Build a speakable walkthrough: the user/task problem, how it was investigated, consequential findings, options considered, the chosen design or research decision, tradeoffs, implementation/verification and what remains uncertain. Use [portfolio](../../portfolio/SKILL.md) for a case narrative and [truth rules](../../resume/references/metrics-and-truth.md) for quantitative claims.
4. Practice a shorter version as well as the full explanation. The interviewer may interrupt. Be able to point to the relevant artifact and answer why the method or decision fit the constraint. A polished slide does not substitute for source evidence.

## Hypothetical design challenge

Confirm the prompt, expected format and time available. Ask only the questions that change the next decision: target user and task, setting, primary objective, constraints and known evidence. If answers are unavailable, state a small set of assumptions and carry them visibly through the solution.

Map the shortest useful flow, then the material empty, loading, error and recovery states. Sketch the primary path and explain the information hierarchy, action feedback, access needs and tradeoff. Compare one plausible alternative when it tests the consequential assumption. Define what could falsify the direction and the smallest realistic evaluation that could resolve it. Do not invent participant quotations, conversion gains or a tested accessibility result.

For a research challenge, connect the decision to the study question, method, recruitment, consent, tasks/questions, evidence analysis and limitation. Explain why a survey, interview, usability session or existing-log review can answer that question; sample size alone does not validate the design. Research Toolkit owns a requested full study plan.

## Practice and feedback

Use one question at a time when interactive practice is requested. After the answer, cite the actual phrase that worked or needs repair. Check whether it answers the question, identifies contribution, supports the outcome and names a tradeoff or learning without fabricated self-criticism. Give one targeted revision or another attempt rather than rewriting the candidate into an invented seniority.

Include likely follow-ups about disagreement, an unsuccessful approach, evidence that changed a decision, handoff to engineering, priorities under constraints and how impact was verified. Keep each example tied to an authentic project. For questions to ask the employer, use unknowns about the real role, decision authority, research access, collaboration and success criteria.

Return the requested walkthrough, practice prompts, feedback or challenge artifact. State which parts are verified experience and which are proposed reasoning. Preparation does not contact the employer, submit an artifact or create a performance result.
