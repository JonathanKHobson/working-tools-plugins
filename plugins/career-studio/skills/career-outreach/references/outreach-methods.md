# Career outreach methods

Use the first-party section matching the actual request and the [native task map](../../../library/source-procedures/INDEX.md). Historical source adaptation and exclusions remain recorded in provenance; the operative procedures below need no separately installed letter-writing plugin. The live task, verified facts and current delivery authorization govern output.

## Follow-ups and thank-you notes

Choose the actual stage before drafting. An application follow-up is generally timed 5 to 10 working days after applying; an interview thank-you is prompt, usually within 24 hours, and mentions one real detail from the conversation; an interview status check follows the date the employer gave. Withdrawal, offer acceptance, and polite decline each need their own clear purpose. These are timing guides, not evidence that a message was sent or received.

Write a short subject line and a warm, professional message of roughly 60 to 150 words with one clear purpose. Return a ready-to-send message with subject line and a shorter variant. Use only verified recipient, role, stage, date and conversation details. A draft stays a draft until the user authorizes and the provider confirms sending.

## Referral and networking letters

For a referral cover letter, name the referrer in the first line only with their permission, explain the connection, then give relevant proof and fit. For a referral request, include the context, specific role and link, why the candidate fits, an easy ask, and thanks; attach or offer the resume when appropriate. For recruiter or hiring-manager outreach, keep a professional-network message under 100 words: who the candidate is, the role, one proof point, and a small request. Return the message plus a possible follow-up for 5 to 7 days later when follow-up is appropriate.

For a letter of interest where there is no advertised opening, use this order: a verified specific detail about the company; the role or team of interest; two supported proof points; and a low-pressure request for a brief conversation. Keep it under 300 words and easy to answer.

Do not invent jobs, employers, dates, degrees, certifications, titles, awards, numbers, relationships or company facts. Mark a necessary unknown plainly, such as `[COMPANY DETAIL]`, and say what the user must fill in. Do not recommend spam, fake connections, or private channels. A suggested follow-up is not a sent one.

## Freelance proposals

Aim for 100 to 250 words when the destination allows. Open with the client's exact problem and a sign that the post was read. Give relevant proof from a similar project and its observed result, adding a sample link only when the user has supplied one. Explain the proposed approach in two to four concrete steps. State a timeline and availability only when supported, or mark the missing fact for confirmation. Close with one useful scope question and a clear, low-pressure call to action.

Never invent jobs, employers, dates, degrees, certifications, titles, awards, numbers, results or client facts. Use a clear placeholder such as `[START DATE]` for a necessary unknown and identify what the user must confirm. Do not state prices unless supplied. Respect the platform's rules and do not suggest moving the conversation off-platform to avoid fees. Return the proposal in a form the user can copy; a proposal draft is not a submission.
