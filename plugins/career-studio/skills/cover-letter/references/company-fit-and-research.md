# Company Fit And Research

Company fit is powerful when it is specific and verified. It is risky when it is generic, copied from an old letter, or invented.

## Company-Fit Rules

- Use company research only when the user supplies it or the host has verified it within the task.
- Do not invent mission, product, culture, values, team priorities, or hiring-manager needs.
- Do not reuse target-company praise from another application.
- If a material company fact is needed but missing, flag it. If a role-specific bridge is sufficient, use the job description without a company-praise placeholder.
- Use the job description's role problem when company research is unavailable.

## Safe Company Signals

- A specific product, service, audience, or market.
- A stated role responsibility.
- A team priority from the job description.
- A verified mission or program.
- A real referral or conversation.
- A public article, page, or source the user provides.

## Unsafe Company Signals

- "I have always admired your company."
- "Your culture is a perfect fit."
- "Your mission deeply resonates with me."
- "I know your team needs..."
- Praise copied from an unrelated letter.

## Company-Fit Audit

For role tailoring, separate required qualifications from preferences, then map each priority requirement to supplied candidate proof. Distinguish direct evidence, relevant transferable evidence and an unsupported gap. Choose the strongest one or two matches for the letter; do not add an unsupported skill just because the posting repeats its keyword. If requested, report keyword coverage with the matched evidence and missing requirements. Do not call a home-made match percentage a real ATS score or predict hiring success from it. Keep each employer's evidence and wording separate when tailoring several letters.

## Requested keyword coverage diagnostic

Use this only when a user asks for a numerical keyword/requirement check. It is a local comparison of supplied text, not access to an employer's ATS. First list the actual posting terms, distinguish requirements from preferences, and group repeated spelling/wording variants of the same requirement so repetition cannot inflate the denominator. Keep tools, credentials and responsibilities separate when they represent distinct criteria.

For each criterion record the posting span, candidate evidence, letter/resume span and status: represented accurately, supported but absent from this artifact, adjacent/partial, unsupported or unknown. An exact word without experience does not count as an accurate match. Suggest an addition only when the candidate's evidence supports it; identify a gap instead of stuffing keywords.

If a percentage is requested, state the counting rule before computing it. A simple default reports represented criteria over assessable criteria separately for required and preferred sets. Keep partial and unknown entries visible instead of silently awarding full credit; disclose excluded unknowns and the resulting coverage. Use user-supplied weights only when their meaning is clear, and disclose the weighted numerator/denominator. Do not use an arbitrary weighted figure as a hiring probability, proprietary parser result or validated fit score. Return the evidence table and most useful truthful additions alongside any local estimate.

## Safe Substitute Pattern

When company research is absent:

```text
What stands out in the role description is the need for [role problem]. My experience with [supported evidence] maps directly to that need.
```
