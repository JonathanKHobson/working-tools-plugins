# Search through confirmed application

Use when the user authorizes sourcing and applying to a defined set or batch of jobs. For one supplied role, skip sourcing. Preparation, submission and outreach are separate permissions; carry forward current authorization without asking repeatedly.

## Select eligible work first

Read the user's current private preferences and evidence before searching: relevant role families, geography, remote jurisdiction, office attendance, travel, relocation, pay and other actual constraints. Apply hard eligibility before ranking or drafting. “Remote” does not establish residency eligibility; a broad national label may still have exclusions. Unclear eligibility is a hold for a bounded employer-source check, not permission to guess or promise relocation.

Search suitable job boards, recruiters and employer sites using task-relevant synonyms. Reopen the official employer listing to verify exact role, requisition, current availability and requirements. Match work substance to real experience; a keyword such as AI must not turn an applied-workflow candidate into a machine-learning engineer. Distinguish required from preferred qualifications. Record the source and observation date; do not invent salary, applicant count or fit probabilities.

Use any suitable job site within the authorized search scope; existing site records are a working directory, not a whitelist. Aggregators may originate a lead; prefer the employer's authoritative listing for identity, availability and eligibility checks. Use available free search/application features; a paid subscription, new agreement or account decision needs its own applicable authorization. Maintain the existing private site directory with site/domain, actual discovery/application use, last observation, account/access blockers, supported fix tried and verified result. Omit passwords, codes, tokens and private auth URLs. A documented fix is a scoped observation, not permission to bypass access restrictions or blindly replay it elsewhere. Deduplicate the same employer/requisition across sites before claiming a role; site URLs do not create separate applications.

Deduplicate against the private ledger, acknowledgment messages and known recruiter submissions by employer plus requisition/canonical role identity. Do not bypass a recruiter already representing the candidate for that role. Distinct roles at one employer remain distinct. Conflicting evidence triggers reconciliation, not a fresh submission.

## Keep the batch moving truthfully

Prepare role-specific files and scoped answers through the existing campaign workflow, then hand form execution to `submissions`. Reuse verified answers when the question and scope match. Make ordinary wording, sequencing and formatting choices independently. A missing material fact requires a bounded source check or focused question; never infer qualifications, insurance, legal eligibility, sensitive disclosures or commitments.

Hold only the affected role when a required fact, verification, account access or eligibility remains unresolved. Continue other authorized applications. Record why, the exact next action and who can resolve it. Do not pad a requested count with weak matches, drafts, outreach or unconfirmed attempts. Report a genuine shortfall with reasons.

For browser challenges, use the supported user flow; never bypass CAPTCHA or defeat access restrictions. On ambiguous submission, verify employer/account/receipt state before retrying. On repeated unchanged failures, preserve the packet and continue elsewhere rather than burning the batch on retries.

## Coordinate and reconcile

Multiple authorized operators use one private campaign ledger and dashboard. For the user's scheduled career workflow, the cap is **three application attempts across Codex and Claude together per scheduled run ID**, not three per operator or per wakeup. Use the same ID on restart; missed runs do not create catch-up capacity. Other campaigns and explicit user overrides retain their separately recorded authorization and limits. A final submit/send attempt consumes a slot even if its result is uncertain; confirmed submissions and attempts are separate totals. Do not reset the cap on retries or count a missing receipt as unused capacity.

Before browser action, claim the exact employer plus requisition/canonical role identity and reserve capacity in the existing shared records. Include owner, run ID, scheduled date/time, claimed time, intended action, and state. Claims must be disjoint; preparation workers may research different roles, but one recorded browser owner executes forms at a time. Re-read claims, prior attempts and receipts and recheck live eligibility immediately before the irreversible action, and record the attempt before clicking Submit or sending. Use the project's atomic claim/lock mechanism when available. If safe shared claiming is unavailable, serialize through one coordinator; do not let two operators independently read an empty ledger and proceed. One coordinator writes canonical campaign ledgers; workers return role-scoped deltas and evidence for merging rather than overwriting whole files.

An abandoned claim is not proof that no submission occurred. Reconcile the old owner's attempt/page/receipt evidence before releasing it. A stale browser claim requires verified handoff or closure before another operator takes control. Attempted and uncertain slots never expire by timeout; uncertain attempts consume the run's cap. Inspect status/receipts before any retry, and do not retry an unresolved possible submission. Release an unused reservation only with evidence that no submit/send attempt happened. Record role IDs, source evidence, final material paths, blockers and receipt references in handoffs. Preserve stronger evidence when importing another operator's report; normalize aliases and prevent duplicate totals. A reported success is provisional until the actual receipt is available.

Keep follow-up and networking within the user's separate authorization. Read the latest conversation and application state, avoid duplicate thank-yous and premature repeated follow-ups, and do not treat rejected roles as active. Scheduling is not sending; sending is not receiving. For social proof posts use Social Content Studio, with approved portfolio claims and source links supplied by Career Studio.

Close with unique confirmed applications, email-sent outcomes, held/excluded roles, material blockers and exact receipt links in the private workspace. Refresh the existing dashboard from source records. Reuse the current web dashboard rather than creating a competing tracker or making a spreadsheet the primary view without request. Any rendering claim requires actual rendered QA.

## Reuse reviewed work and learn within the run

Use the shared private answers record described in [campaign records](campaign-records.md), rather than asking the same unchanged question again or treating previous portal answers as universal. Reuse reviewed resume sections, letter structures and evidence snippets with their review/version and source IDs. Tailor every resulting packet to the live employer, requisition, requirements and supported evidence; a template is an editable starting point, not a generic application or license to manufacture claims. Keep packet variants and answers in the private project, not this plugin.

If an A/B comparison is requested, keep one honest hypothesis at a time. For example: among comparable eligible UX roles, a resume opening with a supported project/result may yield more employer responses than an opening with a supported capability summary. Record the two reviewed variants before use, one changed feature, assignment method, role family, denominator (unique applications actually transmitted per variant), response definition and equal observation window. Track confirmed and uncertain transmissions separately, and report denominators including exclusions/unknowns. A tiny, nonrandom batch with different employers, fit or elapsed time cannot establish causation or a winning template. No responses yet means pending evidence, not failure. Do not change candidate facts, eligibility rules or user preferences to improve a variant's apparent result.

End each scheduled run with a bounded audit in the existing run record: claims/cap agreement; unique attempts and receipts; exact packet/answer versions; blocks and unresolved uncertainty; and at most one actionable learning supported by observed evidence. Do not run a new broad optimization project after the batch. When the user authorizes skill learning updates, promote only the smallest validated recurring correction to the existing canonical source skill/reference, with failure, corrected behavior, proof and scope. Keep one-off employer facts and candidate answers in project records. Validate changed guidance and refresh through the supported plugin manager before claiming it is installed. A single outcome or speculative A/B result does not authorize automatically changing application policy, approval scope, preferences or templates for future runs.
