# Evidence To Case

Use this file when turning raw project material into a defensible portfolio case.

## Evidence Inventory

Before writing, gather:

- project name or neutral label
- target reader
- problem
- candidate role
- collaborators or team context
- timeline
- constraints
- methods actually used
- artifacts created or influenced
- decisions influenced
- outcomes or learning
- metrics and source status
- confidentiality limits
- missing facts

## Claim Support Levels

Use these labels internally while drafting:

- `supported`: supplied evidence directly supports the claim.
- `needs_review`: evidence may support the claim, but a detail needs confirmation.
- `missing`: the claim cannot be made yet.
- `sensitive`: the claim may be true but needs redaction or permission.

## Supported Claim Pattern

```text
I used [method/artifact] to address [problem], which helped [decision/outcome].
```

## Needs Review Pattern

```text
I used [method/artifact] to address [problem]. [MISSING INFO: confirm decision or outcome before claiming impact.]
```

## Missing Evidence Pattern

```text
[MISSING INFO: evidence needed for claimed outcome]
```

## Do Not Smooth Gaps

Do not replace missing proof with confident filler such as:

- "transformed the experience"
- "drove measurable impact"
- "improved engagement"
- "streamlined the workflow"
- "delivered strategic value"

Use the specific supplied fact or leave the gap visible.

## Ownership Check

Ask:

- Did the candidate personally do this?
- Was this a team decision?
- Was this a recommendation rather than an implemented change?
- Was this a prototype, concept, shipped product, research finding, or internal artifact?
- Is the reader allowed to see this?

If ownership is ambiguous, write conservatively:

```text
Contributed to [work] by [specific action].
```

## Case Selection

Choose a case when it has:

- a clear problem
- a defensible candidate contribution
- at least one artifact
- a decision, result, or learning
- a confidentiality path

Defer cases that depend mostly on unsupported metrics, hidden work, or vague memory.

## Reviewer Scan And Visual Sequence

Start from the hiring decision: what must this recruiter, hiring manager, or buyer
understand to continue? A fast scan should reveal the role, relevant problem,
candidate contribution, work stage, and one concrete piece of proof. Then sequence
the case as context/risk → decision → real artifact → supported result → next check.
Use the artifact to explain the decision; do not add process sections merely to
make the case longer. Compare job requirements by their substance, not title alone.

Keep essential findings in selectable native text, tables, or diagrams. Pair them
with enough readable, authentic screenshots or exports to show the actual work.
A native summary improves access; it does not replace all source evidence. Put
optional method detail and alternatives behind clearly named disclosures on the
web, or in a deck appendix. Preserve the main story without opening them.

## PAR / RAP Without Outcome Inflation

- **PAR:** Problem → Action → Result suits a case's explanatory body.
- **RAP:** Result → Action → Problem suits a scan-first hook only when the result
  is directly supported. Otherwise lead with the specific problem or deliverable.
- A result may be a documented decision, reviewable artifact, learning, or measured
  change. Name which it is. Producing a recommendation is not evidence of adoption,
  shipping, user benefit, saved money, or team efficiency.
- Distinguish **before**, **proposed after**, **prototype**, **handed off**, and
  **shipped**. A polished reconstruction does not advance the historical stage.
  Credit collaborators and later refinements without claiming their work.
- Preserve metric dates, population, denominator, and collection limits. Counts of
  checklist rows, enrollments, test fixtures, or tools are not counts of issues,
  completions, customer incidents, or demonstrated business outcomes.

## Reusable Job-Specific Evidence Packet

Reuse reviewed evidence rather than rewriting career facts for each application.
Keep one compact packet: target requirements and dated job URL; relevant evidence
IDs; supported claim and ownership/stage; chosen artifact and caption; site case,
resume bullet, and deck-slide reuse; gaps or adjacent evidence. Adapt selection,
order, and vocabulary to the role. Do not change titles, dates, methods, or outcomes
to manufacture a match. Keep private source locators in the working evidence map,
not public pages, speaker notes, or resume metadata.
