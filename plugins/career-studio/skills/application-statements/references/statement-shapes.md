# Program and personal statement shapes

Load only for admissions, scholarship, exchange, volunteer, or program applications. Follow the actual prompt and word limit over any default outline. Check the program's current AI-use and authorship rules before substantive drafting or rewriting.

For a personal statement, choose one real episode or sustained experience that reveals a decision, change, or insight. Connect its specific actions to the applicant's current direction. Avoid claiming hardship, dialogue, or a turning point the applicant did not provide. An ordinary concrete moment is stronger than a fabricated dramatic opening.

For a statement of purpose, explain preparation, the question or field of interest, relevant work or study, the next goals, and why this program's verified features matter. For a scholarship essay, connect the stated criterion to real action and likely future use of the opportunity without claiming guaranteed impact. For a motivation letter, show the core motivation, preparation, program or organization fit, goals, and contribution. Fit details must be verified and specific; generic praise adds little.

For a short answer, write the answer first, then one compact example or reason. Cover every part of a multipart prompt. Cut repeated scene-setting before cutting evidence or important caveats. Expand with supplied detail, not padding. Compare the final version to source notes for invented facts, shifted chronology, changed roles, and unsupported certainty. If a prompt requires information the applicant has not supplied, keep the gap visible in the private draft and ask one focused question; never submit a placeholder.
