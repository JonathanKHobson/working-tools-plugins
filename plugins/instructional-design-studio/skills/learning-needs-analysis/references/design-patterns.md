# Diagnosis and design patterns

## Task analysis
Ask an expert to walk through a recent concrete example. Capture trigger, goal, inputs, decisions, cues, actions, checks, failure modes, and recovery. Separate the official procedure from a workaround. Compare the expert account with novice work where available.

A learner profile should answer design questions: what can the learner already do, where will they practice, what support is present, and what could prevent access? Avoid invented demographic personas. A sample from one team is a sample from one team.

## Sponsor agreement and dependencies
Distinguish the organizational result, workplace behavior, learner capability, and project deliverable. For substantial work, identify who can accept the deliverable, validate content, change operating conditions, and approve the proposed evaluation. Record actual capacity and unresolved assumptions. Use an existing brief; a narrow lesson edit does not need a new project agreement.

For a dependency such as an approved procedure, tool access, protected practice time, or supervisory support, record: required condition → proposed/accepted owner → due point → readiness evidence → response if unavailable. A proposed owner is not a commitment. Carry this row into production rather than recreating it.

For consequential uncertain claims, use the compact evidence record in [the ADDIE decision cycle](../../instructional-design/references/addie-decisions.md#keep-consequential-decisions-traceable). Preserve the source and context, including evidence that contradicts the preferred intervention.

## Select evidence for the question
Use existing records for error patterns, observation or a representative task for demonstrated performance, interviews for decisions and context, and surveys for broader self-report. Compare contrasting cases where useful. A confident interview response does not establish competence, and a small convenience sample does not establish prevalence. Collect only what can change the intervention decision. [CDC needs analysis](https://www.cdc.gov/training-development/php/about/assess-training-needs-conducting-needs-analysis.html).

When a survey is warranted, use neutral, single-concept questions with a meaningful recall period. Separate reported behavior from preferences and confidence. Offer appropriate not-applicable/unknown options; pilot wording and response routes with intended respondents. Record who was invited and who responded, including coverage and nonresponse limits. Do not convert a survey into a campaign for a predetermined course. [AAPOR best practices](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/best-practices/).

Explain purpose, voluntary participation where applicable, recording, access, intended use, and retention under the actual organization's rules. Data linked to named records or reidentifiable small groups are not anonymous; describe confidentiality controls accurately. Keep researcher observation separate from inferred motives. The [GOV.UK research-data guide](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/managing-user-research-data-participant-privacy) informs these privacy practices in a UK government context; do not transplant its legal obligations to every setting.

## Interpret patterns before prescribing causes
Define the metric, cohort, time window, unit of analysis, and missing data before comparing groups. A scatterplot shows association, a Pareto chart helps prioritize categories, and a fishbone or five-whys exercise proposes explanations. None proves a cause. Check exposure and plausible alternative explanations before attributing a business outcome to learner skill. Use [evaluation patterns](../../learning-evaluation/references/evaluation-patterns.md) when the requested output is an outcome analysis.

## Intervention fit
| Observed gap | Useful starting response | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Cannot recall a rare procedure | Searchable job aid with practice locating it | Can the person find and apply it in context? |
| Knows facts but cannot perform | Worked example, guided practice, feedback, retry | Independent attempt |
| Makes inconsistent judgment | Contrasting cases and criteria, calibration | Rationale and evidence use |
| Cannot access the tool | Access or workflow correction | Successful task start |
| Conflicting instructions | Clarify source of authority | Agreement on actual rule |
| No opportunity or time to perform | Operational change or transfer support | Opportunity observed |
| Experienced learners have varied approaches | Complex cases and peer comparison | Quality of tradeoff reasoning |

This table is a local decision aid, not an exhaustive diagnostic instrument.

## Outcomes
Prefer: “Given a draft and source pack, identify unsupported claims, revise them, and cite the evidence used.”
Weak: “Understand responsible AI.”
Overconstrained: “Identify precisely five issues in 90 seconds” when speed and five issues are irrelevant.

A success criterion can be descriptive: no invented source claims, audience needs addressed, reasoning supported. Numbers need a rationale.

## Choosing a design approach
Backward design is useful for aligning the destination task. ADDIE can organize analysis, design, development, implementation, and evaluation work. Iterative prototyping is useful when the learner interaction or content scope is uncertain. Use only the structure that improves the requested work, and make the first usable asset early.

## SME interview mini-guide
1. “What must learners do?”
2. “Show a recent example.”
3. “Where do beginners stumble?”
4. “Which cues change your decision?”
5. “What would good work show?”
6. “What can the course change?”

Ask one at a time and probe specifics. Keep the SME's claim and your interpretation separate.

## Deliverable example
**Gap:** staff can define a good prompt but accept plausible unsupported AI claims.
**Evidence:** illustrative diagnostic task shows omissions in source checking; replace with actual data when supplied.
**Intervention:** compare two outputs against one source, repair one, retry on a new case.
**Measure:** source-linked rubric on an independent attempt.
**Out of scope:** proving organization-wide productivity gains.
