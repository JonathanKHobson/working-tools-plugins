# Evaluation patterns

## Small pilot
Choose a representative task, define the intended performance, and observe a few learners with varied relevant prerequisites. This can reveal usability and comprehension problems; it cannot establish population prevalence. Record prompt, support given, task result, error type, access barrier, and learner explanation.

Use the evidence to make a specific change, then recheck that interaction. Avoid treating every preference as a redesign requirement.

Define the question and keep/revise/drop decision before the pilot. Record fidelity, participant selection, support, and limits. A script review can expose ambiguity but cannot verify an interactive player's scoring or assistive-technology behavior. Keep expert accuracy review and learner performance evidence distinct.

## Evidence ladder without overclaiming
| Evidence | Can support | Cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Enrollment | Reach | Completion or learning |
| Completion | Reaching a platform completion state | Independent competence |
| Satisfaction | Reported experience | Retention or transfer |
| Immediate performance | Task success under observed conditions | Lasting retention |
| Delayed performance | Retention under those conditions | Universal transfer |
| Changed-context task | Application to that new task | Every domain |
| Workplace measure | Observed operational change | Training caused the change |

A posttest without a baseline supports current proficiency under the test conditions, not a learning gain. Retrospective confidence ratings remain self-report. Follow-up timing should reflect the real chance to apply the skill. [CDC measuring effectiveness](https://www.cdc.gov/training-development/php/about/evaluate-training-measuring-effectiveness.html).

Kirkpatrick categories or LTEM may organize an explicitly requested evaluation, but do not infer a causal chain from movement across categories. A recognition item, decision task, and full performance can support different claims.

## Practical measures
Pick an outcome measure, a process measure, and a balancing measure only if useful. Example: proportion of source-supported corrections; use of the check routine; time burden or access failures. Specify numerator/denominator, collection window, missingness, and what result would change the design.

For business metrics, retain the definition, cohort, follow-up window, aggregation level, and concurrent changes. Distinguish percentage-point change from relative percentage change. For example, a hypothetical rate falling from 30% to 15% falls 15 percentage points, or 50% relative to its initial value; neither identifies its cause. Team averages do not establish individual behavior. Check recruiting, pay, onboarding, tools, coaching, or policy changes when relevant rather than crediting training alone.

## Transfer plan
Destination task; opportunity/date; independent or aided performance; support available; expected evidence; consent/collection boundary; review owner. A learner who lacked a chance to use the skill has not necessarily failed to transfer it.

## Reporting
“Seven of nine participants corrected all contradictions in the supplied exercise; two received hints” is a bounded observation if those data are real.
“This course makes staff 40% more effective” needs an operational definition, actual evidence, and a defensible attribution design.
No such numbers are defaults to insert.

Keep qualitative statements traceable. Quote only exact wording. Preserve contradictory evidence. Recommend the smallest next test when uncertainty matters.
