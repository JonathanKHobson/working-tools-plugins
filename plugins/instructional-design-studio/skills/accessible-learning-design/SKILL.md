# Accessible Learning Design

Inspect the actual lesson and delivery environment. Separate an access barrier from the skill learners are supposed to practice. Keep the assessed construct constant when changing how learners perceive information or respond.

Use [access review](references/access-review.md) for format-specific checks and [research](../instructional-design/references/learning-science.md) for CAST UDL and W3C source boundaries.

For a shared board or facilitated canvas, use the [shared-board participation check](references/access-review.md#shared-board-participation-check) to inspect the real contribution route and its matching text companion.

## Adapt
Identify barriers in prerequisites, language, sensory presentation, navigation, motor interaction, time pressure, participation, and feedback. Offer practical alternatives such as a text version, captioned demonstration, keyboard path, asynchronous contribution, additional processing time, or equivalent individual activity. Do not assign people fixed visual/auditory/kinesthetic learning styles.

An alternative must preserve the objective: text discussion may replace speaking when conceptual reasoning is assessed, but spoken practice needs an accessible spoken route if oral delivery is the actual target. Ask about access needs without requiring diagnoses or disclosure of private histories.

For children or vulnerable learners, use age-appropriate consent and supervision practices consistent with the setting; check current institutional requirements when applicable. Do not import private student records into examples.

## Deliver
Return a prioritized barrier table: artifact/anchor, barrier, affected task, proposed change, equivalent success evidence, and verification. Apply authorized reversible edits. Check the rendered result and accessible interaction where possible. A checklist review is not a claim of legal compliance or complete WCAG conformance; label untested keyboard, screen-reader, caption, or PDF reading-order behavior accurately.
