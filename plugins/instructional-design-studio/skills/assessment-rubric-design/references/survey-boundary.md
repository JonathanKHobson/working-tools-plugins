# Assessment, survey and form boundary

Choose by the intended evidence, not by the widget. A multiple-choice interface may hold a knowledge item or an opinion item.

| Need | Owner and evidence |
|---|---|
| Demonstrate recall, explanation, judgment or performance | Assessment & Rubric Design; key/criteria tied to source and outcome |
| Report experience, confidence, satisfaction or use | `survey-studio:survey-strategist`; decision → construct → question → coded value → analysis use |
| Shared course/workshop feedback | `survey-studio:survey-strategist`; adapt its broad workflow and keep one canonical instrument in the owning project |
| Evaluate learning/retention/transfer using several measures | Learning Evaluation owns interpretation; assessment and survey specialists own their distinct instruments |
| Collect registration or operational information | Survey Studio's form path; validation/necessity, not a knowledge score |

Survey Studio is optional. If unavailable, provide neutral text items, a stated analysis use and pretest limits without claiming an export was validated or creating a new survey engine here. Retain stable IDs and common context when handing over; do not duplicate its canonical instrument/schema.

## Mixed instruments

Label each item as direct evidence or self-report. Keep knowledge scores separate from confidence and satisfaction. A confidence rating after a response can prompt reflection about calibration; confidence never adds knowledge credit. Score the answer without access to the rating when that helps avoid bias. Only link records when needed and accurately describe identifiers/privacy.

Knowledge prompt: “Given these room-booking rules, which request can be approved?” It has a defensible key.

Experience prompt: “How easy or difficult was it to use the booking guide for this task?” It measures perceived ease. Include a non-use path separately from neutral when applicable; it has no correct answer. Route wording, scales, eligibility, missing-data coding and pretesting to Survey Studio.

For a “fun quiz,” distinguish a learning game with justified scoring from an entertainment/personality quiz. Do not imply a validated trait measure. Enjoyment and active participation may explain feasibility; neither is sufficient evidence of mastery.
