# Assignment and performance brief

Use a transparent purpose–task–criteria foundation, associated with TILT. The additional operational fields here are this plugin's authoring extension. See [sources](sources.md) for the source and rights boundary.

1. **Purpose:** what capability will be practiced/demonstrated, why it matters, and the intended inference.
2. **Situation and audience:** a credible role, necessary context, input evidence, intended user of the work. Remove decorative complexity.
3. **Task and product:** specific learner actions, required submission, completion conditions and optional enhancements.
4. **Conditions:** resources, collaboration, AI assistance, time, format, constraints, accessibility alternatives and any genuinely necessary independence.
5. **Evidence:** request only process evidence needed to judge the target: annotations, a decision with a reason, revision comparison, demonstration or small oral/text follow-up. Avoid exhaustive tracking.
6. **Criteria:** observable rubric dimensions tied to outcomes; weights, critical-error/retry rules, accepted alternative solutions, examples and borderline cases.
7. **Feedback and revision:** who supplies feedback, timing, what learners do with it and whether/how a revision changes the score.

If the task requires an unavailable account or device, provide equivalent inputs and actions that preserve the construct. An access accommodation should change incidental demands, not silently change what the score means. Document any unavoidable inference change.

## Choose evidence that survives superficial success

Ask what could produce a polished artifact without the intended knowledge. Use a rationale, changed-condition decision, or focused independent performance only when needed to resolve that ambiguity. Do not equate AI-supported work with unaided performance; either assess the supported workflow explicitly or collect a proportionate independent check. Do not use writing style as evidence of cheating.

For a group product, state which conclusions are about the team. Before claiming individual capability, collect an individual decision, explanation, or application. Reward the target evidence instead of loudness, charisma, winning, or speed.

## Review before handoff

Score a completed example, a plausible partial submission, a flawed submission, and a mixed case. Can every scorable combination receive one band without double penalties? Can a learner understand the brief without guessing hidden requirements? If not, repair the brief or rubric. Keep unobserved/missing evidence separate. A fully specified draft is ready for review/pilot, not proof that learners understand it.
