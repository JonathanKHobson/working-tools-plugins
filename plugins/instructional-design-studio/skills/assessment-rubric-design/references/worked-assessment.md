# Worked packet: booking a learning space

Original fictional example. Draft for formative practice; not learner-piloted or empirically calibrated. All required policy facts appear below. The [JSON authoring example](../assets/assessment-template.json) represents the selected-response version.

## Learner packet

**Purpose:** choose and justify a booking using all stated constraints. This demonstrates one bounded rule-application task, not general event-management competence.

**Rule card, version 1:** Room M has 12 seats and no projector. Room N has 20 seats and a projector. Both rooms are available. Book whole 30-minute blocks and reserve enough time for the full session. A group of 16 needs a projector for 50 minutes. No setup or cleanup time is required in this exercise.

Use only the visible card. First respond individually; discuss afterward. About three minutes is a planning estimate, not a scored deadline. A spoken or typed answer is equivalent. Generated answers are not permitted for the initial response because this check targets your own application of the rules.

**Selected response:** Which booking meets every requirement? Select one. One point for the correct choice; otherwise zero; no penalty.

- A: Room M for 60 minutes.
- B: Room N for 30 minutes.
- C: Room N for 60 minutes.

**Constructed-response alternative:** propose the room and duration, and explain how capacity, equipment, and time support the decision. Use a few sentences or a brief spoken explanation. Use this instead of the selected-response question when independent reasoning is needed; after seeing the key it is a supported learning activity.

## Author-only key and feedback

Key C: M cannot accommodate 16 or supply a projector; N can. Fifty minutes needs two 30-minute blocks, so 60 minutes. A omits capacity/equipment; B rounds down and leaves insufficient time. These error interpretations are author hypotheses until actual learner reasoning is observed.

Feedback: compare the proposed booking against each requirement. Correct a missed constraint, explain its consequence, and retry with changed inputs. Do not say simply “C is correct.”

## Rubric for the constructed alternative

Score three dimensions independently, each 0–2, maximum 6. Apply the first matching rule in each row: contradiction/incorrect result → 0; correct result with incomplete support → 1; correct result with all required support → 2. No assessable response because of an access/recording failure is **unobserved**, not 0.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capacity | Chooses M or states a false capacity rationale | Chooses N without explaining the capacity comparison | Chooses N and explains 20 seats accommodate 16 |
| Equipment | Chooses M or falsely says M supplies the projector | Chooses N without connecting it to the projector need | Chooses N and explains its projector meets the requirement |
| Duration | Proposes other than 60 minutes or asserts a conflicting rule | Proposes 60 with incomplete block/coverage reasoning | Explains two 30-minute blocks cover 50 minutes |

Any proposal with a critical unmet constraint requires revision regardless of total. This is an exercise-specific feedback rule, not a validated pass mark. Extra polished wording earns no credit. Accepted equivalents include “one hour” and accurate verbal arithmetic. A correct phrase accompanied by a contradictory claim receives the contradiction rule for that dimension; do not subtract again elsewhere for the same error.

Calibration:

- “N has 20 seats for 16, its projector is needed, and two half-hour blocks cover 50 minutes.” → 2/2/2.
- “N, 60 minutes.” → 1/1/1; results fit but reasoning is absent.
- “M, 60; the time works, and M holds 20 with a projector.” → 0/0/1; false capacity/equipment; duration result lacks complete support.
- “N holds 20 and has the projector, so book 30 minutes; round down.” → 2/2/0; revise duration even though other dimensions are strong.

Have a second reviewer score the mixed case without seeing these scores; reconcile evidence and descriptors. Source/key consistency is an author review. These illustrative scored responses are not real learner data.

## Playful application and transfer

Pairs act as booking advisers: one proposes, one checks each constraint. Record each learner's initial choice first. Award no points for speed, performance style, or winning an argument. Debrief: “Which condition ruled out an attractive option?”

Independent retry: 10 people, no projector needed, 40 minutes, same rules. Either M or N for 60 minutes is defensible. Accept both; the changed input deliberately tests whether the learner notices that the room is no longer unique. If asking for the smallest suitable room, state that extra criterion explicitly before scoring M alone.

A later unfamiliar rule card can probe transfer; a repeated card after feedback does not establish it. For the pilot, ask target-like learners to explain the directions and their choices, then inspect access difficulties and competing answers. No pilot has occurred.
