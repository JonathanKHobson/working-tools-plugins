# Learning science and design decisions

Use the relevant section, not the whole reference for every task. Research checked September 8, 2026. These are selective, bounded research notes for instructional design, not a systematic review or a claim that every method works in every setting.

## Evidence categories
**Empirical synthesis** summarizes studies and inherits their population, measures, and comparison limits. **Theory/research review** explains mechanisms and conditions without guaranteeing an effect size. **Professional framework** organizes design decisions; it is not experimental validation. **Technical standard** defines requirements within a specified scope. **Local adaptation** is a proposed implementation to pilot.

## R1. Align the evidence with the intended performance
Carnegie Mellon's Eberly Center describes the need for objectives, assessment, and instruction to reinforce one another. A course can fail this alignment by teaching summary while assessing critique, or intending application while testing recall. This is institutional design guidance, not an effect-size claim. [CMU, “Why should assessments, learning objectives, and instructional strategies be aligned?” (undated)](https://www.cmu.edu/teaching/assessment/basics/alignment.html).

**Design implication:** inspect the actual learner action, not only the objective verb. A novice AI lesson can ask learners to find an unsupported claim and explain a revision. Naming a prompting acronym alone does not demonstrate that judgment.

## R2. Design backward, iterate in small pieces
Backward design begins with desired results, acceptable evidence, and then the learning plan. The approach helps avoid choosing attractive activities before deciding what they are meant to accomplish. The ASCD article also discusses examining causes before selecting action. [McTighe and Thomas, “Backward Design for Forward Action” (2003)](https://ascd.org/el/articles/backward-design-for-forward-action).

**Local application:** define the final performance early, then make a representative lesson and inspect it. Treat ADDIE as a lifecycle vocabulary and backward design as an alignment aid; neither establishes effectiveness by itself. Do not impose a rigid waterfall if the user needs a rapid course revision.

## R3. Retrieval, spacing, worked examples, and explanation
The IES practice guide assigns differing evidence levels to recommendations. It supports spacing, alternating worked examples with problem solving, relating concrete and abstract representations, retrieval quizzes, and explanatory questions; it rates some recommendations more strongly than others. The guide dates to 2007 and concerns content learning across educational subjects. [Pashler et al., “Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning”](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1).

**Local application:** have learners attempt recall before seeing an answer, give feedback, and return to important material after a delay. Novices can study an example before trying a similar task. The schedule should fit the retention goal; a single universal spacing formula is not established by this source. An activity that feels difficult still needs enough scaffolding to be productive.

## R4. Activity and learning are not interchangeable
Freeman and colleagues' meta-analysis supports active learning relative to traditional lecturing in undergraduate STEM courses. The study's population and broad variety of interventions matter: it does not validate every game or guarantee the same result for a corporate workshop. The accessible abstract/search record was checked; the full paper was not accessible through the browser on this pass. [Freeman et al. (2014), PNAS, 111(23), 8410–8415](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1319030111).

**Local application:** define the reasoning or performance elicited by participation. More clicks, more discussion, or visible enthusiasm are not adequate evidence that an objective was met.

## R5. Cognitive load and multimedia
Mayer's research program differentiates relevant processing from extraneous demands. The publisher excerpt describes coherence, signaling, proximity of corresponding words and pictures, segmentation, and pre-training. [Mayer, ed., The Cambridge Handbook of Multimedia Learning, 2nd ed. (2014), publisher excerpt](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811070/35201/excerpt/9781107035201_excerpt.pdf). The excerpt was available in the search index; full chapter evidence was not reviewed.

**Local application:** present the explanation near what it explains; cue the important part; break a demonstration at meaningful decisions; teach prerequisite terms before a dense task. Do not convert redundancy findings into a blanket ban on captions, transcripts, or learner-requested text. Those provide access and user control. Test actual task performance and readability.

## R6. Learners, context, and transfer
How People Learn II synthesizes learning research across cognitive, social, cultural, and educational perspectives. It emphasizes the interaction of prior knowledge, motivation, and context, and discusses transfer through underlying principles. [National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (2018), introduction](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/24783/chapter/3).

**Local application:** elicit prior knowledge before deciding support; use more than one context; ask learners to explain the invariant principle. A coffee-shop content exercise can introduce prompting, but a second domain is needed to inspect broader application. Do not promise general transfer after a single familiar exercise.

## R7. Motivation without manipulation
Ryan and Deci distinguish types of motivation rather than treating it as one amount. Their review connects autonomy, competence, and relatedness with motivational processes and explains that extrinsic motivation can be more or less internalized. [Ryan and Deci (2000), “Intrinsic and Extrinsic Motivations: Classic Definitions and New Directions,” Contemporary Educational Psychology, 25, 54–67](https://www.selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_IntExtDefs.pdf).

**Local application:** explain relevance, offer meaningful bounded choice, give achievable challenge and specific feedback, and support belonging. Do not assert that every reward harms motivation or that an entertaining activity creates intrinsic motivation for everyone. Mandatory training can still be designed respectfully.

## R8. Variability and access
CAST UDL Guidelines 3.0 organize options for engagement, representation, and action/expression, with learner agency as the goal. This is a design framework, not a diagnostic system or guarantee that offering choices will produce learning. [CAST, UDL Guidelines 3.0 (2024)](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/).

**Local application:** provide flexible access routes and ways to participate while retaining the learning goal. Consult actual learner needs. Avoid treating a diagnosis, age group, or cultural identity as a complete description of a person.

## R9. Learning-style matching is not an evidence base
Pashler and colleagues found inadequate support for the hypothesis that instruction should be matched to assessed learning styles. This does not mean preferences or sensory access needs are irrelevant. [Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer, and Bjork (2008), “Learning Styles: Concepts and Evidence”](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x).

**Local application:** use varied representations because the content and access requirements warrant them. Do not label a person a visual learner and restrict their instruction accordingly. Avoid unsourced learning pyramids, fixed attention-span claims, “left brain/right brain” teaching profiles, or percentages of retention by modality.

## R10. Accessibility checks have a defined scope
WCAG 2.2 is a W3C Recommendation for web content. Requirements span perceivability, operability, understandability, and robustness; conformance requires checking all applicable criteria at the claimed level. [W3C, Web Content Accessibility Guidelines 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/).

**Local application:** inspect meaningful text alternatives, captions, keyboard access, visible focus, contrast, labels, reading order, timing, and error recovery. A limited visual audit cannot certify conformance. A PDF or in-person workshop also requires checks appropriate to that medium.

## R11. Practical evaluation
Carnegie describes practical measurement as information gathered to help people learn how processes operate and improve them. It emphasizes timely signals tied to an improvement aim. This is an improvement framework, not a design for proving causation. [Carnegie Foundation, “Practical Measurement for Improvement — Overview” (undated)](https://www.carnegiefoundation.org/practical-measurement-for-improvement/practical-measurement-for-improvement-overview/).

**Local application:** choose measures that can change a teaching decision. Use direct performance, error types, access failures, and later-use opportunities alongside feedback. Treat completion as completion and satisfaction as satisfaction.

## Research use and disagreements
When a recommendation conflicts with observed learner needs, investigate the mismatch: population, prerequisite level, task, delivery, measure, or implementation. Prefer targeted pilots over a universal rule. Check current official requirements for LMS, software, accessibility standards, or institutional policy when producing a new artifact.

Separate source-derived claims from proposed implementations. Do not reproduce protected manuals or claim affiliation/certification. Keep narrow quotations accurate; prefer concise paraphrase plus exact links. External framework names are attributed methods, not inventions by Kyle.
