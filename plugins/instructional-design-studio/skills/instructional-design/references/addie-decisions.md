# ADDIE as a decision cycle

Read for an ADDIE request, a course lifecycle, or a decision about whether and how to build instruction. For one rubric or lesson revision, use its owning skill directly. These are local design controls adapted from the supplied research syntheses, not a new validated framework.

ADDIE names Analyze, Design, Develop, Implement, and Evaluate. It supports feedback and revision during development; completing its phases does not prove effectiveness. Respect a supplied institutional process. [CDC training development](https://www.cdc.gov/training-development/php/about/index.html).

## Use the next unresolved decision

| Area | Question | Smallest useful evidence or output | Revisit when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Analyze | What performance is missing, and can instruction address it? | Observed task, gap, constraints, possible nontraining response | Learners can perform but the environment prevents use |
| Design | What would demonstrate the outcome? | An unfamiliar task, criteria, aligned practice and feedback | Assessment asks for something learners never practiced |
| Develop | Does the proposed experience elicit the intended action? | A representative prototype and observed attempt | Recognition succeeds but generation or performance fails |
| Implement | Can learners and facilitators use it in the actual setting? | Rehearsal, access check, support and fallback owners | Tools, prerequisites, or facilitation are unavailable |
| Evaluate | What can the evidence justify, and what changes next? | Performance evidence and a decision with limitations | Results contradict assumptions or the need changes |

Move between these decisions as evidence warrants. A one-page change may resolve the need; an authorized complete course still requires completing its full agreed scope.

## Keep consequential decisions traceable

Use existing course notes or alignment rows. Add only the missing fields; these are not three mandatory new documents.

- **Evidence and assumptions:** claim → status (observation, stakeholder report, research finding, hypothesis, or proposal) → source/locator → context and limits → implication → uncertainty and next check.
- **Learning trace:** need → objective → practice → feedback → assessment → application support → evaluation measure. Give priority outcomes stable IDs. Explain missing connections; do not imply that filling a cell validates the task.
- **Operational dependencies:** required condition → proposed/accepted owner → due point → readiness evidence → fallback if absent. Naming an owner does not record their agreement.

A sponsor's business target, a learner's capability, and workplace application are related but separate claims. Keep expectations and dependencies visible without promising the course will independently produce the business result.

## Prototype an uncertainty

State a question and the evidence that would lead to keeping, revising, or dropping the design. A requested prototype includes usable source/example material, learner instructions, and feedback/scoring guidance; a list of materials to create is not the prototype. Observe the action and help required, interpret the result, revise, and retest. Label simulated observations as simulated.

If a pilot uses numeric cutoffs, mark them provisional and explain their decision purpose; do not present convenience numbers as established mastery standards. Address unusable or confounded evidence before applying thresholds, then give nonconflicting branches including inconclusive results. Success on a revised example after seeing its answer shows supported revision, not independent performance on a new task.

Match fidelity and reviewers to the question. A script can expose unclear reasoning; it cannot prove keyboard operation. SMEs check accuracy, representative learners reveal comprehension and performance problems, and specialist checks address technical or accessibility questions. Rough assets must still permit participation. Allocate review time and a decision owner rather than collecting open-ended feedback indefinitely.

Example, hypothetical: learners can choose a neutral interview probe but cannot generate one. Test a demonstration, a short generative rehearsal, feedback, and a different prompt. Extra definition slides do not directly test the generation problem. Do not invent pilot results or treat a tiny pilot as a population estimate.

## Select frameworks by purpose

Use ADDIE for lifecycle decisions and backward design for outcome/evidence alignment. Use an iterative process when uncertainty benefits from early feedback; iteration does not belong exclusively to a named model. Use a learning approach to shape the actual practice and an evaluation design suited to the claim. Do not require an acronym stack or prescribe a universal best combination.

Before maintaining or expanding a course, reconsider whether the need, destination task, and delivery conditions still exist. Revise, replace with support, or retire obsolete instruction as appropriate within the requested scope.
