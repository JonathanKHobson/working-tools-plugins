# Neutral research next to marketing

**Scenario:** before choosing among routes A–C, the team wants to learn what adults need to know about an online dashboard practice session. The synthetic Open Practice Lab brief is used only as a concept stimulus. This is an instrument prototype, not collected data.

| Purpose | Wording or action | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Neutral concept introduction | “We are considering two online sessions for adults focused on planning and decision practice. People could bring a dashboard or use a fictional dataset.” | Acceptable only if accurate and shown consistently to all respondents. Do not call the offer proven or open for registration. |
| Research question 1 | “What would you need to know before deciding whether to attend?” | Open response; does not presume interest. |
| Research question 2 | “Which preparation option, if either, would you prefer: bring a dashboard, use a supplied fictional dataset, or neither?” | One decision concept; allow “not sure” and an explanation. |
| Research question 3 | “How, if at all, would captions or a recording affect your decision?” | Captions/recording remain pending. For a real study, split these into separate items to avoid a double-barrelled response. |
| Separate optional contact step | “May we contact you with registration information if the event is scheduled? This is optional and separate from your research answers.” | Requires a real consent, data-handling and contact process before use. No contact collection in this packet. |
| Rejected research item | “How excited are you to join our free, limited-capacity lab?” | Leading, assumes interest and injects a scarcity cue. |
| Rejected handoff | Treating “yes, I would consider attending” as a verified lead, registration or demand forecast | A stated intention in a convenience sample does not establish any of those states. |

The [AAPOR best-practices guidance](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/best-practices/) X06 supports neutral, single-concept questions and suitable response choices. Kyle's U13 reflection makes the same design concern; it is attributed coursework, not a validated audience study. Survey Studio/research methodology should own the final instrument, sampling, consent and analysis. Marketing may receive a method-qualified summary only after those steps. No participant answers were collected here.
