# Three different message architectures for one fictional offer

**Fixed facts:** F01–F12 in `claim-ledger.md`. These are original synthetic copy examples, not campaign outputs. Each route is a different order of decision-making, not a headline swap. None is declared effective. All CTA text is a placeholder; no registration page exists in this brief.

## Route A — task-first practical invitation

**Reader decision it leads with:** “Do I have a dashboard decision I want to practice?” Likely use: a task-oriented person who already has a dashboard. **Unverified assumption:** that this is a meaningful audience segment. **Architecture:** concrete task → ways to participate → schedule and terms → known limitations → next step.

> **Bring a dashboard decision to practice.**
>
> Open Practice Lab is two online sessions for adults focused on planning and decision practice. Bring one dashboard you want to think through, or use the supplied fictional dataset. No prior experience is required.
>
> **When:** 20 and 27 October 2027, 6–7:30pm Phoenix time. **Cost:** free. **Registration deadline:** 17 October 2027. Total capacity is 24 participants; current availability is unknown.
>
> Captions are planned but not confirmed. A recording decision is pending. The sessions have no participant-outcome evaluation yet, so this is an invitation to practice, not a promise of a result.
>
> **[Registration information to be supplied — no live signup yet]**

**Objection response:** the alternative fictional dataset means the reader does not have to bring a personal dashboard. **Ethos/logos/pathos lens:** exact constraints and preparation choice carry credibility and reasoning; the invitation respects a practical task without claiming a feeling. **Channel adaptation:** a future direct email could lead with the first two sentences, then include all logistics and limitations in the body. No email is prepared or sent here. **Comprehension check:** ask a reader whether they must bring a dashboard and whether captions/recording are confirmed.

## Route B — access-first entry path

**Reader decision it leads with:** “Can I take part without experience or my own data?” Likely use: a cold reader evaluating fit. **Unverified assumption:** that access questions are the first barrier. **Architecture:** eligibility → two participation paths → purpose → logistics → pending details → next step.

> **No dashboard experience required.**
>
> Open Practice Lab is for adults who want to practice planning and decision-making in two online sessions. You can bring one dashboard, or use a supplied fictional dataset. You do not need prior experience to take part.
>
> Sessions are 20 and 27 October 2027, from 6–7:30pm Phoenix time. Participation is free. Registration closes 17 October 2027. The total capacity is 24 participants, and current availability is not known in this draft.
>
> Captions are planned and still need confirmation; a recording decision is pending. No participant outcomes have been evaluated. The purpose is practice, with no promised achievement.
>
> **[Registration information to be supplied — no live signup yet]**

**Objection response:** clearly separates “no experience required” from any unsupported claim of beginner success. **Ethos/logos/pathos lens:** a factual access path reduces an unanswered practical question; it does not diagnose reader anxiety. **Channel adaptation:** a future landing-page opening could show the two participation paths as equal choices, then the same fact block. Writing/visual owners would decide presentation. **Comprehension check:** ask readers what they think they need to bring and what happens if they do not have a dashboard.

## Route C — transparent tradeoff briefing

**Reader decision it leads with:** “Do the known terms and unknowns make this worth considering?” Likely use: a detail-oriented reader, partner or accessibility-conscious channel. **Unverified assumption:** that seeing unresolved facts early helps this audience decide. **Architecture:** plain offer definition → known facts → pending decisions and evidence limit → opt-in next step.

> **Open Practice Lab: the details available now.**
>
> The purpose is planning and decision practice in two online sessions for adults. No prior experience is required. Bring one dashboard, or use a supplied fictional dataset.
>
> **Specified in this fictional brief:** 20 and 27 October 2027, 6–7:30pm Phoenix time; free; registration deadline 17 October 2027; total capacity 24 participants. Current availability is unknown.
>
> **Still to be decided or confirmed:** captions are planned, and the recording decision is pending. There is no participant-outcome evaluation yet. The sessions do not promise a particular achievement.
>
> If these terms fit what you want to practice, use the registration information when it becomes available. **[Registration information to be supplied — no live signup yet]**

**Objection response:** surfaces availability, captions and replay questions without pretending to answer them. **Ethos/logos/pathos lens:** trust comes from a complete state of knowledge rather than a visual badge or testimonial. **Channel adaptation:** a future partner blurb should keep “planned” and “pending” qualifiers beside any mention of captions or recording; a very short channel should omit these features rather than falsely simplify them. **Comprehension check:** ask readers to name what is still unknown and whether they would wait for an answer before deciding.

## Selection and rejected route

| Route | First question | Distinct structure | What evidence would change selection |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | What task can I bring? | Task → paths → terms → limits | Actual audience research shows task-first entry confuses readers |
| B | Can I participate? | Eligibility → paths → purpose → terms | Readers misunderstand the tool/preparation requirement |
| C | What is known and pending? | Terms → limits → opt-in | Readers miss the purpose or the limits despite early disclosure |

**Rejected:** “24 exclusive seats to transform your dashboard skills—claim yours before they disappear.” It converts total capacity into false current scarcity, invents a transformation, implies exclusivity and replaces the planning purpose with an outcome promise. It also offers no real signup endpoint. A 17 October deadline can be stated as scheduling information in any route without pressure. The [FTC dark-patterns report](https://www.ftc.gov/reports/bringing-dark-patterns-light) X02 supports scrutiny of false urgency, while the route-selection hypothesis remains untested.
