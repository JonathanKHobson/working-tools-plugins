# Discover meaning before fixing the expression

## Choose the decision and the scale

Identify whether the subject is a person, an organization, a product/service or a relationship among them. Name what the user wants to learn or make now, the intended contexts, existing decisions and what stays private or outside scope. Ask in small batches only when needed. A solo exploration may need one conversation and two rough expressions; a contested organizational rebrand needs stakeholder evidence and decision authority. These are different workloads, not maturity grades.

Use a working distinction: a brand involves identifying cues and accumulated associations; brand identity is the expression being designed; reputation reflects others' experience and judgment; positioning concerns relevant alternatives in a particular audience's decision; marketing and advertising distribute messages and offers. A brand profile records current evidence and decisions across these layers. None gives total control over perception, and a person exceeds the public brand.

Check an existing profile's scope, status and known changes before collecting more. Age alone does not invalidate it. Record source authority and applicability: direct current choices govern creative intent, evidence governs factual claims, and old drafts remain historical. A fresh AI document does not outrank an earlier human choice merely because it is newer.

## Gather a small, revealing evidence set

Start with a few concrete episodes and artifacts rather than an adjective questionnaire. Useful prompts:

- Describe a moment when the work felt natural, useful or worth doing. What did you actually do? What did someone else experience?
- Show a piece you recognize yourself in, one you admire but would not make, and one that is polished yet feels wrong. Which specific choices account for that reaction?
- What do people reliably seek from you or this organization? What evidence contradicts that account?
- What would you like to develop, even though it is not yet consistently demonstrated? What should remain a private interest?

For each item record source, date, creator/editor, context, what is directly observed, interpretation, permission for reuse and missing information. Do not treat a ghostwritten post as an unmediated voice sample. Intentional editing can be authentic; spontaneous frequency does not define desired style. A small corpus permits provisional findings, not a fixed minimum-word gate or a personality diagnosis.

For an organization, compare leadership intent with employee practice, customer experience and product/service delivery. Use specific recent incidents and artifacts. If the promise conflicts with delivery, name the operational gap instead of solving it with reassuring copy. Establish who can decide the promise and who must make it true. For a nonprofit or public body, distinguish beneficiaries, funders, staff and public audiences rather than inventing one buyer.

## Form hypotheses with room to disagree

Group evidence around contribution, perspective, recurring practice, relationship with others and expressive choices. For each candidate theme retain supporting examples, a counterexample, contexts where it applies, and an alternative explanation. Repetition may reflect a template, employer policy or platform convention rather than personal preference.

Use three separate labels: **observed pattern**, **chosen emphasis**, **development aspiration**. A person can choose an unfamiliar expressive direction without pretending it is a proven lifelong trait. Ask whether a synthesis feels recognizable, useful and sustainable. Invite rejection or correction without defending the machine's elegant narrative.

Turn abstract values into behavior and tradeoffs: what would this value change in a difficult interaction, a deadline or a design choice? Avoid mandatory archetypes, personality scoring and adjective quotas. They may be optional creative prompts if the user wants them; they are not validated diagnoses.

## Explore taste as evidence

For a few references and anti-references, capture the exact feature, attraction or discomfort, personal association, transferable principle, intended context and what must not be copied. Distinguish material preference from mechanism: liking handwritten marks might mean liking the texture, visible thought, informality or a remembered experience. Test more than one explanation before turning it into a rule.

Use making to learn: sketch an introduction, a simple visual composition or a short demonstration in two meaningfully different modes. Hold factual content reasonably stable. These are probes, not a demand to choose a permanent identity. Move to the design process when expression itself is the main uncertainty.

## Connect expression with audiences and alternatives

Describe each audience by situation, needs, relationship and desired understanding/action. Separate followers, peers, employers, learners, purchasers and beneficiaries when their tasks differ. Use their language from actual evidence; do not invent demographic personas or simulated quotes. Ask about recent behavior and alternatives before asking preferences. Keep consent to research separate from permission to publish a testimonial.

For a commercial offer, compare credible alternatives including self-service, the status quo and doing nothing. Separate expected competence, useful difference, proof, and memorable identifying cues. A combination can be relevant without being globally unprecedented. Avoid unsupported “only,” “best,” or “industry-leading” claims. Record adjacent proof as adjacent; absence of paying customers does not bar exploratory positioning, but limits claims about demand and repeatability.

Build a message ladder for the actual context: a plain introduction, a short explanation, one concrete example or proof, and an appropriate invitation. A tagline is optional. Test whether someone can explain what this is, why it might matter to them and what happens next. Use the existing [evidence-to-message method](../strategy-methods/evidence-to-message.md) for claim checks and substantive message architecture; do not substitute wordplay for strategic difference.

## Compare relationships without forcing a master brand

Map person, role, platform and project separately. Then compare only plausible structures: one shared identity, an endorsed relationship, or distinct identities with selective connections. Consider audience overlap, credibility transfer, confusion, maintenance effort, privacy boundaries and room for independent products or collaborators. Test visitor tasks: find a service, understand authorship, choose a learning resource, or discover a related interest.

Specify what stays shared, what can flex, what remains distinct and what stays private. Similar palettes are not required to establish a relationship; names, authorship and clear navigation may suffice. Topical separation is not guaranteed privacy. Do not decide business structure, naming rights or account consolidation from a branding exercise.

## Close at a useful decision

Use the profile contract to retain accepted choices, provisional interpretations, gaps and the next test. Distinguish an exploratory synthesis from an approved guide. Stop when the user has the requested useful artifact; do not insist on a full research program or a brand book when a provisional expression map resolves the question. Route factual study design to Research, expression to Brand Design, and a settled marketing objective to the existing campaign/offer specialist.
