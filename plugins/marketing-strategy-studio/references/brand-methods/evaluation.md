# Test the question, not a universal brand score

Define the decision before the test. Choose the smallest method that can expose the relevant failure. Prototype counts, time limits and sample sizes below are adjustable design choices, not validated branding thresholds.

| Question | Useful observation | Do not conclude |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does this fit the person or organizational commitments? | Specific choices they embrace/reject, effort or discomfort, examples of lived behavior | An audience or model can decide someone's identity |
| Is the message understood? | Unaided restatement of subject, contribution, audience and next step; particular misreadings | A polished tagline is clear because reviewers like it |
| Is the expression attributable? | Source attribution or confused attribution to alternatives; what cue caused it | A new attractive symbol already has recognition equity |
| Does it feel credible and relevant here? | What supports belief, what needs proof, where expectations conflict | A stated preference establishes demand |
| Can this be sustained? | Time and difficulty producing a second asset; where rules help or constrain | Brand consistency requires identical layouts |
| Does an actual offer work? | Defined eligible audience, exposure, response and downstream outcome | Likes or a few compliments demonstrate revenue impact |

For diagnostic audience feedback, recruit relevant people with permission, show equivalent-fidelity samples in balanced/randomized order where practical, and start with neutral prompts: “What do you think this is?” “What makes you say that?” “What would you expect next?” Ask preference later. Preserve exact observations separately from interpretation. Avoid asking respondents to validate a supplied flattering adjective. Record selection/recruitment limits, context and conflicting responses; a small convenience sample is diagnostic, not representative.

For recognition, distinguish familiarity with an asset from correct and exclusive attribution. Someone may remember a color yet attribute it to a competitor. Keep source names visible when the asset has not earned recognition; blind attribution can diagnose confusion but is not required for every early concept. Formal market estimates of fame/uniqueness require an appropriate audience and study design. A small creative test is not such a measurement.

For personal fit, include a practice test: write or speak a new example without mechanically imitating the prototype, then note what felt natural, strained or energizing. Familiarity is not the only criterion; a chosen developmental direction may feel new. Identify the difference between useful stretch and an unwanted performance. Do not equate hesitation with resistance that must be overcome.

For an organization, test both audience interpretation and delivery credibility with those responsible for the experience. Report disagreement by stakeholder role. A majority vote cannot repair a promise that operations cannot support. Name the decision owner and explicitly unresolved delivery dependencies.

Compare against the present baseline when relevant. Hold factual content stable, change one meaningful variable when attribution matters, and specify before testing what observation would alter the direction. Use a small decision table: evidence for, evidence against, tradeoff, decision and next uncertainty. Do not hide fatal trust/accessibility issues inside a weighted average.

Model critiques, persona simulations and heuristic reviews can identify questions and risks. Label them as model outputs; never describe them as interviews, independent market evidence or human acceptance. Record results in the project profile with dates, source IDs, limitations and actual decisions. Keep approved creative choices separate from factual substantiation and measured outcomes.

When the question becomes campaign effectiveness, use [marketing-measurement](../../skills/marketing-measurement/SKILL.md) for the complete method. For an instrument, study, validated scale or population claim, use the relevant Research/Survey owner. Do not invent a scientific confidence percentage from source counts or aesthetic ratings.
