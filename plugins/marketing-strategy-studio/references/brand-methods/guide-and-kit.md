# Assemble a trustworthy brand guide or kit

Use this method when the requested deliverable is a reusable brand guide or kit, or when auditing one for collaborators. An ordinary post, flyer, resize or single campaign asset should use its production owner. Start with the decision the guide must support, its intended makers and formats, and the smallest existing project sources that can answer it. A guide is useful when another maker can apply its rules with judgment and find the authoritative assets. Its polish alone says nothing about whether it matches the source or has been approved.

## Establish authority before writing rules

First identify which of these situations applies:

- **Approved existing identity:** Use the current approved, scoped brand profile, decisions, source assets and exceptions. Preserve recognizable choices unless the user requested a change. Record conflicting versions or missing evidence for the affected rule; do not silently let the newest file win.
- **Identity inferred from a template or artifact:** Describe observable cues, such as type hierarchy, color roles and layout rhythm, as source-derived observations. Label any proposed brand expression as proposed. A template may embody a useful direction, but it cannot establish the organization's audience, promise, values, positioning or approval. Use the relevant part of [discovery](discovery.md) when those strategic decisions actually matter.
- **New direction selected in this task:** Record who selected it, the scope and date, what it supersedes, and which factual claims still need support. Selection can authorize a creative rule for that scope without proving every claim or authorizing publication.

Apply the [profile status contract](profile.md) to consequential rules. Reuse recorded decisions without another approval loop. If one unresolved question would change the requested guide, ask that question or mark a provisional section clearly; keep independent sections moving. An incomplete strategy does not prohibit a faithful account of observed template mechanics.

## Keep three relationships distinct

Map **identity rules** to meaning and recognition across contexts: names, marks, color and type roles, voice principles, experience cues and exceptions. Map **medium or template rules** to a particular use: page grids, slide layouts, spreadsheet styles, component behavior, export constraints and editable placeholders. Map **writing rules** to voice behavior, examples, terminology and tone by situation. State inheritance and exceptions where they meet. A presentation layout can express an identity without becoming a universal logo or voice rule. A selected writing-template resource can serve a campaign without rewriting the whole brand.

For each consequential rule, make the source trail inspectable: rule ID, observed source location or approved decision, original or proposed status, applicable context, exception, version and owner. Link to the original editable asset and relevant rendered example when available. Where a style is only inferred, say so. Do not reverse-engineer business strategy from visual cues or treat generated examples as independent evidence. Keep confidential source excerpts out of a public guide and carry reuse or license limits forward.

## Build the requested handoff at the right depth

Use distinct parts when they help the next maker, without imposing a folder schema:

1. **Human guide:** purpose, audience, rationale, recognizable invariants, flexible choices, examples and non-examples, accessibility needs, decision status and known limits. Show how to make a second coherent application, not just how to copy the first.
2. **Machine-usable specification:** explicit scoped values and relationships that a tool can read or a maker can implement, with IDs, units, fallback behavior, source/version pointers and exceptions. Use a format the owning project can maintain. `DESIGN.md` or a writing style file can be a useful local handoff when its owner calls for one; neither is a universal brand standard. A specification reduces ambiguity but cannot guarantee identical output across models or tools.
3. **Asset library or manifest:** authoritative editable sources, checked exports, intended contexts, rights, owner, version and superseded pointers. Do not substitute a preview, a prompt or a polished PDF for an editable asset.

Keep the guide and specification linked to the same decision record. Give each a clear version, change owner and source authority. For an update, identify the affected rules and downstream guides, templates, assets or channels; record what must be revised, what can remain, and how a superseded item will be identified or retired. Keep a usable prior version or rollback path for live replacements. Do not publish or alter accounts merely because a guide was selected.

## Verify the claim being made

Separate **identity approval**, **source fidelity**, **technical validity**, **rendered usability**, **delivery** and **publication** in the handoff. For fidelity, compare a small set of source anchors with the actual native artifact and rendered result; visual extraction alone can miss important styles. Route exact native-format inspection and template contracts to `ux-design-toolkit:design-systems` and its template-design-contract method. Route rendered guide and asset production to `graphic-design-studio:graphic-design`, including its brand-guide production method and design review. Writing craft remains with `writing-quality-studio:voice-and-style`. Each owner should verify its changed anchors and exports. Record what was checked, against which version, and what remains untested. A checked export does not establish human acceptance or live availability.
