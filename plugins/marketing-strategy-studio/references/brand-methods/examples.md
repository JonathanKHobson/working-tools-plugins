# Worked examples — fictional, not user evidence

## A person with sparse, different expressions

Input: A fictional facilitator supplies a playful event invitation and a restrained technical explainer. They say both feel like them; they want a clearer introduction without merging audiences. No interviews or performance data exist.

Useful output: “Both examples guide people into an unfamiliar activity” is **H1, interpretation**, supported by the two artifacts and weakened by the possibility that guidance is simply required by both formats. “I want to emphasize participation” becomes **D1, chosen emphasis** only if the person selects it. “Known for transformative learning” remains unsupported and is omitted. The introduction can be drafted provisionally without waiting for paying customers or a long writing corpus.

Two exploratory treatments use the same factual introduction: one reveals the process through annotation and invitation; the other uses scene-setting and a vivid concrete example. The person can retain different treatments by context. No universal logo or parent-company name is inferred. Test comprehension with relevant readers and personal fit through making a second example. Neither test has happened merely because it is described here.

## An organization with competing stakeholder views

Input: A fictional community service wants to look accessible. Leadership prefers an exclusive premium aesthetic; staff report that complicated registration causes drop-off. There is no reliable conversion estimate.

Useful output: retain **H2, hypothesized audience effect**, and **O1, staff-reported registration friction** as separate statements. The design brief tests clear entry information and welcoming imagery; operational ownership of registration is recorded as a dependency. Leadership can select a direction, but that does not prove it improved access. Avoid “welcoming to everyone” as an achieved claim until supported. Do not average leadership preference and participant confusion into one score.

## Reusing a selected temporary asset

A project has an explicitly accepted temporary mark for a specific video format. Use it for that scope without a new approval loop. Record the temporary scope; do not infer approval for all channels, a permanent brand architecture or public deployment elsewhere. A rejected earlier candidate stays rejected even if its export/contrast checks passed.

## A request that bypasses discovery

“Resize this approved campaign graphic for a supplied format” goes directly to campaign-format adaptation. Preserve current assets and requested facts. A missing philosophical brand section is irrelevant to the resize; do not open a discovery interview or change the logo.
