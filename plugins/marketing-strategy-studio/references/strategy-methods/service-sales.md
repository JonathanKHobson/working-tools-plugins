# Service-sales routing for a solo practice

Use when a paid design/research, teaching/facilitation or creative service needs a qualified next conversation, a proposal or a clear handoff. This is a local adapter to existing complete methods, not another CRM or sales plugin.

## Choose the relevant method

- Need a proposal, one-pager, proof narrative or response to objections: read [sales-enablement](../../libraries/marketingskills/skills/sales-enablement/SKILL.md) and its support relevant to the selected artifact. For a proposal structure and stage-specific examples, use the existing [sales enablement frameworks](../../libraries/digital-marketing-pro/skills/campaign-orchestrator/sales-enablement.md).
- Need stage definitions, follow-up ownership or lost handoffs: read [RevOps](../../libraries/marketingskills/skills/revops/SKILL.md). Adapt it to the actual solo workflow; a simple project table may be sufficient. Enterprise role, volume, SLA, lead-score and conversion benchmarks are examples, not defaults or measured facts about the current user’s work.
- Need an actual message: use the existing cold-email or lifecycle method, Writing for voice, and the actual sending owner for an authorized send. A CRM script in a library is not an available authenticated service.

## Apply to the current opportunity

1. Name the prospect's verified problem, requested deliverable and decision context. Separate their words from our interpretation. Use current supplied evidence; do not infer budget, urgency, purchasing authority or consent from engagement alone.
2. Identify what is sufficiently known to make progress: fit, decision-maker/contact, timing, practical constraints, available budget if volunteered, and the smallest useful next action. Missing information becomes a focused discovery question, not a fabricated qualified lead.
3. Use a minimal progression appropriate to the work: inquiry → discovery → scoped proposal → accepted/declined/on hold → delivery handoff. Each stage needs an evidence-based entry/exit criterion and next owner/action/date. A scheduled call is not acceptance; a sent proposal is not revenue; agreement is not payment.
4. Build only the requested sales artifact. A service proposal normally states the problem, deliverables, boundaries, client inputs, schedule assumptions, evidenced proof, price/options when authorized, acceptance criteria and next decision. Mark estimates and unapproved terms. Do not invent ROI, results, testimonials, discounts or artificial scarcity from source examples.
5. For an objection, clarify its substance and respond with supported tradeoffs: narrower scope, different timing or relevant proof. Do not manipulate, pressure or promise work beyond capacity. Send only on current authorization; use Gmail's existing review workflow when applicable.
6. Once agreed, pass the actual scope/decisions to Work Management. Invoice records and payment reconciliation belong to Solo Service Operations when installed; existing paid-GM workflows retain venue/event terms. These handoffs do not automatically create contracts, invoices or accounts.

## Compact working table

| Opportunity | Evidence/source | Stage | Open decision | Next action/date | Owner | Outcome evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fictional workshop inquiry | Request says a 90-minute session is desired | Discovery | Audience size and access requirements | Ask two focused questions | Current owner | None yet |

Success is a clearer supported decision or an authorized delivered artifact. Do not build a pipeline dashboard unless the user needs it. Review source claims, client identity, scope, next action and prepared/sent/accepted distinctions before closeout.
