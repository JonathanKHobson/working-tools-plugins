## M01 — Audience evidence before positioning

- **Trigger and task:** a brief names a target segment, pain, motivation or preferred channel without direct evidence. The strategist must choose a useful first message without pretending to know the reader.
- **Current coverage/owner:** Marketing Strategy Operator L01–L03 already asks for audience, buyer uncertainty and source evidence; audience research belongs to its research owner. U06/U12 favor contextual research, but are Kyle's reflections. X07 advises treating non-user opinions as assumptions.
- **Mechanism:** distinguish a *design audience* (“adults with or without dashboard experience are allowed”) from a *demonstrated audience need* (“adults are afraid of dashboards”). Only the former is supplied here.
- **Decision:** write an audience-assumption row with segment, decision context, observed evidence, alternate explanation, confidence and next check. Select a provisional route by the known task or access condition; avoid demographic psychologizing. If an assumption would change eligibility, safety, cost or the main promise, hold that claim for research.
- **Example:** “Adults with no prior experience are welcome; a supplied fictional dataset is available” is supported by the brief. “Beginners feel intimidated by data” is an untested hypothesis; route B may be *tested* for comprehension, but that emotion must not be asserted.
- **Counterexample:** inventing a persona named “overwhelmed nonprofit director” and claiming this offer was designed for that group.
- **Conditions/tradeoffs:** a specific real audience may warrant a narrow route; with no audience evidence, use a broad factual invitation. More interviews cost time, so first test the decision-changing unknown rather than research every preference.
- **Effort and test:** small for ledger, medium for research. Proposed test: ask actual likely readers what they think the lab is for and which information they need before deciding; compare with the assumptions. Failure: material audience claims survive without evidence.
