## M06 — Research instrument neutrality firewall

- **Trigger and task:** a marketer proposes a survey/interview near a campaign or wants to turn a research response into a lead. The task is to learn what people think without steering them toward registration.
- **Current coverage/owner:** U13 [0005]–[0016] is Kyle's survey reflection. Survey Studio and research methodology own instruments; Marketing owns only the use of consented findings in strategy. [AAPOR's survey practices](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/best-practices/) X06 advise one concept per item and avoiding language that pushes answers.
- **Mechanism:** a promotional preface or bundled “learn and sign up” question can contaminate the answer and the respondent's expectations. The inquiry and invitation need distinct purposes and consent.
- **Decision:** state the research question and sampling boundary first. Ask neutral, single-concept questions with appropriate “not sure” or nonresponse choices; keep event promotion outside the research instrument. If recruitment or future contact is desired, seek separate explicit permission after the task, without conditioning the research response. Never describe instrument replies as proof of demand without considering sampling and wording.
- **Example:** “What information would you need before deciding whether to attend an online dashboard practice session?” is a research question. “Would you like a separate invitation when registration opens?” belongs in a clearly separate, optional contact step.
- **Counterexample:** “How excited are you to join this free, limited workshop?” assumes interest, injects scarcity and collects a flattering metric.
- **Conditions/tradeoffs:** some research can legitimately show an accurate concept description; it must not be optimized to persuade. Research participants may already be exposed to marketing; record that exposure. Privacy, consent and retention are owned by the research process.
- **Effort and test:** small for review, medium for actual fieldwork. Proposed instrument review flags leading wording, double-barrelled items, embedded CTAs and unconsented contact collection. Failure: research answers are treated as conversion data or independent proof of demand.
