# Evidence to message decisions

Load this reference for positioning, offers or cross-channel messages when source evidence is thin, a material claim is consequential, or several architectures must be compared. Use [first move](first-move.md), [planning workflow](planning-workflow.md) and [output contracts](output-contracts.md) for the full strategy procedure. The inherited offer, CRO, copy, launch and measurement methods remain intact; this is a claim and selection check, not another campaign template.

## Freeze the known offer

Write the exact offer as supplied: audience/eligibility, activity or deliverable, format, schedule, price, deadline, preparation, capacity, actual availability, accessibility, recording, proof and CTA state. Mark each field `confirmed`, `planned`, `pending`, `unknown` or `synthetic`. Do not silently convert a planned feature into a promise, total capacity into seats remaining, a synthetic example into social proof, or a placeholder into a live action.

For every material message claim, record:

| ID | Exact proposed wording | Type | Exact supporting source | Supported scope | Likely reader inference | Qualification or narrower wording | Status | Allowed channels |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Types include offer fact, measured benefit, testimonial, illustrative scenario, aspiration and hypothesis. Status is `ready`, `verify` or `remove`. A “ready” claim requires support for what a reasonable reader would infer, not only the literal clause. Put the qualification beside the claim; if it negates the headline's implication, rewrite the headline. Do not reuse a testimonial without source, permission and material-connection review. In US advertising contexts, use the FTC's current claim and endorsement guidance as a review input; seek specialist legal review where needed.

## Choose the reader's decision path

Record the reader question and evidence behind it. If the audience need is merely assumed, label it. For a fixed offer, select one route to test:

- **Task-first:** lead with the concrete thing the reader can practice or do, then participation paths, terms and limits.
- **Access-first:** lead with eligibility and available ways to participate, then purpose, terms and limits.
- **Tradeoff-first:** lead with known terms and unresolved conditions, then a voluntary next step.

Other structures are possible, but a second route must change the first question, information order and objection strategy, not only the hook or tone. Keep all material facts and qualifications invariant. Do not insert social proof, bonuses, guarantee or urgency sections merely because an inherited template has them. The copywriting and Writing Quality owners choose final prose and voice after strategy selects a route.

## Answer objections without creating assurances

For each actual or plausible objection, write `question → known answer → unresolved answer → verification owner → message location`. A real deadline can be scheduling information. A current seats-remaining statement requires a verified live count; total capacity does not supply it. If a limitation changes participation, place it before the CTA. If the answer is not known, say so or withhold the message until the owner resolves it.

Do not repeat inherited offer forecasts such as an uncited conversion-lift range or presume that every offer needs scarcity, a bonus or a guarantee. Define a measurement design from the task: source and denominator for action metrics; reader comprehension and expectation alignment as separate outcomes. A model-written critique is a hypothesis, not participant evidence. No causal improvement claim follows from a draft or a click count alone.

## Adapt without widening

Attach claim IDs to each channel version. Preserve price, eligibility, time/timezone, deadline, preparation, material limitations and CTA status. When a short channel cannot hold every detail, omit a weaker benefit and point to a real full-detail destination once available; do not omit a condition that changes the decision. Research surveys and interviews stay with their research owner: neutral single-concept questions and optional separate contact consent. Marketing may use qualified findings, not tune the instrument for persuasion.

Before release, run an implication audit: ask a reviewer what the entire message, imagery and CTA seem to promise; compare each inference to the ledger; correct or remove unsupported ones. Then verify live logistics and link targets. For a synthetic packet, stop at the draft and label the tests unrun.

## Preserve unknown states and review actual copy

An unavailable page does not establish closed registration, a nonexistent alternate route or a cancelled offer. An absent evaluation in the supplied evidence does not establish that no evaluation ever occurred. Keep the source's scope. Clarify ambiguous counts (capacity, enrollment or attendance), deadline purpose, duration and participation requirements before consequential release. Do not convert them to a convenient meaning for a headline. Relative phrases such as “soon” or “last chance” require a current reference date and supported availability.

Inspect the actual sentences, image implications and CTA against the source, not merely a model's self-audit table. A second review can help but is not proof that all mutations were caught. “No results promised” does not repair an unsupported promise elsewhere. Comprehension probes are proposed until real participants answer; a model response is neither customer evidence nor a conversion experiment.

## Scope, release conditions and test interpretation

Reverify the actual offer at release or when its owner reports a material change. The method integration date is not an offer's freshness date. Name the verification owner for each pending material fact. If a target reader needs an unconfirmed access feature to participate, do not imply that participation is assured or ask them to commit on that premise; provide the verified status and a real way to resolve it, or defer that invitation. This does not automatically block every accurate informational draft.

Use the jurisdiction governing the actual offer for regulatory questions; the US FTC sources illustrate a scoped standard, not universal legal compliance. The strategy owner does not certify legality. A model implication review is a draft check; actual audience comprehension needs appropriately recruited human readers. There is no universal reviewer count in this method.

A claimed measured lift needs a defined comparison, outcome, denominator, uncertainty and defensible causal design. Randomized experiments are one strong design; other designs require justified assumptions and limitations, and descriptive differences do not establish causation. A desired 25% improvement may be an explicitly labelled internal target or hypothesis; it must not become “proven 25% lift.”

For provisional channels use a factual “registration deadline” rather than an actionable “register now/by” instruction when no verified route is available. A future information notice is not necessarily a notice that registration will open: do not infer closed registration from an unavailable page. State unknown current availability directly; do not assume a future page will expose a reliable live count. Keep optional preparation optional in shorter channels when omission would imply a requirement.
