# Paid GM event coordination

Turn a venue offer into one decision-ready coordination message and a clear next action. Protect player value and GM compensation while understanding what makes the event workable for the venue. Finish the current event's coordination step before expanding into outreach campaigns, marketing systems, or adventure development.

This public edition retains the complete original coordination procedure. Read [the synthetic example](references/synthetic-example.md) when a worked example helps. Its rates, conditions and dates are fictional, never defaults for a real event.

## Establish the current offer

Start from the user's message, latest venue reply, and existing event packet. Retrieve history only when it materially changes the answer. Prefer the current source document or conversation over a memory summary; distinguish the user's actions from AI suggestions visible in recordings. If sources disagree, preserve the disagreement until stronger evidence resolves it.

Extract only what this decision needs:

- Event, venue/contact, intended audience, format, capacity, and experience requirements.
- Quoted player ticket price and its unit, previous price or expected range, room charge, GM compensation, and who collects/pays each amount.
- Proposed date and time zone; room access, player session, setup, and cleanup windows.
- Preregistration minimum and deadline, approval conditions, and any cancellation or payment terms relevant to committing.
- What the venue and GM have actually agreed to provide.

Label material terms as confirmed, conditional offer, requested, assumed, or unknown, with source/date where useful. Use `[MISSING INFO: ...]` for absent facts in internal drafts. A venue's conditional offer is not mutual acceptance. Ask the user only for consequential information that cannot be resolved from available context; put venue-owned unknowns into the venue reply.

## Clarify price before accepting it

Lead by acknowledging the room or arrangement's concrete benefit. State the price concern plainly, anchored in the actual baseline or expected range. Ask what the additional cost covers without assuming the venue's motives:

- Is the room charge flat, hourly, per attendee, or a minimum spend? Is it included in the ticket or additional, and who pays it?
- Does the charge address foregone room rental, food/beverage spending, staffing, or another constraint? Phrase these as questions unless verified.
- What attendance threshold makes the arrangement viable, and by when must it be met?

Keep ticket revenue, room cost, and GM income separate. Do not treat a higher ticket price as a GM raise or mistake a per-session ticket for an hourly charge. If a calculation helps the decision, show its assumptions and use only known or explicitly hypothetical values. Gross ticket revenue is price times paid seats; GM income follows the actual pay agreement. Do not subtract an included room charge twice or infer venue profit from gross revenue. Without a cost allocation, leave net earnings unresolved.

## Resolve usable time alongside cost

Ask how long the room is actually available, including access for setup and teardown. Separate that from advertised play time. Check that the promised experience fits the usable window.

If the proposed slot may be difficult for players, ask about a specific alternative such as later Saturday afternoon/evening and whether it carries the same cost and conditions. Treat improved player availability as a hypothesis unless supported by their responses. Do not silently move the event or describe an unconfirmed morning slot as an evening event.

Preserve exact edits to dates and times across drafts. If a room is necessary to deliver the intended atmosphere or content, confirm that dependency before locking the event. A public-space fallback may change the experience; get the user's direction before adopting that change.

## Connect premium positioning to delivery and pay

If the event commands a premium, explain what attendees actually receive: suitable room, distinctive scenario, session length, physical mechanics, prepared materials, or other supported benefits. Keep promises proportional to confirmed resources. A premium label alone does not establish willingness to pay.

Ask whether the venue can contribute budget or practical support for props, take-home mementos, and advertising. Establish who supplies, pays for, and promotes each agreed item. Ask whether special-event GM compensation changes; clarify paid hours or prep coverage when relevant. Do not absorb extra production work into the baseline rate by default.

For horror or mature events, align content expectations with the venue and preserve the actual age/experience requirements. Keep spoilers and private negotiation details out of public copy. Requested extras remain proposals until agreed; they are not attendee promises or proof of past work.

## Produce the smallest useful handoff

Default to one paste-ready message in the contact's conversational tone:

1. Acknowledge the arrangement's value and explain the price question.
2. Ask the unresolved fee, attendance, access-time, and alternative-slot questions.
3. Describe the proposed premium experience and ask about venue support and GM pay.
4. Close with the specific answers needed to finalize the arrangement.

Omit questions already answered. Keep wording collaborative and direct. Consolidate revisions in one final version, checking numbers, time ranges, pronouns, and copied formatting. Do not reproduce the historical app-switching sequence when editing directly is sufficient.

When useful, add a short internal terms table and name the next owner/action. Do not create a full event packet just to send one reply. Reuse an existing packet when an accepted answer changes it. Keep the informal coordination message separate from the formal public listing.

## Authorization and closeout

Drafting and analysis do not authorize sending messages, accepting terms, booking, paying, or publishing. Follow explicit authorization already present in the conversation; do not ask again for the same authorized action. When authorization is needed, first finish the exact message or proposed commitment for review.

After an authorized send, verify the message in the intended conversation. A populated composer or Return key event alone is not delivery proof. If the result is uncertain, inspect the conversation before retrying to avoid duplicates. Report the precise state: drafted, send action observed, sent verified, venue replied, mutually confirmed, or published verified. Neither silence nor a message send establishes booking acceptance.

Before signoff, check that the requested deliverable exists, material terms have sources or explicit unknowns, conditions/deadlines survived editing, pricing units and time ranges agree, and no proposed support became a promise. For live commitments or publication, verify the current venue agreement and the resulting reservation/listing. End with what is complete and the one remaining action, if any.
