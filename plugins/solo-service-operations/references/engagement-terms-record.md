# Agreed service terms record

Store one small dated record alongside the private engagement, using existing agreements and project records. This is an administrative extraction, not a new contract or legal advice. Marketing owns proposal/pricing development; Work Management owns delivery; Solo owns comparing invoices with accepted terms. Paid-GM venue/ticketing owners retain their specialized operational terms.

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Identity and coverage | Client/entity, project, currency, effective period, source cutoff |
| Agreement evidence | Original agreement/message path or ID, date, exact section, acceptance evidence and who accepted |
| Service and exclusions | Agreed scope, deliverables, billing basis; explicitly unresolved areas |
| Price | Hourly/day/flat fee, unit/rate, cap or retainer, rounding rule; source for each |
| Payment | Issue trigger, due-date rule, installments, deposits, credits and their allocation |
| Changes | Dated accepted change, revised amount/scope/effective date; proposed changes remain separate |
| Completion/acceptance | Delivery evidence and actual acceptance conditions; do not invent them |
| Invoice state | Number, period, item IDs, prepared/approved/sent status, prior billed scope |
| Exceptions | Conflicts or missing facts, their financial effect, next verification |

Before preparing or chasing an invoice: compare every billed rate and unit, scope change, cap, deposit/credit, currency and due date against this record. Distinguish source-supported terms from user-approved estimates and assumptions. An invoice number or amount mismatch needs resolution, not automatic replacement. Approval of an invoice alone does not prove amended contract terms or paid status.

Example (fictional): accepted email T1 specifies USD 80/hour with a 10-hour cap. A 12-hour draft totals USD 960 but exceeds the USD 800 cap; flag USD 160 pending explicit change evidence. A proposed email asking for a higher cap is not acceptance. A USD 200 settled deposit allocated to this same engagement reduces payable once, not both the line subtotal and balance again.

Do not sign, renegotiate, refund, apply a late fee or change payment instructions through this record. Preserve evidence and route a requested action to its current owner.
