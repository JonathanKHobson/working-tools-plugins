# Promotional design copy

Identify the audience's decision: attend, register, visit, buy, inquire, or remember. Work from the actual offer and supplied evidence. A poster should answer the relevant decision with an ordered message; it need not contain the entire event page.

Build the smallest useful copy hierarchy:

- **Attention:** the recognizable event, meaningful offer, or specific benefit.
- **Decision:** what it is, who it suits, and facts that change participation.
- **Action:** one primary next step and a destination that fulfills it.

Change this order when the brief warrants it. Price, age restrictions, eligibility, or a deadline may belong high in the hierarchy. A brand-awareness graphic may not need a registration CTA. Preserve user-approved terminology and voice.

For events, verify which date, year, time, time zone, venue, address, cost, eligibility, and registration destination belong to this edition. Do not silently borrow facts from a similar event. If older notes conflict with a newer approved source, show the conflict and use the supported current edition.

Pair the promise with a reason to believe. An illustrative image creates atmosphere; it cannot substantiate attendance, venue endorsement, customer satisfaction, or past results. Use approved evidence where a claim needs it. Avoid invented scarcity and performance claims.

When copy is too dense, remove repetition or move secondary explanation to the destination before shrinking type. Use line breaks at meaningful phrases. A vague adjective such as “premium” needs a concrete basis if the brief allows one; do not invent amenities to justify it. Strong type cannot rescue an unclear offer.

For a printed QR call to action, name the destination or action and provide a usable text route when practical. If the visible backup is a homepage while the QR goes to a booking page, identify that distinction accurately. For mobile placements, prefer the platform's link or companion text route over a QR-only action.

Return final short copy with its proposed priority or placement, plus only the material source gaps. If asked to make the graphic, continue into composition; do not stop at a copy brief. A favorable design review is not a conversion test.
