# Video thumbnails, paired with their titles

Use for YouTube/course-video thumbnail concepts, a series visual system, or meaningful packaging variants. Marketing supplies audience intent and the truthful viewer promise; this method owns the image. Social owns native placement/testing and readback. Start from the actual title, reviewed source frames/transcript, intended surface and existing approved series identity.

## Establish the concept

State what the viewer should understand at a glance and where the video delivers it. Use an actual demonstrated interface/result, an accurate visual metaphor or authorized portrait. Show a screen demo large enough to communicate its focal action; do not counterfeit product UI, results, endorsements or before/after outcomes. Remove private learner/client details and identify asset rights. A face is optional, not a guaranteed click improvement.

Before generation, write one sentence naming the visual job for this specific viewer and action, grounded in the video's actual source. A proof crop, diagram or type-led thumbnail may communicate it better than an illustration. A meaningful metaphor should explain a relation in the lesson; generic paper, coffee, lenses or technical-looking marks do not become useful merely through polished styling. Evaluate recognition of the relevant promise/proof alongside the exact title. Do not claim SEO benefit from a generated image's style.

Choose invariants for the series: restrained type/color family, recognizable framing or marker, and level/tool cues only where useful. Vary the lesson-specific proof and focal object. Avoid placing a course number so prominently that viewers cannot understand the topic without previous episodes. Reuse the approved design rather than inventing a new visual system for each upload.

Pair image and exact title before judging either. Complementary does not mean forbidding every repeated word: a necessary tool name may remain. Use limited readable text appropriate to the composition; no universal three-word ceiling or fixed character-truncation assumption. Never invent numbers to satisfy a lint score. Let the actual mobile/feed rendering establish legibility and truncation.

When the brief asks for a polished, handmade editorial feel, explore grounded photography, print, cut-paper or collage with restrained composition and a lesson-specific idea. Avoid default glow, glossy symbols or decorative swooshes when they add no meaning. This is contextual art direction, not a ban on 3D or other styles. Preserve already approved assets; apply new direction to future work unless a revision is requested. If imagegen finals are requested, use the image tool for finals; an SVG prototype is a prototype, not a substituted final.

## Make useful alternatives

If the user requests alternatives or a test, propose genuinely different concepts—such as visible artifact, key interface action, or a truthful question—not three minor color swaps. Record variant ID, title, image premise, demonstrated proof, intended difference and hypothesis. Compare complete packages when appropriate and attribute results to that package; isolate one component only when that is the question. Do not claim a static critique predicts a winning CTR.

Create an editable source and export each chosen variant. Use the existing composition, image generation/editing and compositing owners. No extra paid thumbnail service or provider key is required by this method. Review with graphic-design-review: keep the focal subject, silhouette, contrast, text and accurate promise legible at realistic feed/mobile scale. Check overlays and crops for the intended placement; a full-resolution view is insufficient.

For generated physical scenes, apply [object consistency](../../graphic-asset-compositing/references/spatial-contact.md#generated-object-consistency), including magnification of the actual underlying content, reflections, contact/shadows and perspective. Inspect the final image at phone size and detail scale before release. Reject an incoherent focal object even if the palette, texture and typography are attractive. Route actual image edits through `imagegen` when available; a request for future improvement leaves published and scheduled artwork intact.

## Verify actual outputs

For visual QA, record 3–8 anchors (source/export, viewport/region, observed fact, intended change), then inspect changed anchors in rendered exports. Compare alongside the paired title at feed scale and one larger viewing size. Mechanical dimensions/filesize checks cannot replace that visual review. A long-form thumbnail adapted to a Short needs recomposition and its own review.

For a Shorts cover, verify the saved native portrait preview as well as the source export. Upload acceptance does not prove that the complete composition is visible: a desktop Studio workflow observed on 2026-10-01 accepted landscape artwork but center-cropped it to 9:16, cutting off a left-side headline, with no Fit/Adjust control in that workflow. Recheck current account controls rather than assuming this is universal. If the saved crop clips important content, preserve the approved 16:9 original and derive a faithful 9:16 composition with the same headline, imagery and palette; reposition the headline and focal image to remain readable. Use the requested image-editing provider for final assets. Do not solve the crop by adding oversized empty letterboxing or silently redesigning approved art. Inspect the actual saved Short preview after upload and save, checking full headline, focal subject and crop/overlay clearance; Social owns the native readback receipt.

Platform facts checked 2026-10-01: YouTube recommends 16:9 video and 9:16 Shorts thumbnails, with large source images; its computer guide specifies 3840×2160 and 2160×3840 respectively and a desktop limit of 50 MB, with different mobile limits. Refresh [official upload requirements](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72431?hl=en) and verify the actual account/UI before export or claiming upload support; older 1280×720/<2 MB guidance is not universal. Do not infer a saved or visible thumbnail from creating the file.

For native experiments, prepare consistent resolution and meaningful variants, then hand off to Social. Current guidance permits up to three variants and ranks by watch time, not CTR alone; a source image below 720p can reduce all experiment image resolution. See [official A/B guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en). No native test is launched by this design procedure.

Deliver: content ID/version | variant ID | exact title | source/proof frame | editable path | export path/hash/dimensions | rendered-QA result | outstanding rights/placement issues | prepared/uploaded/readback/test state. Marketing interprets measured results and Video resolves any promise-to-content mismatch.

## Provenance and adaptation

Pairing title and thumbnail and checking duplicated messaging draw on Jake Schincariol's MIT yt-package method at a2feb2104981a375ffd4f87ee04f4f5344ac43c6. The complete original method/helper and license are retained in `libraries/youtube-packaging-source/` for inspection only. The helper is not an active grading tool: fixed truncation, word-count, number and score rules are unvalidated heuristics. This local procedure replaces those rules, does not create ~/.claude/youtube/voice.md, and uses current task authorization instead of the upstream mandatory approval question. Do not execute a preserved helper merely because an original example shows it.

Acceptance: an honest course-demo thumbnail remains legible at mobile scale, complements its title, preserves the series identity, and ties its promise to a real demonstrated result. Three variants reveal different plausible concepts without invented success claims.
