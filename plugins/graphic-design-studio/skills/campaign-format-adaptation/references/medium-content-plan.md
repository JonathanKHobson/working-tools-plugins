# Recompose the content for the placement

Use when one approved graphic or factual source must serve a different medium.
Keep the existing brief and `DESIGN.md`; add only the missing medium decisions.
The UX Design Studio design-systems owner maintains the shared reader/content
contract. This reference applies it to graphic composition and handoffs without
introducing a second specification format.

For each requested output, record the reader and job, required content, material
to omit or relocate, reading priority, a justified region budget and an observable
check. Keep exact facts, claim qualifications, evidence and source identity
traceable. Budgets below are illustrative decisions for this example, not
platform specifications or rules for every poster.

## Worked example: a fictional workshop

The approved example facts are: “Make a simple paper circuit. An introductory,
90-minute guided workshop. Saturday, November 14, 2:00 to 3:30 p.m. at Sample
Studio. Ages 16 and up. $20, materials included. Reserve a seat.” The identity
uses an existing type system and approved circuit photograph. `example.invalid`
is a reserved example domain, not a working registration destination. A real
job must supply and verify its actual source, artwork rights and destination.

The visual concept is the connection between a drawn path and a lit paper
object. Keep that meaning when recomposing; do not replace it with a generic
technology illustration merely to fit a new frame.

| Output and reader job | Include | Omit or relocate | Priority and budget | Check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Printed poster: decide whether to attend and take away a destination | Workshop name, lit object, date/time, place, age limit, cost/materials, reservation route | Detailed steps belong at registration | Object and name first; one facts group and one action area; size type for actual viewing distance | Inspect final-size proof, frame opening, exact facts and verified URL/QR; phone screen alone does not prove distance readability |
| Square feed image: recognize the activity and open details | Name, lit object, “90-minute guided workshop”, $20/materials and ages 16+ | Date/time/place and full reservation wording can move to verified companion copy when that copy is delivered | Close crop of object plus short title and compact eligibility/cost line; avoid a miniature poster | Read paired image and companion copy at displayed feed size; facts and action remain available together |
| Portrait story: decide and follow a mobile action | Name, object, day/time, age/cost/materials and action cue | Full location or long directions can move to destination; retain enough location to decide | Object across upper region, facts below, action clear of verified overlays; split into two frames if readable facts cannot fit | Inspect actual story crop and overlay-safe area; same-phone reader has a usable link route |
| Web event page: understand fit and reserve | Complete event facts, accessibility/contact details when supplied, source/registration link | Poster-only decoration or scan-dependent QR | Identity and activity first, facts second, reservation control third; page can grow | UX/provider checks narrow layout, accessible action, focus and destination; Graphic supplies the crop and identity |
| Briefing slide: explain what participants will do | Activity statement, object/process relationship, “introductory, guided, ages 16+” when relevant to the claim | Booking logistics move to a companion or closing action slide | One explanatory relationship and one concise statement; split process details instead of shrinking labels | Read at actual presentation size; inspect export and qualification; native slide owner retains production |

The feed image's short copy can read “Make a simple paper circuit” and
“90-minute guided workshop · Ages 16+ · $20, materials included”. Its paired
caption carries the exact date, time, place and working reservation route.
Record the delivered caption and its intended placement; saying “in caption”
without supplying one leaves the output incomplete. In an image-only handoff,
restore decisive missing facts or state that a required companion is pending.

The poster can use a broad photograph, a separate information column and a
printed destination. The square output needs a closer crop that keeps the lit
object and connection visible; move the supporting line into a quiet reading
field, and move logistics to companion copy only when the combined output is
available. The portrait output changes the relationship again: use vertical
space for the object, an ordered facts group and the mobile action. Changing
only canvas dimensions, colors or font size does not perform these decisions.

Do not strip “introductory” and “guided” to claim that participants will master
electronics. Do not replace “$20, materials included” with “free materials” or
hide the age requirement in an unavailable link. Shorter copy must retain the
original promise's meaning. For research-led graphics, apply the same rule to
sample scope, uncertainty and evidence. Required qualification stays with the
claim even if fuller methodology lives elsewhere.

## Review the complete delivered edition

Use Graphic Design Review's existing anchor map, with source and target shown
at their actual viewing sizes. Check focal meaning, facts, type hierarchy and
action route separately. Inspect expanded or supplementary content when the
edition depends on it. Confirm assets and fonts are loaded or that an approved
fallback is explicitly named; an empty image box can make a layout fit while
removing the reason to look.

Use the following paired cases for a new or changed adaptation rule. These are
review prompts, not a claim that an automated evaluator has executed them.
Keep actual results and renders in the owning project's review record.

| Valid edition | Deliberately failing edition | Why the failure matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Square graphic uses an intentional object crop, short message and complete supplied caption | Entire poster is reduced to a square thumbnail | Critical type and action become unreadable even though dimensions are correct |
| Compact image and supplied companion retain age, price and guided/introductory scope | Short version drops age limit or changes the learning promise | Fit has changed eligibility or meaning |
| Story has a mobile link action, with exact destination verified by delivery owner | Story uses only a scan code on the same phone | Reader lacks a practical next step in the tested context |
| Actual photograph and required font are present at export | Missing photograph/font makes the layout smaller and is counted as passing | Acceptance preconditions are absent; fit cannot stand in for the intended edition |
| Each crop preserves the visible connection and lit object | New crop hides the connection and leaves unrelated texture | The shared identity survives, but the explanatory concept has been lost |

For a small factual correction, reuse existing review evidence and recheck the
changed anchor. Do not manufacture extra variants or require a campaign system
when the user requested one output. Graphic Design owns visual composition and
export; UX owns interactive behavior, the slide/document owner owns native
production, and the requested provider owns publishing. A prepared companion
or local export is not proof of publication or platform acceptance.
