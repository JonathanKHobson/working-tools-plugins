# Temporal composition and research intake

Use this guide when a brief needs a motion concept, kinetic type, styleframes, or a visual review across time. The production owner determines the renderer and final timing.

## Visual decisions across beats

- Give each beat a communication job. Mark what appears, what changes, and what the viewer should understand before the next beat.
- Explore continuity through a recurring visual element when it helps. A shape, crop, image or type element can change role across beats; make the relationship recognizable. This is one available mechanism, not a required motif.
- Set an explicit readable hold for consequential text or visual payoff. Test the hold at playback speed and final display size; an isolated frame can conceal a too-short reading window.
- Plan type hierarchy across time as well as within a frame. Stagger competing messages when simultaneous reveals would compete, and preserve exact supplied wording.
- Check the transitions between selected frames, including occlusion, crop, alignment and abrupt changes in scale or visual weight. A polished opening and ending can still fail in between.
- Note sound cue opportunities alongside visual beats for Video Production to assess against the actual soundtrack or narration. Do not infer audio success from visual frames.

The AI Workshop Studio channel intro is a bounded example of a persistent visual object changing role across semantic beats, explicit holds, type hierarchy and sound cues. Its palette, typeface and subject are project choices, not reusable rules. The source is at `an external private source project (not bundled)`; that case alone does not validate UX interaction behavior or narration quality.

## Future research intake

Keep future motion references here when they change a visual decision above. For each candidate, record the precise source and access date, the observed mechanism, the context where it applies, a credible counterexample or limit, and the artifact or playback check that would test it. Prefer primary practitioner documentation, documented examples or direct frame review. Do not promote a single channel intro, trend, palette or tool choice into a universal rule. Add only findings that alter this skill's visual judgment; renderer, audio and UI-state findings belong to their existing owners.
