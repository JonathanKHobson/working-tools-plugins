## DC2 — Editorial public question

**Prompt:** A large program needs an inviting frame and a scannable, compact account of what's offered. **Audience relationship:** curious neighbor reading a local broadside. **Hierarchy:** a genuine question as hook, full logistics, then course evidence. **Type:** display serif for the question; compact sans labels; durable body type for lists. **Image/material:** type and rules become the image; print can use inexpensive one- or two-color production. **Rhythm/density:** broad opening followed by tightly aligned lists and a single invitation interruption. **Color:** accent marks the audience action, not every item. **Motion:** none.

**Precedent/limit:** The Taller de Gráfica Popular made reproducible, street-facing prints in a specific labor and political context [S06]. Its collective politics are not a generic vintage texture. Corita Kent and her students developed teaching rules collaboratively [S07]; that is a process model, not permission to quote the whole poster. **Example:** `examples/direction-b-street-broadside.html`. **Counterexample:** a safety alert or live financial control panel where a rhetorical question delays the urgent state/action. **Transfer:** an editorial dashboard introduction may use a short framing question and sectioned evidence, while metrics keep units, source, timestamp and priority. **Rights:** no borrowed graphics or lettering; original text and system fonts. **Test:** can a passerby find time/place/free condition at viewing distance, then name a suitable workshop?
