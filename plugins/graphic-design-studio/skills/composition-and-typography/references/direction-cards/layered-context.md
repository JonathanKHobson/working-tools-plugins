# Conditional art-direction card

Integrated 2026-09-30 as original transfer guidance. Read the full card for its task. These are contextual mechanisms, not named-artist presets or evidence of a universal aesthetic advantage. Historical interpretation, rights and user-test limits travel with the method.

## DC5 — Layered context, explicit return

**Prompt:** The same information supports a quick overview and deeper examination. **Audience relationship:** explorer who controls depth. **Hierarchy:** stable overview, selectable detail, visible way back. **Type:** expressive scale only at overview; all detail remains legible and navigable. **Image/material:** layering and opacity can show relationship, but hidden text requires a real control and accessible alternative. **Rhythm/density:** keep simultaneous layers limited by task; avoid depth for spectacle. **Color/motion:** motion communicates transition, honors reduced-motion settings, and never replaces labels.

**Precedent/limit:** Accounts of Muriel Cooper's *Information Landscapes* describe spatial typography and viewpoint-dependent hierarchy [S10]. Its original software has partial surviving documentation, so no direct usability conclusion follows. **Example:** an infographic shows enrollment stages as a stable overview, with optional detail on one stage and persistent comparison context. **Counterexample:** perspective-distorted KPI labels that prevent accurate comparison. **Transfer:** suitable for an interactive explainer with user-controlled detail, generally unsuitable for real-time dashboards unless flat data tables and stable units remain available. **Rights:** transfer the question of controlled depth, not a reproduction of the 1994 interface. **Test:** find a detail, compare two items, return to overview, and repeat with keyboard/reduced motion.
