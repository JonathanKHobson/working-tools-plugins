# choose an art direction by reader task

Use only for a new direction or a repeated generic-output problem. This extends the current composition decision guide; do not require it for a small approved-artwork repair.

1. Write a one-sentence decision: “This reader must identify ___, decide ___, and then ___, in ___ context.” Separate essential facts from atmosphere. Copy exact event/price/eligibility facts into a source-of-truth list before layout.
2. Name the relationship: **receiver** (understand a message), **navigator** (find a choice), **comparator** (weigh options), or **contributor** (add meaning). A contributor direction requires an actual intake, moderation, consent and response path. Without that process, treat the invitation as a proposed activity, not a collaborative outcome.
3. Name the physical or interface context: wall at a distance, handheld flyer, editorial spread, widget, or live dashboard. Record brand, language, accessibility, print, rights and production constraints. Distinguish observed source facts from desired effects.
4. Sketch two routes that change sequence or participation, not just skin: e.g. question → offer → logistics versus time → options → next action. For each, specify lead element, secondary scan, stable fact area, expressive type role, information type role, image/material role, rhythm/density and one color function. Reject any route that obscures the decision condition.
5. Choose the route with the clearest fit. For an existing identity, keep its approved marks, palette/type rules and source shell unless the brief authorizes changes. Variety can come from grouping, scale, spatial relationship, evidence order, crop or audience agency inside the identity.
6. Test the exact artifact: first glance, intended distance/size, detail, and required facts; perform anchor-based rendered review. In functional interfaces, separately verify labels, units, status, keyboard/focus, state and real interaction. An attractive poster precedent cannot justify distorted data labels.

**Example:** For a fictional learning-commons open house, a contribution route can make blank workshop spaces central if people can submit ideas on site. A day-route can instead help someone with only Thursdays available. Same facts; different reader task. **Counterexample:** a critical-incident dashboard should foreground severity and response ownership, even if an equal grid looks harmonious.

**Source boundary:** Historical precedents are evidence about past work, not a palette or motif library. Record creator, work, context, production method, reader relationship, rights, and what is inferred before adapting. Keep quotes, images and community contributions under their actual permissions.

## Preconditions for the selected action

The six-step procedure above is the authoritative selection sequence; the original five-step sequence was consolidated because its task, precondition, same-fact, identity and test requirements are retained here and in the complete cards. A navigator needs a real decision/action path just as a contributor needs a real intake path. Verify the offered options, eligibility, selection/booking mechanism and confirmation state; a schedule alone does not establish reservations or availability. Interactive layering needs functioning controls and an accessible return path. If a prerequisite is missing, keep it explicit in a private draft and choose a simpler truthful route for any authorized public output.
