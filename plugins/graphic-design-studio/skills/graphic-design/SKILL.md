# Graphic design

For a capabilities or first-use question, consult the [human guide](../../README.md) and identify the relevant graphic, source inputs, and deliverable. When a graphic is already requested, work from the brief below without repeating onboarding or claiming a remembered tour.

Deliver the requested graphic in a usable format. Adapt the depth of planning to the job: a small revision should stay a small revision. Do not turn an urgent flyer into a branding project, a content calendar, or a research program.

## Establish the design problem

Inspect supplied artwork and the current edition before replacing anything. Infer from context the audience, intended action, viewing conditions, dimensions, required facts, source assets, brand constraints, and output format. Ask only for a missing fact that materially changes the result; continue independent design work while it is unresolved. Keep unknown facts in working notes or marked proof copy, never disguised as confirmed event details.

Describe the visual direction in one useful sentence: e.g., “A lively neighborhood workshop poster with oversized type and a clear booking block.” Name what should attract attention first and what the viewer should understand next. The user’s chosen reference or approved version has priority over a fresh aesthetic.

Before generating imagery, state its communication job in one sentence: what this audience should understand, recognize, or decide, and which source-specific visual evidence will carry that meaning. Choose an actual artifact, proof crop, clear diagram, meaningful metaphor, or type-led composition to suit that job. An image is optional. A professional-looking prop collage that could accompany any topic is insufficient when the brief needs explanation or a specific promise. Marketing usefulness can mean helping the right viewer recognize a relevant promise or proof; an image's style does not establish SEO value.

Meaning and visual craft are separate requirements. Do not repair an incoherent illustration by defaulting to paragraphs inside colored cards: an accurate miniature slide can still fail as an article image or thumbnail. Let a recognizable subject, visual contrast, transformation or relationship carry the idea; use words to sharpen it. Design a deliberate focal point, scale contrast, rhythm and color roles appropriate to the subject. Clear does not mean bland, and expressive does not mean decorative. When the job is an explanatory comparison or mechanism, consult Data Visualization Toolkit's `data-visualization` route and full strategy-and-critique procedure; keep Graphic Design responsible for the final composition, and do not manufacture numerical data or a chart merely to invoke that toolkit.

For a graphic made from web/SVG or other inspectable source when a measurable palette, font, or component comparison would resolve a real design question, use UX Design Studio's [conditional native runtime](https://github.com/JonathanKHobson/working-tools-plugins/blob/main/plugins/ux-design-toolkit/skills/design-studio/references/native-design-runtime.md) through its owned adapter. Graphic Design Studio still decides composition and export; do not install a second binary or hooks here, and do not use a web detector result as proof of print quality.

For a new direction, quick thumbnails can resolve genuinely different arrangements before polishing one. Do not manufacture alternatives after the user has chosen. Keep exact event facts, prices, logos, and QR payloads under direct control; a generated preview is not an editable master or a verified export.

When repeated aesthetic revisions fail, reconsider the construction method or give a capable worker a bounded request to produce an actual alternative artifact; another critique alone may not resolve the problem. Choose the method for the image's job while following current host image-tool requirements. Once the user accepts a specific version with known tradeoffs, preserve that version and record those limits; do not restart redesign merely because a later pass can imagine more polish.

Choose a tool suited to the deliverable and the user's workflow. Native design files, Figma, editable SVG, or reproducible page-layout source are valid. For the selected reconstruction, sticker or meme routes, the host's built-in image capability can execute edits when available; the bundled Pillow helper handles bounded local raster composition. A strictly offline request uses the helper's supported modes and requires alpha or a supplied mask for a subject cutout. Do not require a separate service, app or key solely to use these routes.

When choosing a repeatable image approach or preparing an editable generation prompt would help, read [guided image workflows](references/image-workflows.md). The shared library plans the handoff when available on this host; official `imagegen` remains the execution owner. For an existing editable SVG, vector icon, or logo system, work in the source instead.

Translate the chosen concept into the generation brief: meaningful focal relation, object geometry, materials/light, crop and type hierarchy. For depicted physical objects, use the [spatial consistency checks](../graphic-asset-compositing/references/spatial-contact.md#generated-object-consistency); polish and texture cannot excuse an impossible relation. Route built-in image edits through `imagegen` when available, following its reference-image instructions; keep exact wording and real screen evidence under direct control. Preserve approved assets and apply new art direction to future work unless the user requests revisions to existing outputs.

## Finish

Keep a concise `DESIGN.md` with the editable design or template skill. Capture its actual type roles, colors, composition, dimensions, assets, adaptation boundaries and output checks; link it from template `SKILL.md` and include its support files in downloads. Read [design specification handoff](references/design-specification-handoff.md) when creating or updating this foundation. Reuse the established design instead of inventing a brand kit; a small revision updates the existing document.

Inspect the final render with [graphic-design-review](../graphic-design-review/SKILL.md), then apply the relevant export checks. Return the artifact, a brief explanation of consequential design choices, and exact remaining limitations. Distinguish created, visually reviewed, mechanically checked, approved, published, and printed. Existing authorization governs external actions; this skill adds none.

For a deeper explanation or uncertain design principle, consult [research](references/research.md). Read its linked primary source when exact technical or current placement requirements matter. The research contains design heuristics and professional guidance, not evidence that a composition will increase sales.

## Complete image-led production

For an image-led graphic or coordinated visual artifact, **read and apply the complete [Visual Production Workflow](../../libraries/visual-production-workflow/SKILL.md)**, including its eight production steps, asset provenance, immutable approvals, aspect-ratio recomposition, final-size QA and handoff refinements. This is the shared production method inside Graphic Design Studio, not a second standalone discovery skill. UX Design uses it for product imagery; Video Production uses it for still assets and boards while retaining motion/audio ownership.

For Superdesign canvas work, read the complete [AI Graphic Design procedure](../../libraries/ai-graphic-design/skills/ai-graphic-design/SKILL.md), then its full selected graphic, slides or moodboard reference and CLI contract. Preserve reference/content/brand roles, exact copy, canvas sequencing and iteration decisions. The original package is intact; it adds no registered MCP/hook. Superdesign is an optional external CLI/account/credit service. Before first execution or a version change, inspect the actual package/version and its auth/upload/cost scope with Master Security; do not execute the source's unpinned `npx --yes ...@latest` as an automatic merge/install step. Use current authorized account/tool access, and preserve authorization already given. Never invent canvas links, project IDs or completed exports.

When a named designer, movement or community precedent materially guides the direction, use the complete [precedent-transfer check](references/design-sources.md). Preserve cultural context, authorship, rights and actual participation conditions; adapt mechanisms into original work.

For external visual inspiration or deck composition, read [gallery-to-original-design](references/gallery-to-original-design.md). It enables bounded Godly/Deck.gallery research and an actionable reference packet for builders; it does not replace the approved design or native artifact provider.
