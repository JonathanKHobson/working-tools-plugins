# Source to visual explanation

Use when an article, source, research brief or authored argument needs a visual, deck or video companion. This is a conditional handoff among existing owners, not a requirement to produce every format. Begin with the user's requested output; when the format is open, choose the smallest format that explains the subject well.

## Preserve the source before choosing the medium

Keep a compact brief alongside the existing project artifact: source link or local path and revision/date; intended audience and takeaway; approved wording and identity; source-supported claims with page/section/record anchors; uncertainty and exclusions; rights or attribution requirements; target placement and requested format. For an authored article, freeze the version being adapted and distinguish its argument from independent evidence. A source's factual error must not become a more persuasive visual error.

Reuse an existing brief rather than adding a new tracker. Read enough of the actual source to verify selected claims; do not turn an abstract, title or search snippet into a whole-article summary. Keep measured, inferred, illustrative and unknown information distinct. If a needed claim is unresolved, resolve it or label/omit it within the authorized scope.

## Choose by the communication job

| Communication job | Existing production owner | Useful output and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A memorable subject, metaphor or article image | Graphic Design; official imagegen for image generation/editing | Image plus generation/editing brief; pixels do not contain independently editable text or shapes. Keep exact facts and typography under direct control. |
| Relationships, a mechanism or process | Data Visualization Toolkit structural diagrams; Creative Production Draw and Think when its current tools fit | Evidence-linked diagram, SVG or saved canvas. Verify save/export capability before promising a portable editable canvas; fall back to a supported source format. |
| Quantitative comparison, distribution or calendar | Data Visualization Toolkit | Source data, explicit transformations and accessible chart/HTML. Never invent numbers to justify a chart. |
| A composed infographic or static explanation | Graphic Design infographic-design | Editable SVG/design source when supported, plus inspected image/PDF export and alt text. |
| Exploration, toggles or a navigable explanation | Data Visualization interactive-explanations; UX owner for product/site interaction | Self-contained HTML when appropriate; retain code and data. Name network/runtime dependencies and offline limits. |
| A sequenced briefing for a meeting or asynchronous reading | Graphic Design for composition plus the available native presentation provider | Editable native presentation and requested export; inspect actual slides and report object-level editability. |
| A change over time, demonstration, narrative or personal voice | Video Production for storyboard, timing, audio and assembly; Graphic motion-design for visual direction | Motion graphic, narrated video or mixed footage. Preserve project/timeline/source assets plus the rendered video, captions and transcript as appropriate. |

Use one primary format; add a companion only when requested or clearly useful to the established deliverable. An attractive animation is not a reason to animate a claim whose meaning is clearer as a still diagram. Conversely, preserve motion when sequence or transformation is the explanation. Do not install a new renderer, service or skill solely because the source directory recommends it.

## Build and check

1. Adapt the source into a short visual argument or beat outline. Each factual label, chart or narrated claim retains its evidence anchor. Preserve caveats that change the conclusion.
2. Load the selected owner's complete production procedure. Hand off the brief, approved assets, dimensions/placement, evidence links, expected editable master and acceptance checks. Do not duplicate that owner's implementation pipeline here.
3. Check the final artifact against the source: no implied causation, misleading visual scale, invented quotes or unsupported before/after. For personal voice, use authorized voice/likeness material; an authored article does not itself authorize voice cloning.
4. Perform rendered review at the actual destination size. Check 3–8 source/viewport anchors, hierarchy, labels, contrast, clipping, keyboard behavior where interactive, and motion/readability where timed. Include meaningful alt text or a text equivalent; video normally needs captions and a transcript, with relevant visual meaning conveyed in accompanying text/audio.
5. Use Work Management's [editability handoff](https://github.com/JonathanKHobson/working-tools-plugins/blob/main/plugins/work-management/skills/work-handoff/references/editability-handoff.md). Deliver the requested output with its real editable source and limitations; distinguish rendered, reviewed, uploaded and published.

## Article and Substack companions

For the user's own articles, Social Content owns editorial placement and platform delivery; Video Production owns an optional motion/video companion. Start from the approved article version and audience. Choose a useful explanatory excerpt or visual argument rather than narrating every paragraph. Preserve the article's link, meaning and caveats; personal presentation, voice and branding follow the approved brief. A reusable method update does not authorize creating a content queue or publishing. Verify destination format/upload behavior at production time, and report prepared, uploaded and published separately.

This original method was informed by the public capabilities of Skillry's article-brief, visual-deck and explainer listings. No third-party implementation is required.
