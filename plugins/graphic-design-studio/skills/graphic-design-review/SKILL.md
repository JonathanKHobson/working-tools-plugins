# Graphic design review

Review the current rendered artifact. Establish audience, intended action, dimensions, viewing context, and visual source. If only source code or a description is available, limit the review accordingly and do not claim visual approval.

Before visual claims, create a compact anchor map with three to eight useful regions:

`ID | source + viewport/page | normalized region or element | observation | intended action | verification`

Keep anchors in review notes, not on the finished graphic. Distinguish what is visible from hypotheses about why it happened. Reference anchors in findings and recheck changed anchors after export.

Before judging fit or calling an export ready, confirm the expected edition,
required images/fonts and any companion copy are actually present. Name an
approved fallback separately. Missing assets or an unopened required detail
cannot count as a successful test of the intended composition. For a medium
adaptation, use [the paired review cases](../campaign-format-adaptation/references/medium-content-plan.md#review-the-complete-delivered-edition) and check the source's decisive facts, eligibility and claim qualifications against the full delivered edition. Test only relevant cases and record what was observed.

## Review lenses

- **Message:** Can the audience identify the offer and next step? Are decisive facts findable and accurate?
- **Meaning:** Does the image perform its stated communication job for this audience and action? Trace the focal symbol or metaphor to the actual subject, source or demonstrated result. Reject semantically nonsensical decoration even when its palette and finish look professional; choose a clearer proof crop, diagram or type-led concept when appropriate.
- **Hierarchy:** Does one element lead? Do text, subjects, contrast, and empty space cooperate?
- **Visual explanation:** Does the image carry its idea through recognizable forms and relationships, or does the reader have to read most of a paragraph first? Inspect a thumbnail and briefly ignore secondary copy: the focal subject and contrast should remain understandable. This is an editorial diagnostic, not a measured audience scan-time claim. Reject a text-card replacement when the intended job requires a visual hook or explanation.
- **Mark semantics:** Check what filled, dashed, faded, revealed and hidden areas imply. These choices must agree with the claim's actual known, unknown, inspected or hypothetical state; an attractive reveal must not make unobserved content look established.
- **Composition:** Is balance intentional? Do crop, scale, alignment, overlap, and directional gestures serve the reading route?
- **Typography:** Are roles distinct, actual words legible, breaks meaningful, and spacing deliberate?
- **Integration:** Does text have a reading field? Do masks, perspective, edges, and contact support the intended visual relation?
- **Object consistency:** For physical scenes, inspect [optics and object relations](../graphic-asset-compositing/references/spatial-contact.md#generated-object-consistency): magnified content, reflections, grip/contact, receiving shadows, perspective and paper/object continuity. Distinguish a deliberately coherent surreal/collage premise from an accidental contradiction.
- **Taste:** Does the result fit its actual subject and reference? Challenge repeated icon tiles, badges or decorative statistics only when they obscure the message or imitate a template without a purpose. Preserve approved art direction and meaningful repetition; avoid arbitrary bans on fonts, colors, grids, or effects.
- **Output:** Are required assets present, margins viable, codes clear, and the export faithful to source?
- **Interest:** Is there a purposeful, source-specific reason to look: a revealing crop, decisive scale contrast, unexpected but coherent relation, or expressive composition? Neutral colors and fewer words do not by themselves establish good design. Check both perceptual clarity and visual interest; do not use one to excuse the absence of the other.

Inspect the whole composition, intended viewing size, and relevant details. For digital variants, test the actual displayed scale and crop. For print, screen inspection is a proof review; physical readability, stock, ink, glare, and framing remain untested unless examined physically.

For a brand guide or source-derived identity kit, apply the separate fidelity,
craft and delivery checks in [brand-guide production](../graphic-design/references/brand-guide-production.md).
Compare against the actual source design as well as the guide itself; a polished
overview cannot establish that its palette, typography and applications describe
the retained design correctly.

For generated graphics and thumbnails, inspect the actual final image before release at phone/feed size and at a detail scale. Judge meaning and hierarchy together with the paired title or surrounding copy; verify that the focal evidence survives shrinking and cropping. If no final render was inspected, report that limit instead of calling the image visually accepted. Keep approved/published/scheduled assets intact when the instruction is forward only.

Writing Quality Studio's `natural-prose-editor` and its conditional No AI Slop method review wording, specificity and editorial patterns when copy needs that work; they do not certify image geometry or authorship. UX Design Studio can help resolve meaning, attention and reading order. Do not substitute a generic style-detector score for either craft judgment or claim a full specialist review from an isolated check.

Prioritize the few changes with the strongest effect. Explain each as observed issue → consequence → specific correction. A local mask repair should not become a full redesign. Preserve what already works and the user's latest direction. For creation or edit requests, perform authorized fixes, export, and inspect again.

Report a contextual verdict: ready for review, needs targeted correction, or needs a different direction. Pair it with named evidence and limits. Mechanical checks cannot certify taste, user approval, rights, accessibility as a whole, or conversion performance. Do not report fabricated audience tests, scores, or scan times as measured evidence.

When teaching, show why the changed relationship helps and one situation where the same move would be inappropriate. This makes the skill reusable without accumulating universal rules from a single flyer.
