# Analyze Video Content: Design and Accessibility

## Temporal design

A video is not a sequence of isolated posters: hierarchy changes over time. Define what viewers should notice first, what arrives next, how long it remains readable, and how the eye is guided into the following shot.

## Composition

- Preserve a dominant focal point and meaningful negative space.
- Use explicit subject anchors for responsive crops.
- Recompose across aspect ratios instead of merely scaling.
- Review at native target size and a small/mobile approximation.
- Make local contrast robust across changing frames with scrims, boxes, outlines, shadow, or tracked backgrounds.

## Typography and captions

- Measure text and line breaks at target resolution.
- Use caption sidecars whenever supported, even when a burn-in variant is needed.
- Include meaningful non-speech audio in captions when required for understanding.
- Allow localization expansion and right-to-left layout where relevant.
- Keep captions visually above or after overlays in the composite order.

## Motion

Motion should explain relation, direct attention, reinforce rhythm, or create deliberate emotion. Use reduced-motion alternatives when rapid movement, flashing, or constant parallax could impair access. Avoid simultaneous independent reveals for information-heavy content.

## Review questions

Can a viewer understand the message muted? Can they understand dialogue without vision through captions/transcript? Does essential information survive common UI overlays? Is any color-only distinction supplemented? Does the pacing allow the intended audience to read and process the content?
