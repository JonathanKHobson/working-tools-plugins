# Pacing, focus, color and sound across video formats

Use when a brief combines pause cleanup, reframing, a visual look, B-roll and caption/motion treatments. Interpret a supplied example as editorial intent, not a mandatory effect list. Select techniques for the audience, format and source material. The same brief can concern a talking head, screencast, object demonstration, narrated slides or a silent graphic sequence.

## Tighten around meaning

Separate an accidental wait or abandoned start from useful comprehension time, a breath, a demonstration completing, suspense or an intentional reaction. Keep the cause/action/result legible. Edit the smallest meaningful beat, preserve qualifications and inspect audio/visual boundaries; silence detection proposes candidates, not automatic deletions. Recompute captions from the final output timeline after cuts, joins or speed changes. For silent footage, pace by visible action and reading time instead of manufacturing a dialogue rhythm.

Plan a punch-in or return around a shift of attention: orient, focus on the relevant element, allow it to register, then restore context when useful. The target may be a control, product feature, graph or face. Check resolution headroom, crop boundaries, movement, caption collisions and the viewer's ability to follow the action. Avoid arbitrary zoom loops or enlarging pixels beyond usable detail. A video zoom is not enabled by the native image-only motion setting; author it through a verified editor/compositor and pre-render if necessary.

## Correct before applying a look

Inspect source color metadata and the actual image; establish an appropriate exposure/white-balance baseline, then match adjacent shots before adding an intentional look. Warmth is an option when requested, not a plugin default. Preserve neutrals, skin where present, known product/brand colors and information-bearing UI colors. Protect highlight/shadow detail and inspect the strongest correction across the shot, not one attractive still. Use available scopes and a relevant reference when accuracy matters, without pretending a single skin-tone target fits every person or lighting condition.

For mixed screen capture and camera footage, avoid a blanket grade that changes the meaning of interface status colors or unreadably tints white backgrounds. Match the transition perceptually while retaining the source's truth. Any LUT/grade must run in an actually supported tool; metadata normalization alone does not apply a creative grade. Color QA remains unverified until the rendered result is inspected.

## Maintain audio continuity beneath picture changes

Place B-roll around the spoken explanation while the intended voice track continues on the output timeline. Decide explicitly whether B-roll source sound is absent, supporting ambience or necessary evidence; do not accidentally double voices or bring back muted source audio. Cut back when the original view adds useful context. Use the existing J/L-cut and room-tone methods to avoid clicks and abrupt changes.

Clean objectionable noise while preserving consonants, breaths and natural tone; denoising that sounds metallic or gates word endings is a failed repair. Balance narration before music. Use dialogue-driven automation/ducking with appropriate recovery through pauses; a gap in speech need not cause a sudden music surge. A reveal sound should support the visual event and remain subordinate to speech, not imply an unperformed UI action. Preserve music/SFX rights and separate stems. Measurements supplement listening; an explicit quiet test retains its unlistened limitation.

Use [expressive captions](../../create-video-typography/references/expressive-captions.md) for emphasis and reversible style changes; use [tracked compositing](../../create-motion-graphics/references/tracked-layer-compositing.md) for masks, layers, labels and selective blur. Not every project needs either. Work in existing briefs, EDLs, slot specs and audio maps; do not create a new manifest vocabulary or a mandatory planning packet for a small edit.
