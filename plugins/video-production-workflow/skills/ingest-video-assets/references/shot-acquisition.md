# Acquire footage for a specific shot

Use when a storyboard has an asset gap. Start from one concrete shot and the existing rights ledger; do not download a broad corpus just because a source offers one.

1. Define what the shot must communicate, visible action, subject, setting/era, target duration, framing, movement and whether it is factual evidence or illustrative atmosphere. List hard exclusions such as identifiable students, brand confusion or anachronistic equipment.
2. Inspect owned project media first. Then search appropriate item-level stock or archive sources with a few concrete queries. Keep query variants tied to the missing subject/action rather than a vague mood alone. A generation route is an explicit alternative when acquisition fails, not an automatic fallback.
3. Compare a small set of candidates. Record source page and item ID, creator, rights statement and verification date, attribution/release requirements, usable time range, crop tolerance, required action and reason accepted/rejected. A public-domain collection label or government hostname does not establish rights for every item, embedded soundtrack, logo or identifiable person's likeness.
4. View the actual candidate interval before selecting it. A thumbnail or semantic similarity score cannot prove motion, identity, historical context, continuity or rights. Check that the meaningful action remains visible after the intended crop and that the hold is long enough to understand it.
5. Acquire only the chosen permitted asset through a supported download route. Preserve the original, record its hash and decoded properties, and derive working copies through the normal ingest flow. Do not bypass source access controls or bulk-scrape an archive to fill a few shots.
6. Review the sequence: unintended clip repetition, misleading juxtaposition, inconsistent time/place, adjacent shots that add no new information, and audio conflicts. Deliberate repetition may support a motif; document that purpose. Do not enforce arbitrary uniqueness, cut-frequency or corpus-size targets.

If a shot remains unfilled, show the concrete gap and the lowest-cost alternatives: existing media, a diagram, a purposeful still, a new capture, or authorized generation. A clip that looks related is not evidence for a factual claim. Preserve unresolved provenance rather than filling missing rights fields with guesses.

Build a local reusable media index only when repeated retrieval across an existing permitted collection justifies it. Bound storage and include rights/expiry metadata; stale licensing can invalidate reuse even when file bytes match. Existing project manifests remain authoritative.

Source context: independently authored after inspecting OpenMontage's footage retrieval pipeline and corpus/search interfaces at [9327439db69021ab4b0e2776729bf3b58fdb5a87](https://github.com/calesthio/OpenMontage/tree/9327439db69021ab4b0e2776729bf3b58fdb5a87). No upstream code, prompts, assets, source adapters, CLIP model or media database is installed. This method can be followed with existing browser/search and ingest tools.
