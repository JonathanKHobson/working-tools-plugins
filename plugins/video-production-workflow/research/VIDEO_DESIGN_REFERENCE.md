# Video Design Reference

## Temporal hierarchy

At every moment, answer: what should the viewer notice first, second, and last? A frame can be visually attractive yet fail because the important element enters too late, leaves too quickly, or competes with another reveal.

## Composition system

- Use a grid and spacing scale rather than ad hoc coordinates.
- Preserve one dominant focal point for information-heavy shots.
- Assign subject anchors and no-crop zones to faces, hands, products, diagrams, and embedded text.
- Use negative space intentionally for overlays and future aspect-ratio variants.
- Recompose 16:9, 9:16, 1:1, and 4:5; do not assume a center crop.

## Typography

- Define roles: display, title, subtitle, body, caption, lower third, label, annotation, credit.
- Measure text at the target resolution and actual font.
- Use explicit line breaks and avoid orphaned words.
- Provide a hold after a reveal; viewers cannot read while everything is still moving.
- Test text against changing frames, not only a hero still.
- Treat platform UI safe zones as reviewed overlays, not memorized constants.

## Motion grammar

- **Enter:** establish an element and its origin.
- **Emphasize:** direct attention without replacing hierarchy.
- **Connect:** show relationship or continuity.
- **Transform:** preserve object identity across a change.
- **Exit:** clear space and prepare the next focal point.

Use easing that matches meaning. Ease-out suits arrivals; ease-in-out suits continuous transformations; linear suits constant rates such as timers or physical data. Add anticipation or overshoot sparingly. Hold payoff frames.

## Transition grammar

A small coherent set is stronger than random variety:

- hard cut for clarity, energy, contrast, or continuity;
- dissolve for time/emotional blending;
- directional wipe/slide when spatial progression is meaningful;
- match cut when shape, motion, gaze, or sound bridges shots;
- dip to color/black for chapter boundaries;
- graphic transition when a brand or narrative motif earns it.

## Accessibility

Captions, contrast, readable text, language support, color-independent encoding, flashing checks, reduced-motion options, and alternative descriptions should be considered during design. Accessibility often improves ordinary mobile viewing rather than serving a small edge case.
