# Audio and Music Reference

## Audio editing hierarchy

1. Meaning and intelligibility.
2. Performance and emotional continuity.
3. Noise/room consistency.
4. Music and ambience relationship.
5. Effects and polish.
6. Loudness/true-peak conform.

## Music analysis layers

| Layer | Evidence | Editorial use |
|---|---|---|
| Signal | waveform, peak, RMS, silence | clipping, fades, macro dynamics |
| Pulse | tempo, beats, onsets | local accents and candidate snap points |
| Meter/bar | recurring beat groups, downbeats | medium-scale visual changes |
| Phrase/section | novelty, energy, harmony, cadence | scene/chapter boundaries |
| Meaning | lyrics, genre, cultural context, user intent | narrative and ethical fit |

A waveform cannot identify all of these. Feature agreement strengthens confidence; disagreement should produce alternatives rather than false certainty.

## Dialogue edits

Use word and silence evidence, but listen to performance. Preserve breath and room tone. Small fades prevent clicks; room-tone beds mask gaps; J/L cuts preserve continuity; b-roll can cover visual discontinuity without falsifying speech.

## Mixing

- Keep voice/dialogue in front of music.
- Use automation or sidechain ducking around speech.
- Avoid constant heavy compression that removes emotional dynamics.
- Align effects to visible causes or meaningful beats.
- Check mono compatibility and small-speaker intelligibility when relevant.

## Loudness

ITU-R BS.1770 defines programme loudness/true-peak measurement methods; EBU R 128 applies them in broadcast practice. Delivery targets vary. Measure first, mix appropriately, and use two-pass normalization when exact final loudness matters. True peak is important because encoded inter-sample peaks can exceed sample peaks.
