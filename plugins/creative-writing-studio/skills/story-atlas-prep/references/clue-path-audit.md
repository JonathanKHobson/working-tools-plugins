# Clue-path audit

Use this check for a mystery, investigation, or scenario in which a required conclusion or next step could become inaccessible. Keep it bounded to the current adventure or session plan. Do not require three clues for optional lore, and do not turn a player-discovered shortcut into an error.

Read the complete [selected original check](../../../libraries/gm-apprentice-clue-audit/clue-redundancy.original.md) alongside this host adaptation. Keep all five checks; the current project supplies the source material, not an assumed Obsidian vault or an unavailable upstream agent.

## 1. Name the necessary conclusions

List only facts the players must infer or next steps they must find to keep playing. For each, state why it is necessary, the source in the scenario or current canon, and whether the table has already learned it. Do not assume a prepared clue was discovered merely because it appeared in a plan.

## 2. Map actual routes

For each necessary conclusion, make a compact row for every clue: what the players can observe, its scene/NPC/location/handout, how they can access it, any required roll or interaction, and whether it was found in play. Count distinct evidence routes, not repetitions of the same statement in one location.

## 3. Test for a single point of failure

Check whether at least three independent clues can lead to each necessary conclusion. Then check separately whether all clues are concentrated in one scene, gated by the same skill check, or depend on one NPC cooperating. A count of three does not rescue a single fragile access route. Trace links between scenes: mark a clue that points to a cut or nonexistent node, a conclusion with no incoming route, or a sole bridge between otherwise disconnected parts of the scenario.

For a live table, repair the smallest bottleneck: move a necessary fact into an alternate observable surface, let a different action reveal it, or let the world move the clue forward without requiring a particular player choice. Preserve the meaning of choices and established canon.

## 4. Report and repair

Return one row per necessary conclusion:

| Conclusion | Clues and locations | Distinct access routes | Already found | Risk | Smallest repair |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Label `blocked` when no reachable clue leads to a required conclusion, `thin` when fewer than three independent clues are prepared, `fragile` when routes share one gate, and `robust` when at least three independent routes are prepared, so one missed clue still leaves at least two routes. Multiple risks may apply. Cite the scenario or play-note source for each finding. A proposed clue is prep until the GM adopts it; an improvised clue is canon only if established in play or explicitly approved. Keep GM-only revelations and private player information out of player-facing output.
