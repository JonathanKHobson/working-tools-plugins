# Diagnosis and Revision

Use this reference when the user provides existing material for critique, repair, or revision.

## Diagnose before prescribing

Identify the two or three highest-leverage causes, cite representative moments from the supplied draft, and explain their effects. Distinguish structural problems from sentence-level symptoms.

| Symptom | Likely cause | Useful repair |
|---|---|---|
| Spooky but not tense | weak protectable thing, consequence, or immediate objective | attach the disturbance to a valued person, obligation, or choice |
| Confusing rather than mysterious | evidence is vague or contradictions lack a governing model | clarify observable facts and destabilize interpretation |
| Repetitive scares | escalation changes volume but not the problem | change proximity, certainty, scale, cost, or available options |
| Every scene screams at the same volume | intensity has replaced pacing and contrast | rotate pressure modes and add orientation, attachment, discovery, or temporary relief |
| Passive protagonist | choices do not alter knowledge or consequences | give competence, a motivated theory, and costly decisions |
| Lore dump | history is detached from present conflict | reveal lore through procedure, evidence, argument, or consequence |
| Cosmic lore dump | taxonomy and ancient history replace model failure and present choice | build an evidence chain that widens scale, contradicts a working model, and changes an obligation |
| Unknowable by adjective | the draft calls the force incomprehensible but supplies no observable failure of understanding | let a reasonable model fail against specific evidence and attach the failure to a local consequence |
| Cosmic nihilism by default | vastness erases character, attachment, and choice instead of pressuring them | give the character a costly human value that remains locally meaningful without defeating or validating it through the cosmos |
| One-sided incomprehension | humans struggle to interpret the force, but its responses assume perfect knowledge of human meaning | define what each side misclassifies and build contact through costly, revisable translation |
| Sympathy erases harm | revealing the threat's pain or good intent silently excuses coercion and abandons its victims | separate intent, method, and impact; let empathy complicate the response without cancelling accountability |
| Psychological horror by confusion | every perception may be false, so the audience cannot infer or care about any test | protect one source of self-trust and establish material, relational, or procedural anchors |
| Threat scary only in theory | the draft asserts danger, but neither character nor audience has reason to believe it can reach what matters | supply observed evidence, a feared forecast, and a condition that makes harm credible |
| Static paranoia | suspicion recurs without changing theory, relationship, action, or cost | vary the evidence, force a consequential test, and let the result alter behavior |
| Isolation as empty room | the character is alone, but no useful corrective relationship, record, or routine has been lost | remove a specific source of feedback and preserve a costly choice to reconnect |
| Dread without payoff | foreshadowing and ominous reminders accumulate without recognition, consequence, or meaningful subversion | return to the promise in changed context and force contact, choice, or aftermath |
| Spiral that resets | false recovery and relapse return the character to the same state | preserve a gain, compromise, exposure, dependency, or changed relationship on every cycle |
| Unreliable-viewpoint cheat | the reveal depends on hiding thoughts or facts the viewpoint plainly knew | present the accessible experience honestly; motivate omissions and recontextualize established evidence |
| “Madness” shorthand | a diagnostic or stigmatizing label replaces a pressure mechanism | specify changes in attention, memory, perception, sleep, language, judgment, and choice |
| Blunt monster metaphor | the threat exists only to illustrate one named theme | give it causal rules and independent resistance, then make interaction force a thematic choice |
| Static symbolic artifact | a journal, collection, image, or specimen repeats without changing its role | change its meaning, custody, condition, or ethical cost as the maker and witness change |
| Arbitrary monster | threat abilities expand whenever needed | define triggers, limits, feedback, and adaptation |
| Arbitrary hostile environment | hazards punish without readable cause or counterplay | give each hazard a sensory tell, trigger, response, counterplay, and persistent residue |
| Mechanical hellscape wallpaper | pipes, gears, flesh, and industrial suffering decorate a place with no operating logic | define the system's optimization target, classifications, inputs, beneficiaries, ignored feedback, and point of intervention |
| Flat gore | bodily detail changes nothing | connect injury or transformation to function, identity, or relationship |
| Hedged peak | the scene's most intense moment is summarized, abstracted, or cut away from | commit to a stated intensity level; replace category nouns with named tissue and texture; extend duration |
| Body-horror mode drift | subtle ambiguity, tragedy, extreme explicitness, and surreal logic appear without purposeful transitions | choose a dominant mode and make every shift mark a change in certainty, embodiment, or agency |
| Shock tableau | disturbing imagery neither changes choice nor reveals the threat | turn it into evidence, temptation, consequence, or remove it |
| Hollow twist | reveal invalidates earlier investment or evidence | recontextualize established facts and force a new decision |
| Dead world | everything waits for the protagonist | advance NPC agendas, institutions, and material processes offstage |
| Game helplessness | player verbs are removed without meaningful alternatives | restore a consequential verb or make loss brief and legible |
| Communication as dialogue quiz | contact with an alien force is solved by selecting the author's approved explanation | make translation a testable verb with incomplete feedback, mutual adaptation, and consequences for both sides |
| TTRPG dread stalls | the scenario withholds the threat but never lets partial knowledge become action | reveal testable rules, then phase from uncertainty into player-chosen pursuit, containment, bargain, or escape |
| Reset-loop grind | repetition only removes resources or repeats exposition | preserve useful knowledge, alter the state, and make each return change a decision |
| TTRPG railroad | required information or outcome has one path | add redundant clues, reactive nodes, and multiple costly end states |
| Atmosphere as wallpaper | mood words, props, or sensory inventories do not alter understanding or action | select recurring anchors whose meaning changes and make each detail orient, teach, contradict, or pressure a choice |

## Revision order

Revise in this order unless the user's request is narrower:

1. investment and central fear;
2. causality and character objectives;
3. threat logic and consequence;
4. information, clues, and reveals;
5. agency and choices;
6. escalation and pacing contrast;
7. offstage world activity;
8. scene turns and residue;
9. prose, dialogue, interface, or presentation;
10. ethics, cultural specificity, and originality.

Do not polish sentences that a structural repair will delete.

## Preserve authorship

When revising, identify what already works and preserve it as an anchor: viewpoint, rhythm, image system, humor, restraint, character dynamic, or formal device. Make the smallest change that resolves the diagnosed cause unless the user requested a full rewrite.

For a diagnosis, provide:

1. a concise verdict;
2. prioritized causes with draft evidence;
3. a bounded revision plan;
4. one representative rewrite or redesigned beat when it proves the repair;
5. anything intentionally left unchanged.
