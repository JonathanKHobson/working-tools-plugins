# Story Architecture

Use this skill when the story itself needs shaping or diagnosis. The output should make the next writing decision easier, not bury the user in theory.

## Inputs

Gather only what matters:

- Story promise, genre, audience, format, and intended emotional experience.
- Current stage: premise, outline, draft, revision, continuity repair, or adaptation.
- POV/tense, protagonist, antagonist/pressure, central conflict, stakes, constraints.
- Known canon, timeline, open questions, and deliberate mysteries.

## Architecture Checks

- Promise: does the premise tell the reader what kind of satisfaction is coming?
- Causality: do events happen because of character choices, pressure, and consequences?
- Agency: who chooses, who pays, who changes?
- Stakes: what becomes harder, better, worse, or irreversible?
- Scene purpose: what changes by the end of each scene?
- Show/tell purpose: what must be dramatized, what can be summarized, and what
  concrete evidence lets the reader infer the emotional or thematic point?
- Arc pressure: how does each plot or character arc advance, pause, or transform?
- Setup/payoff: what is planted, what is paid off, what is still a promise?
- Clue logic: are necessary clues available, redundant, and fair?
- Pacing: where does the reader need speed, friction, relief, or reflection?
- Line support: use `show-vs-tell-craft` when a scene review finds flat
  emotional labels, unsupported claims, unclear clues, or over-described
  transitions.
- Voice/POV: does the narration know only what it can know?

For narrative revision, resolve structural choices before polishing sentences. Do not impose one linear arc, a tidy resolution, or an explained moral on a story built around parallel threads, withheld knowledge, or ambiguity. Treat these as author choices to test against the intended experience, not defects inferred from generic writing patterns.

When a scene or chapter's sentences work but its narrative effect does not, use [narrative revision](references/narrative-revision.md) to inspect agency, causality, time, revelation, perspective and reader relationship before a line pass. Read it only for a developmental or narrative diagnosis.

## Continuity Triage

Label findings precisely:

- hard contradiction
- likely contradiction
- unresolved question
- deliberate mystery
- deliberate unreliable account
- flashback or flash-forward
- alternate continuity
- draft placeholder
- authorial choice

Do not "fix" ambiguity that is deliberately marked. Ask for author choice when a fix would change theme, voice, canon, or reader interpretation.

## Creative prose revision

Use this included method for a requested naturalness, voice or line-level revision. It requires the draft and the author's constraints, not another writing plugin.

1. Establish the scene's point of view, tense, emotional movement, canon facts and desired revision depth. Preserve distinctive language the author wants to keep. If a missing fact would change the story, leave it open rather than manufacture canon.
2. Read the whole passage before editing sentences. Identify what changes in the scene and where the reader needs information, uncertainty or surprise. Repair confusing causality before polishing adjectives.
3. Review each paragraph for its job: action, perception, thought, speech, orientation or transition. Retain deliberate repetition, fragments and exposition where they serve the scene. Avoid an automatic rule that every statement must become sensory detail.
4. Anchor perception in what this viewpoint character could notice. Choose details for dramatic relevance; do not add decorative smells, gestures or metaphors that change the setting or character without permission.
5. Distinguish characters by intent, diction, restraint and what they leave unsaid. Check pronouns, speaker attribution and physical continuity. Read dialogue in context, preserving dialect and cultural register without caricature.
6. Shape sentence rhythm around pace and attention. Remove redundant explanations and empty intensifiers; keep purposeful pauses and the author's expressive range. Do not optimize for detector scores or simulated human errors.
7. Compare old and new text for changes to events, agency, relationships, promises and tone. Restore unintended changes. If the request is diagnostic, return a few specific observations and illustrative revisions rather than rewriting the entire work.
8. Deliver the requested revision, then identify substantial craft choices and unresolved story questions separately. A revision is not a claim of originality clearance or publication.

Check with three cases: a quiet scene must not gain an invented conflict; an unreliable narrator must keep intentional ambiguity; an exposition-heavy passage may keep concise explanation when dramatizing it would derail the scene. Use [show-vs-tell craft](../show-vs-tell-craft/SKILL.md) when that balance is the central problem.
