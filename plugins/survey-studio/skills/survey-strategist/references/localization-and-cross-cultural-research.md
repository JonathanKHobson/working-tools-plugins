# Localization and Cross-Cultural Research

Translation is not word substitution. The goal is measurement equivalence: respondents in each language and culture should understand the intended construct and response scale in sufficiently comparable ways.

## Workflow

1. Define constructs, target locales, literacy, scripts, devices, and cultural contexts.
2. Create a source questionnaire in plain, translation-ready language.
3. Maintain a glossary, do-not-translate list, construct notes, and screenshots/context.
4. Use qualified translators familiar with survey research and the domain.
5. Conduct independent review, reconciliation, or team translation; back translation can reveal issues but is not sufficient alone.
6. Test layout, fonts, right-to-left behavior, pluralization, dates, units, addresses, names, and input methods.
7. Cognitively pretest each language with target-like participants.
8. Assess measurement invariance or differential item functioning when comparing groups quantitatively.
9. Version every language and keep codes stable across translations.

## Cultural adaptation

Review examples, institutions, education levels, income bands, race/ethnicity, gender, household, employment, geography, holidays, time, health concepts, and response norms. Do not force one country’s demographic categories onto another.

## Scale equivalence

Agreement and extremity response styles can differ. A translated label may not have the same intensity. Preserve construct meaning, not literal word count. Document unavoidable differences and avoid overinterpreting small cross-country gaps.

## Layout and technology

Plan for text expansion, non-Latin scripts, right-to-left interfaces, font fallback, vertical space, keyboard/input methods, localized decimal/date conventions, low bandwidth, and local assistive technologies. Never bake essential text into images.

## Analysis

Keep language/locale, translation version, and mode in the dataset. Before pooling, check missingness, distributions, reliability, factor structure, and item behavior across groups. Translation quality is a measurement issue, not only a publishing issue.
