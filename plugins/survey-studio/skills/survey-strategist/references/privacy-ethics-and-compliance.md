# Privacy, Ethics, and Compliance

This is operational guidance, not legal advice. Applicable requirements depend on jurisdiction, organization, population, topic, technology, and research relationship. Escalate to responsible legal, privacy, security, ethics, or IRB owners.

## Key distinctions

- **Anonymous:** the organization cannot reasonably link responses to a person, including through indirect identifiers or stored linkage.
- **Pseudonymous/confidential:** identity is separated or protected but linkage is possible.
- **Identified:** responses contain or are linked to identity.

Do not promise anonymity when using personalized links, collecting contact fields, retaining IP/device identifiers, linking product telemetry, recording media, or holding a re-identification key.

## Privacy-by-design workflow

1. Define purpose and lawful/authorized use.
2. Minimize questions and metadata.
3. Classify identifiers and sensitive categories.
4. Document collection, source, recipients/processors, international transfers, security, retention, deletion, and respondent rights.
5. Separate research responses from contact/incentive data when possible.
6. Limit access and use encryption/secure storage appropriate to risk.
7. Provide accurate notice and consent/choice when required.
8. Define incident, withdrawal, deletion, and data-subject request processes.
9. Review before changing purpose or linking datasets.

## Consent and notice

A concise introduction should explain the purpose, voluntary nature, expected time, topics/risks, data collected, identifiability/confidentiality, data use/sharing, retention, incentive, withdrawal/skip rights, and contact. Layer detailed policy links rather than hiding essentials behind legal text.

Consent is not valid merely because a checkbox exists. Avoid bundled, deceptive, or coerced consent. Participation in required service forms may rest on a different legal basis than optional research.

## Sensitive information

Health, biometrics, precise location, race/ethnicity, religion, political beliefs, union membership, sexuality, immigration/legal status, financial data, credentials, minors’ data, and experiences of violence or wrongdoing can require heightened controls. Ask only when necessary, make optional where possible, provide context/support, and avoid free-text overcollection.

## Children and vulnerable participants

Confirm age thresholds, parental consent/assent, school/employer power dynamics, safeguarding, mandatory reporting, incentive effects, and comprehension. A generic adult consent statement is not sufficient.

## Research ethics

Human-subjects research may require ethics/IRB review, informed consent documentation, risk minimization, equitable selection, confidentiality, and special protections. Product research outside formal regulation still has ethical duties: respect, beneficence, justice, transparency, and avoidance of dark patterns.

## Jurisdiction prompts

- **GDPR/EEA/UK-style regimes:** purpose limitation, data minimization, transparency, lawful basis, special categories, processor/controller roles, retention, rights, transfers, DPIA where high risk.
- **California privacy law:** notice at collection, rights processes, sensitive personal information, sale/sharing and service-provider relationships where applicable.
- **COPPA/children’s privacy:** verifiable parental consent and limits for covered online collection from children under applicable age.
- **Sector rules:** health, education, financial, employment, telecommunications, and government records may add duties.

## Re-identification

Even without names, combinations such as exact role, small team, location, age, incident date, and verbatim quote may identify someone. Use aggregation, suppression, redaction, broader categories, access controls, and minimum reporting cells.

## High-risk anti-patterns

- collecting email in the same “anonymous” survey;
- using invisible metadata without notice;
- requiring sensitive fields for a nonessential study;
- sending confidential answers through unsecured email;
- sharing raw verbatims containing names;
- retaining data indefinitely “for future research”;
- using responses for employment or eligibility decisions without clear authority and notice;
- uploading identifiable data to an unapproved AI or analytics service.

## Review output

Document data inventory, identifiability, purpose, necessity, notice/consent, retention, access, sharing, security, respondent rights, vulnerable groups, unresolved legal/ethics questions, and named owners.
