# Question Subject Areas

Subject area describes **what** an item concerns, not whether it belongs in the survey. Include a domain only when it supports eligibility, analysis, service delivery, safety, or a named decision.

| Area | Examples | Common use | Key caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Demographic | age band, gender, race/ethnicity, disability, household | fairness analysis, weighting, segmentation | sensitivity, cultural fit, re-identification |
| Behavioral | actions, frequency, recency, sequence, workaround | product usage and problem prevalence | recall error; define timeframe |
| Psychographic | attitudes, values, aspirations, risk tolerance | segmentation and messaging research | weak links to behavior; stereotype risk |
| Technographic | device, platform, tools, connectivity | compatibility and technology context | rapidly changing categories |
| Geographic | country, region, urbanicity | eligibility, localization, service availability | precise location may identify people |
| Socio-economic | income, housing, occupation, resources | policy, access, affordability | high sensitivity and nonresponse |
| Health and wellness | symptoms, diagnosis, functioning, wellbeing | health research and service evaluation | special-category data; ethics and distress |
| Educational | attainment, enrollment, learning context | education research and product fit | country-specific systems |
| Employment | status, role, tenure, industry, conditions | workforce and B2B research | employer retaliation fears |
| Customer satisfaction | satisfaction with defined experience | service improvement | insufficient diagnosis alone |
| Product feedback | feature use, ease, success, value, problems | product development | avoid roadmap voting as sole evidence |
| Service evaluation | access, timeliness, quality, resolution | operations and training | channel and case-mix differences |
| Brand perception | awareness, associations, trust, differentiation | brand strategy | exposure/recognition bias |
| Lifestyle | routines, interests, household practices | context and segmentation | intrusive breadth, cultural assumptions |
| Opinion and beliefs | policy views, confidence, norms | social research and polling | order, framing, social desirability |
| Financial | spending, debt, income, access, confidence | financial services and policy | extreme sensitivity and legal duties |
| Cultural | language, identity, practices, norms | localization and cultural research | essentializing groups; translation limits |
| Environmental | beliefs, impacts, behaviors | sustainability and policy | attitude–behavior gap |
| Legal | experience with systems, rights awareness | compliance and justice research | legal advice, privilege, distress |
| Ethical | values, dilemmas, governance expectations | ethics and organizational policy | hypothetical bias |
| Future intentions | planned adoption, purchase, attendance | demand planning | intention is not behavior |

## Rules for demographic items

1. Define the analytic or operational use before asking.
2. Use categories appropriate to the jurisdiction and population; do not import one country’s categories globally.
3. Prefer self-description or multiple selection when identities are not mutually exclusive.
4. Add “prefer not to answer” for sensitive disclosure when participation can remain valid.
5. Place later unless needed for eligibility or branching.
6. Report small cells carefully; suppression or aggregation may be necessary.
7. Do not use demographic proxies as explanations for structural differences.

## Behavior before attitude

When a survey concerns a product or service, start with what happened: recency, task, channel, outcome, workaround, and consequences. Attitude items become more interpretable when anchored to a concrete experience.

## Minimality test

For each proposed domain ask: Would the study decision change if this field were unavailable? Is a less sensitive proxy sufficient? Can the information be obtained from a trusted existing record with appropriate permission? If not needed, remove it.
