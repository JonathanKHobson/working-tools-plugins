# Validated Measures

## What “validated” means

A measure has evidence for reliability and validity in specific populations, languages, contexts, modes, and uses. Validation is not a permanent badge for any paraphrase. Changing wording, response labels, timeframe, item order, point count, scoring, or translation can change the measure.

## Reuse workflow

1. Identify the construct and intended interpretation.
2. Find the authoritative instrument, scoring instructions, validation evidence, translations, and license/permission terms.
3. Confirm fit for the target population, mode, language, and context.
4. Preserve wording, options, order, and scoring when benchmark comparability matters.
5. Document version, citation, license, modifications, and consequences.
6. Pretest comprehension in the target population even when the instrument is established.
7. Reassess reliability/structure when adapting or translating.

Do not reproduce an entire copyrighted or licensed scale in a general reference. Retrieve it from the authorized source when needed.

## Common UX and service measures

| Measure/family | Typical construct | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| System Usability Scale (SUS) | perceived system usability | licensed/attribution status and exact 10-item scoring; reverse coding |
| UMUX / UMUX-Lite | perceived usability/usefulness | wording/scoring and benchmark transformations vary |
| Single Ease Question (SEQ) | post-task perceived ease | exact wording/scale needed for benchmark claims |
| Customer Satisfaction (CSAT) | satisfaction with a named object | not one universal standardized instrument |
| Customer Effort Score (CES) | effort/ease in service context | vendor versions and directions differ |
| Net Promoter Score (NPS) | likelihood to recommend plus category scoring | diagnostic and predictive limitations; 0–10 convention |
| SUPR-Q | website user experience quality | licensing/authorized source and scoring |
| NASA-TLX | perceived workload | variants and weighting choices; not simply “difficulty” |
| Standard health/wellbeing scales | specific clinical or wellbeing constructs | special-category data, scoring, risk protocols, permissions |

## Benchmarks

A benchmark is comparable only when population, context, mode, wording, scale, scoring, and timing are sufficiently aligned. Published normative data may not fit a product, country, or recruitment channel. Report the benchmark source and mismatch risks.

## Reliability and validity checks

Depending on the measure and sample, consider internal consistency, test–retest reliability, inter-rater reliability, content validity, convergent/discriminant validity, criterion validity, factor structure, differential item functioning, and measurement invariance across groups/languages.

Cronbach’s alpha alone does not prove unidimensionality or validity. A high alpha can result from redundant items.

## Adaptation labels

When changing an item, say “adapted from” and avoid claiming the original measure’s benchmark or validation applies automatically. Keep the original and adapted versions distinguishable in IDs and documentation.
