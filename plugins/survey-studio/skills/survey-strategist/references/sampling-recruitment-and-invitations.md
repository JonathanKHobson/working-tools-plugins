# Sampling, Recruitment, and Invitations

## Define the population precisely

Write an inclusion statement that a recruiter could apply without interpretation:

> People aged 18 or older who personally attempted to transfer money in the mobile app within the past 30 days in the United States, excluding employees and research vendors.

Separate:

- **target population:** who the decision concerns;
- **sampling frame:** the list/channel from which people can be selected;
- **eligible population:** operational inclusion/exclusion rules;
- **respondents:** those who complete enough of the survey for analysis.

Differences among these groups create coverage and nonresponse risk.

## Sampling approaches

| Approach | Appropriate claim | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| Simple/stratified/cluster probability sample | population estimates with design-based uncertainty when executed well | frame quality, weights, nonresponse |
| Panel sample | estimates for the panel-reachable population under documented calibration | panel conditioning and opt-in bias |
| Customer list sample | experience among reachable listed customers | excludes prospects, churned, or offline users |
| Intercept sample | triggered users/events at that surface | only people who reach and accept intercept |
| Convenience/social sample | descriptive insight about respondents | no automatic population generalization |
| Census invitation | attempts to contact all frame members | still subject to nonresponse/coverage error |

## Sample size

Do not choose sample size from a universal rule. Base it on:

- primary estimate and required precision;
- expected prevalence/variance;
- subgroup comparisons and minimum cell sizes;
- design effect and clustering;
- expected response, eligibility, and completion rates;
- multiple comparisons or model complexity;
- practical decision threshold;
- qualitative coding saturation is not a survey sample-size formula.

For nonprobability samples, a narrow mathematical margin of error can be misleading because selection bias may dominate random error.

## Recruitment invitation

A clear invitation includes:

- who is conducting the research;
- why the person is invited, without revealing gaming criteria;
- topic and honest time estimate;
- voluntary nature and ability to stop;
- incentive terms and odds, if applicable;
- privacy/recording/data-linking essentials;
- accessibility/accommodation route;
- contact and legitimate sender verification;
- deadline and one clear action.

Avoid urgency, deceptive scarcity, inflated social proof, or claims that participation will directly cause a desired outcome.

## Incentives

Match incentive to burden, population, and local rules. Consider undue influence for vulnerable groups, payment method accessibility, tax/identity collection, drawing disclosures, duplicate prevention, and whether incentive seekers change sample composition. Never withhold earned compensation because a respondent gives negative feedback.

## Contact cadence

Plan invitation, reminders, close, and opt-out. Vary time/day when coverage benefits; limit reminders; suppress completed and opted-out contacts; coordinate across overlapping studies to prevent fatigue.

## Nonresponse assessment

Track invitation delivery, starts, eligibility, breakoffs, completes, disposition reasons, and item nonresponse. Compare respondents with frame variables where legitimately available. Weighting can adjust observed differences but cannot guarantee removal of unobserved selection bias.

## Recruitment QA

Verify links, parameters, language, device behavior, quota rules, duplicate controls, accessibility, sender reputation, incentive fulfillment, and the exact version respondents will see.
