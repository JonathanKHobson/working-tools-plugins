# One lead for an initiative across chats

Use at the start of a new initiative, or when scope grows into independent durable outcomes. Identify the requested outcome, subgoals, deliverables, dependencies and existing owners before choosing the working shape. Count independent outcomes and context/ownership boundaries, not checklist steps.

Three or more independent workstreams, or two durable goals with distinct owners, contexts or dependencies, is a useful signal to consider one human-facing coordinating chat. This is a judgment heuristic, not an automatic threshold or permission to create chats. A small task or shared-context subtasks stays local or uses bounded subagents. Reuse suitable durable chats under current user authorization; tool requirements govern creation and messaging. Do not start a new manager runtime or create a chat for every step.

Keep one initiative lead accountable for translating the human's intent, resolving cross-owner scope and assembling the result. Each durable owner keeps its local goal, permitted tools, artifacts and acceptance evidence. A lead's coordination does not merge unrelated goals or override an owner awaiting a human decision. The human should be able to steer the initiative through the lead without repeated brief-writing or contradictory autonomous owners.

Dispatch a compact brief: concrete outcome, source pointers and dates, ownership, allowed side effects, acceptance check, dependencies, what not to touch and return evidence. Send only task-relevant context. Relay corrections to affected owners, name the corrected boundary and verify that final artifacts honor it. Do not distribute whole histories or ask every worker to repeat the same source review.

Use bounded subagents or verified Claude/local workers for independent steps within a goal. Check the actual supported capability and validate outputs; a rejected mechanical extraction must not control consequential dates, claims or actions. Reuse active jobs and preserve other owners' UI sessions. Maintain the existing project handoff/state record when useful; do not add a duplicate permanent coordination ledger.

Integrate with native status/cursors and exact artifact receipts. Dispatched, in progress, prepared, scheduled, sent, published and verified remain distinct. Resolve conflicts, check the combined result and report uncovered limits. A seven-panel graphic, a screenshot of an app or a count of chats does not prove seven agents were simultaneously active. Label capture times, completed versus active states and original versus derivative graphics.

Capture the selected process as a private evidence candidate when authorized: initiating human instruction, decomposition/owner IDs, compact brief examples, corrections, actual outputs, rejected attempts, assistant/human contribution and proof limits. Reduced human briefing, smaller task-specific context and lower cost are hypotheses until measured against a defined baseline. Do not infer token, money, time or quality savings from parallelism or a successful dispatch. Claim acceptance and public clearance are separate decisions.

## Durable chat names

Choose an intentional `Project or domain · Durable role` title when creating a durable chat, using the native creation tool's title field. Name the lasting responsibility, such as Project Coordinator, Publishing, Evidence Curator or Workshop Producer, rather than echoing the first task sentence. At the first substantive turn or a scope change, check this chat's title and correct an automatic, vague or obsolete name through the native tool. For one-off work use a concise domain and concrete outcome. Preserve a title the user chose; avoid title churn and unrelated mass renaming.

For authorized updates use native set_thread_title, then verify the returned/current title with native readback. Use the current returned title when identifying the chat. The existing work-start reminder routes this check; do not add a separate hook, title service or second chat registry.
